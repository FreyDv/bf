## README.md — how the decision log is organised

- Folder `docs/decision-records/` is "this repo's own decision log (zone 1)" — the repository-standards project's own ADRs, flat in the folder.
- Clients never receive copies: they "adopt these decisions by reference from the living standard - always latest" (ADR-004). The shipped tree carries only the empty log skeleton `standard/docs/decision-records/` for a client's own records.
- Reading rule: "Read the table instead of opening every record - the gist column says what each one settled." (ADR-033 relies on this: the index table bounds the spec loop's read.)
- Length rule (bold in source): "Keep a record as short as the decision actually is." Length is a cost, not a sign of rigour; write the shortest thing that still answers *why* for someone who was not in the room; a section with nothing to say may be one line. "Comprehensive means every question is answered, not that each answer is long - a record nobody finishes reading fails at the only job it has."
- Before writing a record, check it earns the shape: a choice that is cheap to reverse is a convention for `CONTRIBUTING.md` or a rule for `SPEC.md`, not a full Context/Options/Consequences record.
- Index table columns: `#`, `Title`, `Decides` (gist), `Status`. One row per record ("Add one row per record"); the template ships in the tree at `standard/docs/decision-records/adr/_template.md`. "Numbers are gapless and never reused."
- Statuses used in the table: `Accepted`; `Accepted, revised by NNN` (008 by 014/016; 010 by 028; 011 by 040; 024 narrowed by 049; 026 narrowed by 035; 028 renamed by 041; 045 revised by 062; 051 revised by 053; 054 revised by 062; 055 revised by 061 and 062; 057 revised by 058; 061 revised by 062); `Superseded by 015` (013 only).

## ADR-001 — Decision records use MADR; ADR = broad technical, BDR = business (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-07)

- Tags: meta, governance, decision-records. Author: Łukasz Bodurka.
- Context:
  - The standard tells other repos to record decisions but never defined, for itself or for them, what counts as a decision record, in what format, and how the technical/business split works.
  - Three doubts kept re-opening: (1) is "choose a CSS framework / a library over its alternatives" an ADR, a BDR, or a revived TDR? (2) one decision umbrella, or separate streams? (3) is it worth minting our own scheme (reinterpreting "A" as "Any", sub-type acronyms like ATDR / AADR)?
  - Left undefined, the answer drifts per author and per agent. "A standard that does not dogfood its own decision system has no authority" — this record sets the policy and is itself the first instance of it.
- Decision drivers: interoperability + familiarity (engineers and tools — Log4brains, adr-tools — already read "ADR" and MADR); low ambiguity for coding agents filing records; technical and business decisions have different stakeholders, altitude, lifecycle; avoid bespoke jargon in a repo whose purpose is standardization; extend to future decision kinds without re-cutting the taxonomy.
- Options considered:
  - A. Single "decision record" umbrella, type by frontmatter (MADR's "Any Decision Record", one folder) — simplest, but over-flattens genuinely different governance.
  - B. MADR format; two streams ADR (technical, broad) + BDR (business); sub-scope via `Tags` — keeps the standard acronym, separates concerns, extensible. **Chosen.**
  - C. Reinterpret "A" as "Any" and mint sub-type acronyms (ATDR, AADR, ...) — playful, but breaks the universal meaning of "ADR" and adds jargon.
- Decision (Option B) and rules:
  - Format: every record uses MADR (Markdown Any Decision Records). "The 'Any' we like lives in the format's name - it needs no new acronyms."
  - ADR = an architecturally-significant *technical* decision, read broadly: framework, library, tooling, infrastructure, data-model or protocol choices — not enterprise-architecture-only. The standard acronym is kept; its definition is widened in the glossary.
  - Sub-scope (`architecture` / `data` / `infra` / `security` / ...) goes in the record's existing `Tags`, never in the acronym.
  - BDR = a business / product decision — a separate stream, different stakeholders and altitude. "Business decisions do not enter the technical log."
  - No TDR stream (the ADR/TDR line is arbitrary; "living technical design" is behaviour in the capability specs + structure in `ARCHITECTURE.md`). Confirms the 0.3.0 removal of TDRs.
  - No bespoke sub-type acronyms (ATDR / AADR / ...): a `Tags` value does the same job, filterable, with a stable acronym.
  - Future non-technical kinds (e.g. marketing) get their own stream when one actually arises — same MADR format, not pre-built.
- Consequences: positive — standard format + acronym (tooling, familiarity); clean technical/business split; sub-typing without folder or acronym proliferation; the "Any" mnemonic is legitimate (MADR's name). Cost accepted — "ADR" nominally says "Architecture" while used for broader technical decisions, mitigated by the glossary definition and the community's own MADR "Any" rebrand. Follow-ups: MADR-align the record template (add `Confirmation`) — done here; state the policy in the decision-records glossary — done here; a record-format lint — not yet built.
- Confirmation: the glossary in `../README.md` ("what counts as a decision record here") defines ADR scope (broad technical), BDR (business), MADR format and `Tags`-as-scope (in place); every record uses the MADR `_template.md` (`../../standard/docs/decision-records/adr/_template.md`), which now carries a `Confirmation` field (in place); [not yet built] a lightweight record-format check in CI, analogous to `spec-structure.mjs`.
- Revisit when: a genuine non-technical, non-business decision stream (e.g. marketing) is needed often enough to warrant its own home; the ADR / MADR convention or its tooling shifts materially in the ecosystem; single-umbrella friction (cross-stream queries, duplicated governance) starts to outweigh the split.
- Related: confirms the TDR removal from 0.3.0; drives `decision-records/README.md` (glossary) and `decision-records/adr/_template.md` (`Confirmation` field).

## ADR-002 — Capability specs are organized by capability/domain, not by ticket or page (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-07)

- Tags: methodology, specs.
- Context: a capability spec needs one organizing axis; three candidates recur, and the wrong one keeps coming back in practice — a consumer's align produced `specs/cms/001-core/` (Spec Kit's ticket numbering leaking in). Records the axis and rejected forms so it is not re-litigated.
- Decision drivers: a spec is the long-lived truth of a domain, not a per-change artifact; a cross-cutting concept must have exactly one canonical home; coding agents file these — the axis must be unambiguous and mechanically guardable.
- Options: per-ticket / Spec Kit `NNN-feature/` — disposable, re-created per branch; maps to changes, not living truth (leaked as `specs/cms/001-core/` in a real align) — rejected. Per-page / route (`pdp/`, `checkout/`) — a concept like *packages* spans homepage, PDP and checkout, so per-page specs duplicate it and drift — rejected. Per-capability / domain — one domain, one canonical spec — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules:
  - Specs are organized by capability / domain.
  - Which pages or routes a capability surfaces on is a cross-reference in the docs, never a spec axis.
  - No ticket-numbered or page-named spec folders.
- Consequences: positive — one canonical spec per domain; no duplicated/drifting concepts; docs answer "where does this show up". Cost — "which capability owns this?" is a judgment call for genuinely new domains.
- Confirmation: `scripts/spec-structure.mjs` fails on ticket-numbered spec paths (`specs/**/NNN-*`); `specs/README` "Structure" states the capability axis + the page/route ban; per-page naming is not fully mechanizable (a page name and a capability name are both just folder names) — so it is also a spec-review check.
- Revisit when: a domain genuinely cannot be expressed as a capability, or the capability-boundary model stops matching how the product is reasoned about.
- Related: drives `specs/README` (Structure), `scripts/spec-structure.mjs`; confirms the intent behind the 0.7.0 spec-structure guard.

## ADR-003 — Capability specs are buildable, not descriptive (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-07)

- Tags: methodology, specs.
- Context: "How deep is a spec?" is a real, contestable choice. A spec that only describes *what* the system does cannot be implemented or verified from it — it drifts into vague prose and the code becomes the real source of truth.
- Decision drivers: a spec must let an engineer or agent implement AND verify the capability from the spec alone (plus shared schemas / standards), without reverse-engineering code; money / security / data-integrity / external-contract capabilities cannot be vague; thin, peripheral capabilities should not be forced into heavyweight detail.
- Options: Descriptive — prose "what it does", no contracts; cheap; not implementable or checkable; drifts — rejected. Buildable — carries the contracts: data, interface, algorithms, state machine, config, acceptance criteria (Given / When / Then); implementable + verifiable — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules:
  - Specs are buildable by default: they carry the contracts, quoted verbatim (real field names, enums, error codes, endpoints).
  - A lighter behavioral tier is allowed for thin / peripheral capabilities but MUST be declared (`Spec tier: behavioral`) so the gap is explicit.
  - Anything carrying money, security, data integrity, or an external contract MUST be buildable.
  - Unknowns go in Open questions, never glossed.
- Consequences: positive — specs implementable and testable from the spec; the spec, not the code, is the source of truth; acceptance criteria seed the tests. Cost — buildable specs are more work to write and keep current, accepted for the capabilities that matter.
- Confirmation: `specs/README` "Spec depth" states the bar, the contracts, the tiers; `capability-spec.template.md` carries the required buildable sections + the `Spec tier` declaration; depth is not fully mechanizable — a spec-review / reconcile check.
- Revisit when: the buildable bar proves too heavy for the domain, or tooling can generate / verify the contracts well enough to change the trade-off.
- Related: drives `specs/README` (Spec depth), `capability-spec.template.md`.

## ADR-004 — The standard's decisions reach client repos by reference, not by copy (status: Accepted; mechanism extended to method docs by ADR-023; reading semantics set latest-first by ADR-025 (references resolve at `main` — the living standard), date: 2026-07-07)

- Tags: methodology, distribution, decision-records.
- Context: the standard makes methodology decisions (ADR-001..003...). A client that adopts follows the rules those decisions produce (`specs/README`, conventions). Open question: are the standard's ADRs copied into each client repo, or referenced?
- Decision drivers: deviating from a standard prescription is rare; re-aligning to a new standard version is frequent. A client adopts the standard's decisions — it does not own them. A deviation should be a visible, governed record, not a silent edit. A client's decision log should be about the client's product, not cluttered with the standard's meta-decisions.
- Options: Copy — ADRs copied into client repo, edited to deviate; easy to deviate but every standard revision conflicts with the edited copy (forked-config drift), clutters the client's log, blurs ownership — rejected. Reference (link) — ADRs stay in the standard (public rationale); the client gets the rules (`specs/README`) with a link; to deviate, the client writes its own ADR superseding the standard's default; trivial to update, deviation is a first-class client-owned record, zero noise — **chosen**. Overlay layer over copied ADRs — rejected: noise, and does not fix the update conflict.
- Decision / rules:
  - The standard's methodology ADRs live in the standard and are referenced, not copied.
  - Clients receive the rules (methodology docs, which ship to `dist/` at the time of writing) and link to the standard's ADR for the rationale.
  - To deviate, a client writes its own ADR that supersedes the standard's default. No copies, no overlay.
- Consequences: positive — standard updates stay trivial (no forked copies); deviations explicit, client-owned; the standard's public ADRs double as adoption/marketing rationale; client logs stay about the client's product. Cost accepted — deviating takes a small ceremony (a superseding client ADR) rather than an in-place edit; fine because deviation is rare and "the ceremony **is** the correct, visible way to record it."
- Confirmation: `dist/` ships the rules (`specs/README`, conventions) + the ADR template — not the standard's own filled ADRs; the standard's ADRs (`ADR-001..N`) exist source-only in `decision-records/`; client deviations are recorded as client ADRs that reference the standard's default.
- Revisit when: a reflection engine (manifest + versioned migrations, Copier-style 3-way merge) makes updateable copies cheap enough to reconsider — though a client's edits would still conflict on update.
- Related: builds on ADR-001; governs how ADR-002/003 and future methodology ADRs reach clients. Revised by ADR-025 (references resolve at `main`), extended by ADR-023 (method docs), reused by ADR-040 and ADR-052 (`exceptions` as the recorded-deviation hatch).

## ADR-005 — The align/update engine reads a versioned manifest, not just prose (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-07)

- Tags: methodology, align-engine, distribution.
- Context: a repo adopts with `align-to-standards` and stays current with `update-to-version`; both are prose skills an agent interprets. The keystone work (versioned pin `.standards-version` + `self-verify`) made compliance checkable, but align/update was still an agent reading instructions.
  - Three unmet forces: **Repeatability** (prose interpreted differently each run; agents demonstrably make mistakes aligning; same repo + same version should converge on the same result). **Measurable drift** ("How far is this repo from `v0.7.2`?" should be a number, so a fleet owner can see which repos are behind). **Precise updates** (applying a `vY -> vX` delta needs a machine-readable description of what changed, not "re-read the changelog").
  - Without losing: adapt-to-the-stack (not blind copy), stack-agnostic Layer-1 simplicity, client deviations surviving an update (ADR-004).
- Options: A — prose skills only (status quo): zero new artifacts, max flexibility; interpretation varies, drift not measurable, updates lean on re-reading the changelog — rejected ("the reliability and measurability gaps are the whole point"). B — a declarative manifest the engine reads — **chosen**. C — executable migration scripts per version (`up()` like DB migrations / Copier tasks): precise and ordered but heavy, brittle across real client repos, stack-specific, fights adapt-to-the-stack ("a script cannot reconcile like an agent can") — rejected ("too rigid for the adapt-to-stack, brownfield reality").
- Decision / rules:
  - The align/update engine is driven by a versioned, declarative manifest; the agent applies it (adapting to the stack); `self-verify` checks the repo against the pinned version's manifest.
  - Drift = diff between the repo and the manifest (a number). Update = diff between two versions' manifests (the exact plan). Deviations are recorded manifest exceptions.
  - The manifest is data (Layer 1 — an agent applies it, no tooling required); any runner/tooling is Layer 2. "This does not replace the prose skills - it gives them a spine to read."
- Consequences: positive — drift is a number; updates precise; `self-verify` has a concrete thing to check; a fleet can be reported on. Negative — a manifest schema must be designed, versioned, and kept from drifting out of sync with the standard (mitigation: the skills read it so it cannot rot unused; `self-verify` asserts it). Follow-ups (landed, ENG-2): `standard.manifest.json` exists; `self-verify.mjs` reads it and emits a drift score. Still open at the time: teaching `align-to-standards` / `update-to-version` to compute plans from the manifest delta (they reference it; full mechanization a later increment).
- Confirmation: "Confirmed." `self-verify.mjs` reads the pinned version's `standard.manifest.json` and checks every entry (files, required sections, static guards), reporting drift as a number; a version/manifest mismatch fails. Schema dogfooded (this repo ships its own manifest).
- Revisit when: the manifest becomes as hard to interpret or maintain as the prose it replaced (over-engineered) — then step back toward A for the parts that resist description; a Copier-style 3-way merge tool makes executable, adapt-aware migrations cheap enough to reconsider C.
- Related: builds on ADR-004 and the versioned self-update mechanism (`.standards-version`, `update-to-version`, `self-verify`); governs the align-engine; backlog ENG-2 (built) and ENG-3 (this record).
- Appendix — the manifest (`standard.manifest.json`, at the time reflected to `dist/standard.manifest.json`; `repository-standards` describing itself at the current version; an aligned client carries a copy at its pinned version):
  - Shape: four lists plus two policy blocks, each entry carrying a `since` version and an `adapt` rule.
    - `files` — paths an aligned repo must have, each with an adapt rule `copy` / `merge` / `fill-from-repo` / `reference` saying how it arrives. Guards are `copy`; `ARCHITECTURE`/specs are `fill-from-repo` (scaffold the shell, author the body from the repo — never blind-copy); the standard's own decisions are `reference` (ADR-004).
    - `sections` — required headings inside a file (e.g. AGENTS.md must state `Altitude`).
    - `guards` — checks that must pass, `static` (run anytime) or `diff` (run on a PR diff).
    - `decisions` — the catalog of forks a repo must consciously record, each mapped to `ADR`/`BDR`.
    - `specs` / `exceptions` — the buildable-tier default and how a deliberate deviation is recorded so an update never silently overwrites it.
  - Three operations unlocked: **Verify** — today `self-verify.mjs` hardcodes the skeleton list; with manifest it reads the pinned version's manifest and checks every entry (one source of truth, not a JS copy). **Drift** — today a judgement call; with manifest `count(manifest entries the repo fails)`, a number a fleet owner can sort on. **Update `vY -> vX`** — today re-read the changelog; with manifest the diff of the two versions' manifests (keyed by kind + id/path), filtered by `since`.
  - "The manifest never replaces the prose skills' judgement (adapt-to-stack, brownfield reconciliation an agent does better than a script - why **C** was rejected); it gives them a spine to read."

## ADR-006 — Personas are a first-class, validated product artifact — a gate, not a poster (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-08)

- Tags: methodology, product, specs, personas.
- Context: the standard makes behavior verifiable against the code (buildable specs, the coupling guard) but had no answer to "for whom?". Ideas, specs, backlog items, business rules were validated against code and catalogued technical decisions — never against a named user; a capability could be perfectly buildable and perfectly useless. User's requirement: personas first-class; everything downstream (ideas, plans, specs, business requirements, backlog) validates against them; "For whom?" answerable before "what?" and "how?".
- Options: A — personas as an informal UX/marketing doc (a page written once, never consulted; gates nothing; drifts into fiction — "the failure mode the standard exists to kill, applied to product") — rejected. B — first-class, validated artifact that gates downstream work — **chosen**. C — full personas + journey maps + JTBD tooling as a heavyweight discovery layer — imports a product-discovery apparatus most repos will not sustain; over-engineered — rejected.
- Decision / rules:
  - Personas are a first-class artifact (`docs/personas.md`, from `personas.template.md`) and a validation gate.
  - Altitude: personas sit above specs and the backlog. A spec names the persona(s) it serves and states how; a backlog item names the persona whose job it advances; an idea with no persona is parked.
  - Primary persona wins ties unless a BDR says otherwise; a persona-vs-persona conflict is resolved by a recorded decision (BDR).
  - Jobs-to-be-Done is the durable core of a persona (features change, jobs do not); JTBD framing is borrowed without adopting a whole discovery methodology.
  - `greenfield-start` gathers personas before modules and specs; brownfield `onboard-repo` reconstructs them from the product and existing users.
- Consequences: positive — "for whom?" answered at decision time; specs/backlog items checkable against a user; persona conflicts surface as decisions. Negative — one more living artifact; a wrong persona misleads everything downstream so must be corrected deliberately (reviewed at each PO stage, revisited when the market moves). Follow-ups: `personas.template.md`; taxonomy row; the PO stage in `ways-of-working`; spec and backlog templates naming a persona; `greenfield-start`; "target personas" as a catalogued product decision (BDR).
- Confirmation: a spec without a named persona (and how it serves them) is incomplete at review — same tier as a spec missing its error table; the backlog template requires a persona field; the decision checklist lists "target personas" as a product decision. A mechanical check ("every capability spec references a persona in the roster") is a natural extension of the spec-structure guard — follow-up, not yet built.
- Revisit when: most adopting repos outgrow the light gate and need Option C's heavier machinery; or the mechanical follow-up check ships and shows review-time confirmation was not catching persona-less specs — the confirmation mechanism, not just tooling, needs to change.
- Related: mirrors buildable specs (ADR-003) on the product side; builds on the decision checklist (target personas = a BDR); governs `greenfield-start` and `onboard-repo`. Cited by ADR-020 (for-whom -> what -> how), ADR-028, ADR-042 (non-deferral pattern), ADR-048, ADR-057.

## ADR-007 — Modernization is plan-then-refactor, grounded in the recorded knowledge (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-08)

- Tags: methodology, modernization, adoption.
- Context: a common reason to reach for the standard is a repo that fell behind (old runtimes, stale dependencies, dated patterns). The naive move — bump the lockfile, chase breakages, repeat — loses behavior nobody remembered was load-bearing because the *why* was never written down before it was changed. Question settled: where modernization sits relative to the reconstructed knowledge (personas, specs, decisions) — before or after.
- Options: A — bump-first / reactive (every fix a guess about intent; breaking changes cascade; repo ends "current" with quietly-different behavior and no record) — rejected. B — plan-then-refactor grounded in recorded knowledge — **chosen**. C — big-bang rewrite (occasionally right for a tiny or truly rotten repo; usually most expensive and riskiest; throws away the behavior the specs were about to capture) — rejected, kept as a narrow escape hatch for trivial or unsalvageable repos, recorded as its own ADR when taken.
- Decision / rules:
  - Modernization is a plan-then-refactor activity that runs at the end of adoption.
  - "Sequence is a hard rule: understand (checkmap) -> record the decisions -> *then* refactor. No load-bearing change ships before the ADR that explains it."
  - Each non-trivial move (major bump with breaking changes, framework swap, pattern change) is an ADR/BDR naming the affected capability specs, written before the code moves.
  - Output: a sequenced, counted migration backlog (small, reversible, green steps) plus a maintenance strategy so currency is kept, not achieved once.
  - Delivered by the `modernize` skill; the migration is executed against the specs + tests the earlier gates built.
- Consequences: positive — migrations serve recorded intent; every direction change explained and reversible; "current" comes with a paper trail and a maintenance rhythm. Negative — must document and decide before refactoring (slower to start, deliberately); mitigation: the adoption checkmap already produced most knowledge. Follow-ups: the `modernize` skill; a Modernize phase in `docs/adoption.md`; the migration backlog format (reuses the standard item shape — cap, persona, why, DoD).
- Confirmation: a modernization PR that changes a dependency major or a pattern without a preceding ADR (and without the touched specs green) is incomplete at review — same tier as a spec missing its error table; the `modernize` skill refuses to plan a refactor before its preconditions (personas + specs + foundational ADRs) are met.
- Revisit when: Option C's escape hatch gets used more than rarely; or the record-before-code requirement stops finding real disagreements (if the specs and tests — ADR-003's safety net — already catch what would have been lost, requiring an ADR/BDR before every non-trivial move should be reconsidered).
- Related: builds on the adoption checkmap (`docs/adoption.md`) and ADR-003; distinct from the standard's own `update-to-version`; backlog MOD-1.
- 2026-07-22 note: the standalone `modernize` skill was folded into the adoption guide's plan-then-refactor pass (`standard/docs/adoption.md`) in the one-tree wave (ADR-014); every reference to "the modernize skill" now means that pass; the decision itself (knowledge first, refactor last) is unchanged.

## ADR-008 — The standard repo separates three zones — repo-own, source, shipped (status: Accepted (2026-07-22); zone mechanics revised by ADR-014 (zones 2 and 3 merged into the single `standard/` tree); stacks clause revised by ADR-016 (per-technology overlays became satellite repos), date: 2026-07-21)

- Tags: structure, repo-layout, dogfood.
- Context: the 2026-07-21 re-review found the repo failing its own first impression: no root `AGENTS.md`; marketing and working files (`landing.html`, wheel experiments, blog drafts, a Polish notes file) loose in the root; source directories (`github/`, `claude/`, `agents/`, `gitleaks/`) whose names collide with normal dot-dir conventions. An agent (or human) could not tell what is this repo's own life, what is the source of the standard, and what is the shipped skeleton. "For a repo whose whole pitch is 'structure that agents can navigate', that is the one failure it cannot afford."
- Options: A — flat root, documented only (root `AGENTS.md` maps the zones; cheapest; the `github/`-vs-`.github/` collision stays; root reads as a grab-bag) — rejected. B — three explicit zones, source grouped under one directory — **chosen**: Zone 1 = the repo's own life (governance, `backlog.md`, `tools/`, `apps/landing-page/`, `docs/working/`); Zone 2 = the standard's source, grouped under a single directory (working name `standard/`) so nothing collides with normal repo conventions; Zone 3 = `dist/`, build output only. Cost: a one-time migration PR rewriting the `reflect.mjs` map and internal links (`STRUCT-1`). C — merge source into `dist/` (single tree, no reflect) — rejected at the time: the divergent class (template vs filled example) genuinely needs two forms, and consumers should receive a clean skeleton, not the factory. (ADR-014 later measured this rationale and reversed it.)
- Decision / rules (Option B): zones are named; the root `AGENTS.md` is the map; repo-own public artifacts live in `docs/` and `apps/`; working/idea notes live outside the repo (owner rule, 2026-07-22); `dist/` is never edited by hand; the physical regroup of zone 2 under one directory executed as dedicated migration `STRUCT-1` once Accepted (it rides alone because it rewrites the reflect map and many links).
  - Clarification 1 (owner direction 2026-07-22): Zone 3 has two shipped surfaces, and technology never leaks into the first: the universal core skeleton (today `dist/` — Layer 1, any language) and per-technology overlays (`stacks/<technology>/` — today `node-ts`; a Python or Go stack slots in beside it without touching the core). A Python adopter takes the core plus their stack; nothing Next.js/TS-flavored may live in the core. The `.mjs` verify scripts and the `.nvmrc` pin are the standard's own toolchain, not a stack choice — documented as such. (Superseded by ADR-016: overlays became satellite repos.)
  - Clarification 2 (owner decision 2026-07-22): the name `dist/` stays — "this structure IS the repo's output", `dist/` is literally built by `reflect`; the name universally reads "generated - do not edit by hand" (a `skeleton/` invites editing). STRUCT-1 therefore moves zone-2 sources only. (Superseded by ADR-014: `dist/` is gone.)
- Consequences: positive — an entering agent reads one file and knows where it is; root stops accumulating strays; the standard models its own taxonomy. Negative — one migration PR of pure churn; until then zone-2 name collisions remain, mitigated only by the map.
- Confirmation: root `AGENTS.md` exists and names the three zones; `reflect --check` drift 0 after every zone-2 change; no working/marketing file sits directly in the root.
- Revisit when: the regroup (`STRUCT-1`) breaks external links or consumer expectations, or a monorepo layout (`apps/`) proves overweight for a docs-first repo.
- Related: ADR-005 (the manifest stays the client layout, unaffected by zone-2 grouping); `STRUCT-1` in `backlog.md`; the 2026-07-21 re-review notes. Also referenced by ADR-010 ("ADR-008/#46 drew the rule" that no records are minted for un-approved ideas).

## ADR-009 — Skills are lifecycle or transition — and transition skills never ship (status: Accepted (2026-07-22), date: 2026-07-21, revised 2026-07-22 while Proposed)

- Tags: skills, dist, lifecycle, adoption.
- Context: skills are two kinds. Some ARE the ways of working — a repo uses `spec-impact` or `pre-pr-review` on every change, for life. Others exist only to get a repo TO the standard — `align-to-standards`, `onboard-repo`, `modernize`, `greenfield-start` — the standard repo's own utility, not the target repo's process. `dist/` initially shipped all thirteen as one set. Owner's call (2026-07-22): a separation error — `dist/` is the resulting repository, and conversion machinery does not belong in the result. Decisive proof: `greenfield-start` runs before the target repo even exists — it cannot be a file inside it.
- Options: A — no distinction (every aligned repo permanently carries scaffolding; one-shot conversion machinery presented as daily process) — rejected. B — transition skills never ship; they run from the standard repo — **chosen** (the transition is always driven by an agent pointing at the standard: "align this repo to <standard>@<version>"; the target only receives what it keeps). C — ship both, classify, clean up at aligned (the first draft of this record): ships debris only to delete it later, needs reliable cleanup and re-materialization logic in `update-to-version` — "complexity purchased for nothing option B does not already give" — rejected.
- Decision (Option B) — classes:
  - Lifecycle (ship, stay forever): `spec-analyze`, `spec-converge`, `spec-impact`, `spec-reconcile`, `spec-update`, `add-to-backlog`, `backlog-from-specs`, `pre-pr-review`, `update-to-version` (recurring maintenance).
  - Transition (never shipped): `align-to-standards`, `onboard-repo`, `modernize`, `greenfield-start`. They live in the standard repo only (`skills/`), guarded source-only by `reflect.mjs`, and run in the agent's context when the user points a repo at the standard.
  - `SKILL-1` shrinks accordingly: no cleanup-of-skills machinery; `self-verify` just flags transition skills if found inside an aligned repo (a hand-copy mistake).
- Consequences: positive — `dist/` is purely "what the target repo keeps"; no cleanup/re-materialization; the utility-vs-skeleton separation is structural. Negative — a transition needs the standard repo reachable (fetch/degit), already true of align/update; offline transition of an air-gapped repo requires bringing the standard checkout along.
- Confirmation: `standard/.claude/skills/` contains exactly the lifecycle set named in the class table (root `AGENTS.md` — eleven since the engine extraction, ADR-015); `tree-check` fails if a transition skill leaks into the tree; `self-verify` flags transition skills found in a consuming repo. 2026-07-22 note: original confirmation said "exactly nine in `dist/`"; count and paths moved with ADR-014/015; class rule unchanged. The lifecycle set became the 11-skill spec-* family (analyze/converge/backlog-from-specs folded away), the leak guard is `tree-check.mjs`, and modernize lives as adoption's plan-then-refactor pass.
- Revisit when: skills gain a plugin/marketplace distribution (then nothing may need shipping in `dist/`), or a real air-gapped adoption case appears.
- Related: ADR-008 (zones rule applied to skills), ADR-010 (ephemeral class covers plan/tasks and idea docs; transition skills are simply never in the target), `SKILL-1` in `backlog.md`. Later: ADR-045's Correction moved `record-run` into this transition class.

## ADR-010 — One artifact lifecycle — permanent vs ephemeral — and the tracker posture (status: Accepted (2026-07-22), revised by ADR-028, date: 2026-07-21)

- Tags: lifecycle, ideas, spec-kit, tracker, backlog.
- Context — three questions kept re-opening (2026-07-21 notes pass; IDEA-1): (1) before a decision — where does a speculative idea live without polluting the decision log (ADR-008/#46 drew the rule; the home was undesigned); (2) after execution — Spec Kit's `plan.md`/`tasks.md` are scaffolding; left after a feature ships they are noise agents keep reading; (3) around the work — enabling/organizational tasks ("open an IT ticket for a token") are required but not spec content and worthless once done; and which work tracker, given many projects can't pay for one. These are one question: which artifacts are permanent, which ephemeral, where each phase lives.
- Options: A — everything stays in the repo (plans, tasks, done-work history): full audit trail but accretes debris, agents read dead scaffolding, "what is current" drowns in "what happened" — rejected, "git already IS the history". B — everything lives in the tracker (specs and decisions too): knowledge leaves the repo, agents lose context, wiki-rot returns — rejected, "the failure the standard exists to kill". C — one arc, two classes, one bridge — **chosen**: Permanent & living (repo): specs, decision records, docs, personas, backlog *intents*. Ephemeral (repo only while active): idea docs, `plan.md`/`tasks.md` (transition skills go further — never enter the target, ADR-009). Tracker: execution state, enabling/organizational tasks, work history.
- Decision (Option C), the arc phase by phase:
  1. Idea — `docs/ideas/<slug>.md`, statuses `idea -> exploring -> approved | parked | dropped` (then `graduated`). Explored end-to-end, no records minted (ADR-008 rule). On approval it graduates: backlog intent + spec + ADR/BDR as needed; the idea doc gets `graduated` + links. `parked` / `dropped` docs stay — cheap memory of why not.
  2. Refinement — the capability spec carries a Status: `in-refinement -> ready-to-develop -> in-development -> live`. `ready-to-develop` requires the clarify gate (a `## Clarifications` section, zero open `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]`) — discovery mechanically separated from build-ready work (the PO's view of the pipeline).
  3. Enabling work — anything required for development but not spec content (tokens, access, "agree X with Y") is encoded in spec front-matter (`needs_decision_records`-style keys); the bridge emits it to the tracker as a blocking Story. The spec prose never mentions it (field-proven in a private production repo).
  4. Execution state & history — live in the tracker, not the repo. The repo backlog holds intents; git holds change history. Default tracker: GitHub Issues (free, unlimited, code-adjacent, `gh`-scriptable). Adapters behind the same bridge convention: Jira (free <= 10 users; a field-proven git -> Jira bridge is the reference), Linear (free tier: unlimited members, 250 active issues). Bridge convention: one-way, git is the source of truth, created keys written back into front-matter (field-proven).
  5. Close & cleanup — when a feature reaches `live` and is reconciled, a cleanup step verifies against the code (not by interrogating the user) that the work landed, then removes scaffolding: `plan.md`, `tasks.md`, satisfied enabling keys. The spec, records, docs — the living truth — remain.
- Consequences: positive — agents read only living truth; the PO gets a pipeline view (in-refinement vs ready-to-develop); enabling work stops leaking into specs; free-tier projects get a zero-cost tracker posture; one bridge convention covers Jira/Linear/GH. Negative — cleanup must be reliable and code-verified (SKILL-1 mechanics, with ADR-009's transition-skill removal); teams without ANY tracker must at minimum use GitHub Issues; work history moves out of the repo — accepting that git + tracker are the ledger.
- Confirmation: `docs/ideas/` exists with the status header and index; capability specs carry `Status:`; self-verify flags a `live` capability that still has `plan.md`/`tasks.md`; the bridge refuses a slice failing the clarify gate.
- Revisit when: a regulated project needs the full work history in-repo (then an `archive/` overlay, not a reversal); or the tracker default stops being free.
- Related: ADR-008 (no records for un-approved ideas), ADR-009, the clarify gate (field-proven 2026-07), `LIFE-1/2`, `SKILL-1`, `IDEA-1` in `backlog.md`; tracker research: owner's private notes 2026-07 (free tiers verified: GH Issues unlimited, Jira <=10 users, Linear 250 active issues). Narrowed later by ADR-028 (clause 4: one aggregate outcome per closed cycle) and ADR-030 (`assignee` as present-tense cycle state); extended by ADR-046 (backlog `type`), cited by ADR-029, ADR-032, ADR-049, ADR-051, ADR-054.

## ADR-011 — One standard, two profiles — core and scale — declared in the manifest (status: Accepted (2026-07-22); trigger and solo/enterprise framing revised by ADR-040 (scale binds on what leaves the room, not headcount; CI is core), date: 2026-07-22)

- Tags: profiles, manifest, adoption, solo, enterprise.
- Context: the standard must serve a one-person side project and a corporate-grade repo without becoming a toy or a bureaucracy. Some patterns are universal (living specs, decision records, the agent entry point); others only pay off with more people (tracker bridges, release-notes curation, CI-enforced gates, UX research cadence). Owner's constraint (2026-07-22): keep it one repo — a fork or "light edition" must not appear — but a solo adopter must not carry enterprise ceremony, and the distinction must not rot into unreadable footnotes.
- Options: A — a separate light repo / branch / subproject (two artifacts drift immediately, every fix lands twice, light->full becomes a migration — "the exact split-maintenance failure the owner fears") — rejected. B — two prose documents ("full" vs "light" checklists): prose drifts, nothing enforces it — rejected as the primary mechanism (fine as a rendered view). C — a `profile` field on manifest entries, verified per profile — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C) / rules:
  - Every entry in `standard.manifest.json` (files / sections / guards / decisions) declares `profile: "core"` or `"scale"`. A repo states its profile next to its version pin; `self-verify --profile core|scale` (solo/team accepted as aliases) counts drift against the right subset. Docs render the two views FROM the manifest so prose cannot drift. Upgrading profile is a flag flip plus the delta — same muscle as `update-to-version`.
  - Dividing principle (so future entries classify themselves): "**Core is whatever keeps knowledge alive; scale is whatever coordinates people.**" (second half made specific by ADR-040).
  - Core (every repo, even one person): `AGENTS.md` entry point, taxonomy, living capability specs, decision records + catalog, backlog, ideas space, version pin + manifest + self-verify, living-docs rule, supply-chain cooldown.
  - Scale (teams / enterprise): tracker bridge + statuses mirrored out, curated release notes (two-changelog stays, curation is scale), CI-enforced gates (solo runs the same guards locally/pre-commit — ADR-040 later found this description backwards: CI is core), personas as a hard gate with a full roster (solo: one persona minimum, the gate stays), UX research cadence + design-token handoff, C4 diagram discipline.
  - Exact per-entry assignment executed as `PROF-1` (manifest field + self-verify support + the adoption doc's profile picker).
  - Note (2026-07-22): human-facing vocabulary unified to the manifest's words — `--profile core|scale` everywhere; solo/team stay accepted as deprecated aliases.
- Consequences: positive — one repo, one source of truth; a solo project adopts in an afternoon; an enterprise gets the full posture; profile upgrade is a measured delta; light/full views are rendered, never hand-maintained. Negative — every future manifest entry must declare a profile; the standard's docs must resist explaining everything twice (the profile column, not parallel chapters).
- Confirmation: `standard.manifest.json` entries carry `profile`; `self-verify --profile` exists and CI uses it; `docs/adoption.md` tells an adopter how to pick; a solo repo passing `--profile core` shows drift 0 while visibly not carrying scale-only artifacts.
- Revisit when: a third audience genuinely appears (e.g. regulated industries needing an `audit` profile), or profile flags start appearing on individual rules inside docs — a sign the two-profile granularity is too coarse.
- Related: ADR-005 (manifest schema extended), ADR-008/009/010, `PROF-1` in `backlog.md`. Revised by ADR-040; cited by ADR-016 (stacks reuse the `core|scale` axis), ADR-028 (scale only).

## ADR-012 — In-repo instructions are the source of truth — never personal memory or config (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-22)

- Tags: consistency, agents, conventions, onboarding.
- Context: "how to work here" knowledge (agent rules, repo gotchas, tool settings, "always pass the full tenant id when editing its template") accumulates closest to whoever learned it: a personal `~/.claude` config, an agent's memory, a head. The repo splits into invisible variants; each contributor (and their agent) works under different rules, undiscoverable by construction. "For a standard whose premise is 'the repo is the context', this is the quietest possible failure."
- Options: A — tolerate personal stores, document the important bits sometimes (guarantees divergence; review impossible — "works under my rules") — rejected. B — in-repo instructions are the single source of truth — **chosen**. C — central org-wide rulebook outside any repo (solves per-person divergence but reintroduces wiki-drift and breaks the self-contained-repo premise) — rejected.
- Decision (Option B), two operational halves:
  1. Location discipline (CONS-2): every rule kind has a named home — conventions in `AGENTS.md`/`docs/conventions`, process in `ways-of-working`, contribution mechanics in `CONTRIBUTING`, behavior in specs, decisions in records. A rule that exists only outside the repo (memory, personal config, chat) is treated as missing; finding one is a defect fixed by landing it in its home.
  2. Elicitation on entry (CONS-3): `align-to-standards` and `onboard-repo` explicitly ask for the unwritten rules — tribal knowledge, personal-config instructions, "everyone knows" gotchas — and land them in-repo at the right home.
  - Rules: personal memory and global configs may *point* at repo rules, never *hold* them; "It's in my memory / my dotfiles" is rejected in review as a location for a repo rule.
- Consequences: positive — any agent or human, fresh, works under the same rules; review can check rule changes like code changes; the repo survives personnel and tool churn. Negative — personal convenience configs must be split (pointer vs content); the elicitation step adds questions to onboarding — deliberately.
- Confirmation: conventions state the discipline; align/onboard skills carry the elicitation step; review culture rejects "it's in my memory" as a rule location.
- Revisit when: agent platforms ship portable, repo-scoped memory that is itself versioned in the repo — then it *is* an in-repo instruction and the distinction dissolves.
- Related: the CONS-1..3 epic in `backlog.md`; ADR-004; the coding-agent persona's pains in `docs/personas.md`. Cited by ADR-019, ADR-021, ADR-027.

## ADR-014 — One authored tree — standard/ is the standard, at real-repo paths (status: Accepted (2026-07-22), date: 2026-07-22)

- Tags: structure, repo-layout, distribution, simplification.
- Context: ADR-008 split the repo into a by-concern source (`standard/`) and a committed assembled snapshot (`dist/`), synced by `tools/reflect.mjs`. Negative — one large migration; the tree mixes hand-authored files with client-authored shells, so the manifest's `adapt` field carries the distinction alone; git history for moved files crosses a rename boundary.

- Revisit when: the tree needs per-client build-time variation that authoring cannot express (then a build step returns for that slice only), or a second consumable tree (a new layer) appears and leak rules need generalizing.
- Related: ADR-008 (revised), ADR-004, ADR-005 (manifest stays the client contract), ADR-015 (same wave); the 2026-07-22 review and decision plan (owner's private notes). Cited by ADR-023, ADR-031.

## ADR-016 — Technology best practices live in satellite repos, discovered by a registry (status: Accepted (2026-07-22); standards-range clause revised by ADR-022 (stacks are linked, not version-locked), date: 2026-07-22)

One repo per technology in the `repository-standards` organization, named for the technology alone; first: `repository-standards/node` (shipped as standalone `repository-standards-<technology>` while repos lived under a personal account; the org now holds the family, so the prefix moved from the name into the namespace).   3. The registry carries no versions; the core moves and satellites chase, never the reverse. Adopters degit stacks at tags. (Range clause revised by ADR-022: no range.)
  5. The align router detects the target repo's technology (lockfiles, manifests) and offers the matching best practices; greenfield asks, then degits the stack's starter.
  - Alignment mechanics (added 2026-07-22, same change): a stack ships `stack.manifest.json` — the same schema as the core manifest (ADR-005), plus `technology` and its `standards` range. The core's align machinery is reused whole: the router classifies a brownfield repo against the stack manifest's entries and walks it in waves; an adopting repo carries the stack manifest beside the core one, and `self-verify` merges both into one drift number. Greenfield gets the same file copied at scaffold time. The stack's update story (a versioned manifest delta like `update-to-version`) is future work, noted so it is not mistaken for done.
- Consequences: positive — the core repo is technology-silent (R20 reworded); stack releases never touch the standard's version; the starter gets real CI; a new technology is one new repo plus one registry line; `repository-standards/node` becomes the standard's first genuinely aligned adopter once it pins `.standards-version`. Negative — two repos to maintain (different kinds of care); cross-repo links replace relative ones; the registry is one more file whose entries the owner alone merges.
- Confirmation: `stacks.json` exists and lists node; `stacks/` is gone from this repo; the align router's technology step reads the registry; the stack repo's `stack.manifest.json` declares its standards range and points back here.
- Revisit when: a second maintainer or organization wants in (the registry's solo-merge gate needs a policy), or stack repos multiply past what one registry file reads cleanly.
- Related: ADR-008 (its stacks-beside-core overlay clause superseded by this), ADR-014, ADR-011 (`core|scale` axis reused), R20 in `standard/SPEC.md`. Cited by ADR-017, ADR-020, ADR-021, ADR-022, ADR-037, ADR-039.

## ADR-017 — Everything a repo consumes is pinned exact (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: supply-chain, dependencies, reproducibility.
- Context: a repo consumes software it did not write: package dependencies, container base images, CI runners and actions. Every one has an auto-resolution mechanism that moves underneath the repo — semver ranges (`^`/`~`) resolve to newest, `latest` and floating image tags (`node:24`, `postgres`) re-point on every pull, actions tracked by branch or major tag change without a commit. Result: drift nobody reviewed — local, CI and production can each run a different graph; a rebuild of the same commit produces a different artifact; a compromised upstream release walks in on its own. The standard already had a release-age cooldown principle (never install a version younger than seven days) but said nothing about pinning, so the cooldown could be satisfied while the resolved graph still floated.
- Options: ranges plus a committed lockfile (freezes the graph only where a lockfile exists and is honored; images, actions, runners have no lockfile; range manifests invite silent re-resolution in tooling that ignores or regenerates the lock — half the surface stays floating) — rejected. Pin exact everywhere — **chosen** (cost: upgrades stop being free; must be performed deliberately and regularly or the repo quietly ages). Pin only production dependencies (dev tooling and CI float) — rejected: dev tooling and CI are exactly where supply-chain attacks land first (postinstall scripts, build steps), and "it works in CI, fails locally" drift stays.
- Decision / rules:
  - "We will pin exact, everywhere, and upgrade only by explicit diff."
  - Dependency manifests, overrides and resolutions carry exact versions — no `^`, `~`, ranges or `latest` — sealed by a committed lockfile.
  - Container images, CI runners and actions name an exact version or digest, never a floating tag.
  - A new version additionally clears the release-age cooldown before adoption; a critical security fix may bypass the cooldown through a recorded, temporary exclusion.
  - Normative as R21 in `standard/SPEC.md`; per-stack mechanics (package-manager settings enforcing cooldown and exact saves) live in the stack repos (Layer 2).
- Consequences: positive — reproducible builds; no unreviewed code via auto-resolution; upgrades are visible, reviewable diffs with a place in the changelog. Negative — routine dependency-update passes become a recurring chore that must happen; exact pins produce larger, noisier update diffs. Follow-ups: stack repos document enforcement mechanics for their package manager and container tooling.
- Confirmation: review — an update PR shows exact-to-exact diffs and nothing else floats. Stack repos MAY add mechanical checks (a lint rejecting range specifiers and floating image tags) and enforce the cooldown in the package manager itself.
- Revisit when: a consumed ecosystem makes exact pinning impractical (a platform that only accepts ranges), or lockfile-plus-range tooling becomes reliable across the whole surface — images and CI included — making manifest-level pinning redundant.
- Related: `standard/SPEC.md` R21; `standard/docs/PRINCIPLES.md` (supply-chain cooldown, exact versions); ADR-002-style capability specs unaffected; stack mechanics per ADR-016. Cited by ADR-022 (the stack link is discovery metadata, not a consumed artifact).

## ADR-018 — History lives in the changelog, never inside living documents (status: Accepted; the `changes/` fragments mechanism it mentioned was removed on 2026-08-02 — one path at every profile, `## Unreleased` in `CHANGELOG.md`; the decision stands unchanged, date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: documentation, changelog, specs.
- Context: the standard has a changelog process with two outputs — the complete technical `CHANGELOG.md` and the curated stakeholder `RELEASE-NOTES.md` — fed from one source. In the field a second, unplanned history channel appeared: capability specs growing their own `## Change log` sections, one dated entry per change, partly to satisfy the coupling guard's "touch the spec" demand on changes with no behavioral delta (dependency bumps matched by broad capability globs). Structurally wrong: the spec — a living document whose contract is "the current version is the truth, git is the history" (R4) — starts duplicating git and the changelog, grows without bound, buries behavior under bookkeeping.
- Options: per-document change-log sections (duplicates git and changelog, drifts from both, bloats, three competing histories) — rejected. Git only (no curated history) — rejected: two audiences (engineer, stakeholder) need curated views git does not give. One history home: the changelog process — **chosen** (living documents describe the present only; every change event recorded once in the changelog source — Unreleased section, or `changes/` fragments at scale [since removed] — assembled into the two outputs at release).
- Decision / rules:
  - "We will keep exactly one accumulating history: the changelog process."
  - A living document — a spec, ARCHITECTURE, a runbook — describes the present and MUST NOT carry a `## Change log` / `## History` section; git holds every past state, the changelog holds the curated record (technical and stakeholder). R4 in `standard/SPEC.md` now states this.
  - Supporting rule: capability-map globs bind behavior-bearing source and SHOULD NOT match dependency manifests or lockfiles (a version bump is not a behavior change).
  - Supporting rule: when the coupling guard fires on a genuinely behavior-free change, the answer is to reconcile the spec's content or narrow the map — never to append a history note to the spec.
- Consequences: positive — specs stay readable and stay specs; one history, two audiences; routine dependency updates stop forcing ceremonial spec edits. Negative — a reader wanting "what changed lately" looks at git or the changelog — one hop away. Follow-ups: repos that accumulated in-spec change-log sections migrate them into the changelog and strip the sections; capability maps get narrowed so manifests and lockfiles no longer trip the guard.
- Confirmation: review, plus the structure lint direction: a spec containing a change-log section fails the spec shape; until that check ships, `pre-pr-review` and align flag it.
- Revisit when: a regulated context requires an in-document audit trail that git and the changelog provably cannot serve, or the changelog process is retired.
- Related: R4, R11, R18 in `standard/SPEC.md`; `standard/docs/changelog-process.md`; `standard/specs/enforcement.md` (coupling guard, map hygiene); ADR-001. Cited by ADR-027 (schema file carries no change-log either), ADR-035, ADR-051 (the record ADR-051 narrows and "the strongest case against" it).

## ADR-019 — Lifecycle procedures are agent-portable — Claude skills are the reference form (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: agents, skills, portability, manifest.
- Context: the manifest required `.claude/skills` and `scripts/spec` for the core profile, citing rules that never mention them (the CI-gate rule mandates guards gate CI; the clarify rule mandates passing the gate — neither says a repo must carry a Claude-format skill directory). Worse: a team whose agent tooling is Cursor, Copilot or Windsurf could not reach drift 0 without committing another vendor's agent layout, undercutting "stack-agnostic Layer 1". Yet dropping the requirement would gut the core promise — the lifecycle procedures (spec loop, backlog capture, pre-PR review, version updates) are the standard as executable behavior; a repo without them has the documents but not the practice.
- Options: A — make the skills optional (`required: false`): honest about vendors but a compliant repo could carry no executable procedures — rejected. B — ship the procedures for every agent vendor: multiplies maintenance by vendor count, each with format churn — "premature today" — rejected. C — one reference implementation, mandatory port — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C) / rules:
  1. New spec rule R22 makes the lifecycle procedures normative: they MUST ship in the repo in a form the repo's coding agent can execute.
  2. `.claude/skills/` plus `scripts/spec/` is the reference implementation — the paved road, shipped in the tree, required by the manifest.
  3. A repo whose agent tooling is not Claude MUST port the shipped skills to its agent's own instruction mechanism (e.g. `.agents/skills`) — strictly, file by file, gates included — before claiming compliance. The manifest's `altPaths` accepts the ported location, so `self-verify` counts either form.
  4. A partial port is drift, not a variant: the port carries every procedure and every gate, or the repo is not aligned.
  5. The manifest entries for `.claude/skills` and `scripts/spec` cite R22 — the rule they actually enforce.

Negative — "strictly and completely" is review-verified, not mechanical (self-verify checks the ported directory exists, not that the port is faithful); the port is real work for a non-Claude team, and they keep it current when the reference skills move.
- Revisit when: a second agent format stabilizes enough (adoption + spec stability) that shipping it alongside the Claude form costs less than every adopter porting individually — then option B returns for that one format.
- Related: ADR-009, ADR-012, ADR-015. Cited by ADR-045 (why `record-run` ships as an executable skill).

## ADR-020 — Adoption is intake-first — measure, then one question round (status: Accepted (2026-07-29), date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: methodology, adoption, layer-2, process.
- Context: the adoption flow had grown three heads. The align router (`skills/align-to-standards/SKILL.md`) ran the technology step "after routing, detection first"; the greenfield phase asked about the stack at its step 4; the brownfield phase offered it at step 8 — the very end, after specs and guards. Three files, three timings. The technology question reached the user late or never: a brownfield run could reconcile a long while before the stack offer; Layer 2 consent was never explicitly gathered; no route for "tell me where I stand and give me the plan" (assessment only) or "just add the node stack to my pinned repo". The gates in `standard/docs/adoption.md` claimed one rigid order for both directions, though brownfield factually assesses before it has personas. The counted plan said how much work but not who (business, architect, dev, or the agent) must act on each item.
- Options: A — keep the late/stack-last flow — rejected (timings contradicted each other; Layer 2 consent implicit; assessment-only unnameable). B — technology-first hard reorder (ask the stack first, scaffold immediately) — rejected: for whom -> what -> how holds for greenfield (ADR-006); the stack serves the product, so its ADRs and scaffold stay after personas and product. C — intake-first — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C):
  1. Step 0 — intake opens every align run. Measure first: `.standards-version` present (run `self-verify`), a partial skeleton (`AGENTS.md`, `docs/`, `specs/`) without a pin, or nothing. Then one short question round: intent (start a new repo / bring an existing repo to the standard / assessment only / update the pin), technology (detected from evidence — `package.json`, `pyproject.toml`, `go.mod`, `Cargo.toml`, `*.csproj` — then confirmed; greenfield asked outright), appetite (one focused PR vs a program of waves), plan-only vs execute. (ADR-042 lists the round as: intent, technology, appetite, profile, tracked-work location, existing-knowledge sources, plan-only-vs-execute.)
  2. Layer 2 consent is gathered at intake with the technology answer ("this repo is <technology> - I'll offer the <technology> best practices from the registry alongside Layer 1; ok?"). Reconciliation stays phase-defined: brownfield right after the assessment (not at the end), greenfield after personas and product, a pinned repo adding a stack immediately (a fourth routing row). Compatibility stays loose: the stack links by its `registry` back-pointer, not a core version range (ADR-022); a core manifest-contract break is a recorded migration — on any mismatch the agent warns and the user decides, never a hard stop.
  3. One gate walk, two evidence sources: both directions walk 0 -> 2 -> 1 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5 -> 6. Greenfield's vision interview and brownfield's assessment are the same gate (2) with different evidence; personas (1) are named from that evidence and then gate everything downstream. Brownfield: personas reconstructed from the assessment's evidence (auth roles, UI surfaces, API consumers), then confirmed. Invariants: no specs before confirmed personas; no recorded decisions before intake + assessment; every gate produces its artifact.
  4. Assessment-only is a legal, named outcome: deliver the health report plus the counted plan, then stop. "A product of the flow, not a failure mode."
  5. Every plan item names its owner role — product/business (`PRODUCT.md`, BDRs, personas confirmation), architect (ADRs, boundaries), dev (specs, code, guards), agent (mechanical work it can do alone) — as an `owner` field in the backlog format, grouped the same way in the assessment's health report.
- Consequences: positive — one question round replaces scattered-or-never; Layer 2 consented before work starts; the phase files agree; "where do I stand" is a first-class run; the counted plan says who must act so it can be handed to a team. Negative — one more question round before any work, even a trivial run; the phase files must stay cross-referenced to the gates (each step naming the gate it fills) — a coupling every future edit must maintain.
- Confirmation: `SKILL.md` opens with step 0 and routes four ways; `onboard.md` runs the stack offer right after the assessment and reconstructs personas before any spec; `greenfield.md` scaffolds without re-asking; `adoption.md` states both walk orders and the assessment-only stop; the backlog format carries `owner`.
- Revisit when: a new routing path emerges beyond the four named (start new / bring existing / assessment only / add a stack to a pinned repo) needing its own timing for the technology question; or the phase files drift out of sync with the gate cross-references to the point the single question round no longer reflects what each phase does.
- Related: ADR-006, ADR-016, ADR-022. Extended by ADR-042 (intake artifact), ADR-043 (decision-process detection), ADR-048 (plan-only/execute split into intent + consent), ADR-055/059/060 (Step -1 before Step 0).

## ADR-021 — Adoption feeds the standard — the loop closes upstream (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: feedback, ecosystem, issues, adoption.
- Context: every adoption run generates exactly the signal a standard lives on — and all of it stayed in the target repo: deviations became manifest `exceptions`, drift went to the client's backlog, tribal knowledge landed at the client's taxonomy homes. Nothing flowed back. Registry blind spot: when a technology has no official stack, the fallback writes a local `docs/stack-decisions.md` and the demand signal (which stack to build next — what the stacks backlog waits for) is discarded. The standard's North Star (guidance quality, retention) had no inbound channel; the open-questions system invites human challengers but no adoption-path file points the executing agent at it.
- Options: A — automatic telemetry / auto-filed issues (an agent publishing to a third-party repo without a per-run human yes is the silent side effect the standard bans elsewhere) — rejected. B — leave it to humans (status quo; the human never sees the friction the agent hit; signal dies in the session) — rejected. C — consent-gated agent offers, structured channels — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C):
  1. Missing-stack requests: when the registry has no stack for the detected technology, the align flow — after offering the local fallback document — offers (with consent, never automatically) to open a stack request issue on the standards repo, attaching detection evidence and the generated `stack-decisions.md` as seed material.
  2. Adoption friction reports: the align flow closes with an upstream review — if the run recorded manifest exceptions, hit an instruction it could not follow, had to ask the user something the standard should have answered, met a registry gap or a guard false-positive — the agent offers to file each as an adoption friction issue (or a PR, for a concrete doc fix). `update-to-version` carries a lighter echo of the same step.
  3. Structured channels: the core repo carries `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/` forms — stack request, adoption friction, bug — so agent-filed and human-filed signal arrives in one shape. CONTRIBUTING gains a "Feedback from adopters" section naming the channels.
  4. Consent is per action: the agent proposes with a ready title and body; the user says yes or no per issue. No consent, no upstream side effect — the learning still lands in the target repo's records.
- Consequences: positive — the registry gets its demand signal; structured field evidence (same genre as the case studies); the ecosystem stops depending on the owner noticing everything. Negative — one more decision point at the end of a run; issue quality depends on the agent's summary discipline — templates carry required fields to keep the floor.
- Revisit when: agent-filed issue quality proves poor despite required fields (the only thing standing between this and option B); or the no-silent-side-effects stance changes elsewhere (the only thing making option A reconsiderable).
- Related: ADR-016, ADR-020, ADR-012, the open-questions system. Pattern reused by ADR-045 (`record-run`), cited by ADR-047, ADR-055, ADR-061.

## ADR-022 — Stacks are linked, not version-locked — no standards version requirement (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: stacks, versioning, coupling, ecosystem.
- Context: ADR-016 gave each stack repo a `standards: ">=X <Y"` range. In practice fiction on both sides: the only stack declared `>=0.8 <1` while the core stood at 0.7.2 (formally incompatible with everything that existed), no tool evaluated the range, and the `<1` ceiling would have excluded the eventual 1.0 release. Deeper: a stack depends on the core's manifest contract (schema and adapt classes that let `self-verify` merge the two layers), not on the core's release cadence; that contract changes rarely; a range models a lockstep that does not exist.
- Options: A — keep the range, enforce it (adds a semver gate; enforces a coupling that is not real; every core release invites a pointless range chase). B — loosen the range (`>=0.7`, no ceiling; still implies lockstep, still needs maintaining). C — drop the requirement; keep the link — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C):
  1. `stack.manifest.json` carries no standards version requirement. The `registry` back-pointer (plus `technology`) is the linkage: this repo is a satellite of repository-standards, discovered via `stacks.json`.
  2. `self-verify` notes the stack layer's presence and technology and merges its entries — nothing version-related is checked or warned about.
  3. If the core ever breaks the manifest contract itself (schema, adapt classes), that is an explicit, recorded migration in the core's changelog — stacks chase it as a bug fix, not a range bump.
  4. ADR-016's range clause revised; everything else in ADR-016 stands.
- Consequences: positive — fictional incompatibility disappears; stacks and core release on independent clocks; one less field and gate. Negative — nothing mechanical stops a stale stack drifting behind a core manifest-contract change; the ecosystem relies on the stack's own CI (the boot pulse) and the delisting policy in `stacks.json`.
- Revisit when: the core breaks its manifest contract (named as requiring an explicit, recorded migration); or stale stacks drifting behind a contract change go undetected by the boot pulse and the delisting policy.
- Related: ADR-016, ADR-017. Cited by ADR-020, ADR-025, ADR-037.

## ADR-023 — Method docs live beside the tree, not inside it (status: Accepted; reference semantics revised by ADR-025 (the manual is read at latest (`main`), the pin records the state a repo last aligned to), date: 2026-07-29)

- Tags: structure, repo-layout, distribution, reference.
- Context: the shipped tree promised to be "the client repo at day zero" yet carried documents no adopted repo has reason to own: the adoption checkmap (how a repo gets ON the standard — a process the client has finished), the repo assessment (a procedure the transition agent runs from a checkout of this repo), the taxonomy and decision checklist (already `adapt: reference` — "do not copy" — while sitting in the copyable tree). The reference class was internally contradictory: reference entries carried `required: true` and self-verify checked file existence in the client repo, mechanically forcing a copy of the thing the class says is never copied. Every copied method doc drifts on every revision — the failure ADR-004 kills for decision records.
- Options: A — reference-in-tree (flip to `adapt: reference`, leave in tree; the tree keeps lying about being the client layout). B — method docs move out of the tree — **chosen**. C — keep copying — rejected on ADR-004's logic.
- Decision (Option B):
  1. The standard's method manual lives at `docs/method/`: the adoption checkmap, the repo assessment, the taxonomy, the decision checklist, ways of working, and the changelog process. Versioned with the standard and rendered on the docs site from the same source.
  2. The shipped tree carries only two kinds of documents: client-authored artifacts (PRODUCT, ARCHITECTURE, PRINCIPLES, personas, backlog, records, ideas, journeys, research, runbooks, specs, conventions — shells and merge-content the client owns) and operating manuals for shipped tools (`docs/self-verify.md` beside the verifier, `specs/enforcement.md` beside the guards).
  3. Clients adopt the method manual by reference at the pinned version (ADR-004 extended; ADR-025 later: at latest). The manifest carries them in a `references` section (id, path, purpose, rule); `self-verify` notes them and never checks for files; `tree-check` verifies each referenced path resolves in this repo.
  4. The spec's taxonomy wording follows: the map of where knowledge lands is the standard's, adopted by reference — not a file every client carries.
- Consequences: positive — the tree is exactly "what you get"; the reference-but-shipped class is gone by construction; method revisions reach every client instantly; manifest reference semantics match ADR-004's words. Negative — reading the method needs the standards checkout or the pinned link (offline clients keep only their own artifacts); every existing link to old tree paths re-pointed once; `update-to-version` deltas for references are metadata-only.

## ADR-024 — Discovery dossiers live beside the specs — and are never normative (status: Accepted; narrowed by ADR-049 (the stamp bounds asking, not reading), date: 2026-07-30)

- Tags: method, specs, discovery, taxonomy, lifecycle.
- Context: a feature rarely starts as a spec; it starts as weeks of discovery — meetings, mails, screenshots, half-decisions — whose provenance matters ("it was said at THAT meeting", "this came from the client's mail"). The taxonomy had no home. An idea (`docs/ideas/`, ADR-010) is a one-file speculation that may never ship; a spec is the updated specification of current or target state — polluting it with raw materials would rot it. Teams held transcripts in chat and drives — failure mode #1 ("decisions evaporate in chat") wearing a different coat. Dangerous edge case: a spec is built, clarifications answered and recorded — later an agent reads discovery material that differs from what the spec decided and re-litigates; the user explains the same call a second time.
- Options: A — materials inside the spec directory (`specs/<cap>/discovery/`) — rejected: discovery precedes knowing the capability; one topic feeds many specs (and vice versa); lifecycles differ (a spec lives forever, a dossier peaks and freezes); `specs/` is a guarded normative surface. B — stretch `docs/ideas/` — rejected: an idea is a one-file maybe with no materials; a dossier is an active, materials-bearing stage between idea and spec. C — transcripts in the repo — rejected: volume, noise, personal data; the repo gets the distillate, raw recordings stay in the meeting tool or drive, linked. D — a dossier folder per topic, upstream of the specs — **chosen**.
- Decision:
  1. Home: `docs/discovery/<topic>/` — one dossier per discovery topic, named after the topic, not after a spec or capability; linking by reference both ways (dossier README links the specs it fed; a spec cites dossier entries in its `## Clarifications`).
  2. Provenance is first-class: each entry stamped with date + source (`2026-07-30-meeting-with-x.md`, `2026-07-28-mail-from-y.md`), carries the essence plus a link to the raw source. Raw transcripts stay out of the repo.
  3. A dossier is never normative. Altitude: PRINCIPLES -> records -> specs -> conventions -> **discovery**. A difference between a dossier and a spec is not a conflict — the spec IS the resolution. An agent never re-asks about it.
  4. One-time consumption is recorded, not remembered: the dossier README carries a `Last reconciled:` stamp (date / spec commit). Agents ask only about entries newer than the stamp. Entry lifecycle: `new -> folded-into-spec | superseded-by-record | open`. A consciously rejected discovery input becomes a record (MADR's rejected alternatives), documenting the difference once and forever. (Rule 4 narrowed by ADR-049: the stamp bounds asking, never reading.)
  5. Typed blockers make an early spec buildable-in-progress: a spec drafted during discovery uses the open-marker family — CLARIFICATION (a question), DECISION (a missing ADR/BDR), INPUT (e.g. a UX design), ASSET (e.g. credentials) — each naming what is missing and who brings it. Any open marker keeps the spec at `in-refinement`; the clarify gate blocks the whole family, not just questions.
  6. Curator and consumer are separate skills: `discovery-digest` curates the dossier (ingest a transcript/mail, update the summary, flag contradictions between entries, report readiness for a spec) and never writes specs; the `spec-*` skills consume the dossier at specify/clarify/plan time and never curate it.
  7. No new SPEC rule: the taxonomy (R5) names the home; the manifest ships the optional `docs/discovery` shell under it. If practice hardens the dossier into a MUST, a rule can be minted then.
- Consequences: meeting knowledge has a home with provenance; the spec stays a specification. The re-ask problem is closed mechanically: precedence (never normative) + the stamp + entry markers mean a user explains a call exactly once, and the explanation has an address. A team can draft the spec on day one of discovery and see what is missing and who owes it. One more lifecycle skill ships (`discovery-digest`); the method gains a worked-example doc (`docs/method/discovery.md`, adopted by reference — ADR-023).
- Confirmation: the clarify gate (`scripts/spec/check-spec-clarified.sh`) fails a spec with any open marker of the family; `docs/discovery` is a manifest entry (optional, R5) so tree-check holds the shell and self-verify counts it when the repo opts in; the method doc resolves as a manifest reference (tree-check gate). Dossier hygiene — stamps updated, entries marked, contradictions surfaced — is review-verified via the `discovery-digest` skill's checklist, in the judgment tier.
- Revisit when: practice hardens the dossier into a MUST — the record declines to mint a new SPEC rule now (`docs/discovery` ships as optional, not required) on the basis that a rule can be written once the need is real.
- Related: ADR-010, ADR-023. Narrowed by ADR-049; cited by ADR-033 (dossier is non-normative; the decision log is what binds), ADR-050, ADR-054.

## ADR-025 — The standard is living — latest is the only target (status: Accepted, date: 2026-07-30)

- Tags: versioning, distribution, philosophy, ecosystem.
- Context: two readings of the version mechanics coexisted and disagreed. Mechanics: a repo pins the state it aligned to (`.standards-version`), updates apply deltas, self-verify checks against the pinned manifest. Prose, in places, said more: "adopted by reference at the pinned version" implied references resolve at a tag, while every shipped link points at `main`; the first stack once declared `standards >=0.8 <1` (killed by ADR-022). The review flagged the gap as a defect ("pinned version is words - links point at main"). Owner's call: the links are right, the words were wrong.
- Decision / rules — "The standard is living, and every repo's target is always the latest.":
  1. No version ranges or requirements, anywhere, ever. Not between stack and core (ADR-022 stands), not on landings, not in manifests, not in prose. "The standard is not a dependency to constrain; it is a reference to true up to, and the reference moves."
  2. `.standards-version` is a bookmark, not an anchor: it records the state a repo last aligned to (what makes an update a delta and self-verify a meaningful assertion). It never names a version to stay at; nothing may treat it as a compatibility constraint.
  3. References resolve at `main`, deliberately. `main` IS the living standard. Method docs, decisions and every by-reference link read latest; the canonical phrase is "adopted by reference from the living standard - always latest". Tags, when cut, are milestones for changelogs and update notifications — never anchors for references.
  4. Staying current is a notification, not a lock: the channel proposes a pin bump the repo's agent then executes (`update-to-version`): a Renovate custom manager watching `.standards-version` against this repo's tags, a shipped watch workflow for repos without a bot, possibly an npm package later — a tooling and reach channel, not a dependency.
- Options: A — version-anchored references (`blob/<tag>` links rewritten at align/update) — rejected: freezes clients on stale method text, invents a re-point step nobody needs, models the standard as a dependency. B — version ranges — rejected once (ADR-022) and again on principle. C — latest-first, pin as bookmark — **chosen**.
- Consequences: the "pinned version" phrasing left every live surface; historical records keep their original wording with revision notes (ADR-004, ADR-023). A repo can be behind (its pin says so) but never *wrong* for being behind; fleet owners sort by pin distance, not "supported" status. Anyone reading a by-reference link sees the standard's current thinking — the accepted trade: text may be newer than the reader's pin until they update.
- Confirmation: ADR-022's mechanics already enforce the range ban; the canonical phrase is greppable — a live surface saying "by reference at the/your pinned version" is a defect; the update channel lands as shipped templates (watch workflow, Renovate rule) — in the backlog until then.
- Revisit when: the update channel (Renovate custom manager, shipped watch workflow, possible npm package) fails to keep repos actually moving forward — staying current is a notification, not a lock, which only holds if the notification works; without it a repo can be behind indefinitely, reopening the case for some form of anchoring.
- Related: cited by ADR-031 (each repo adopted at its own latest), ADR-056 (what a tag is), README gist of ADR-004.

## ADR-026 — Rebase-merge onto a linear `main` (status: Accepted; base-on-the-mainline clause narrowed by ADR-035 (a declared, never-rewritten release line is a legal base too), date: 2026-07-31)

- Tags: git, workflow, contribution, history.
- Context: the standard said how work is specified, decided, reviewed and released — and nothing about how it reaches `main`. This repo lost three PRs' worth of work to that gap (the stacked-PRs case study `../case-studies/stacked-prs-on-a-moving-base.md`), and the base-on-main rule written afterwards landed only in this repo's own `CONTRIBUTING.md` — a client never received it. Rebase-vs-merge is the textbook re-litigable choice, so the claim was tested against evidence. Two parts hold unconditionally: back-merging the base into a branch is bad practice (kernel maintainer guidance: muddies history, imports unrelated breakage into bisect, invalidates testing the branch earned — docs.kernel.org/maintainer/rebasing-and-merging.html); rewriting history others build on is destructive (Git's Golden Rule — Pro Git, "The Perils of Rebasing"). One part does not hold as usually stated: "merge commits are an antipattern" — a merge commit at integration time is legitimate (the kernel takes them by design; `git log --first-parent` / `git bisect --first-parent` read it fine). What is harmful is the braid repeated back-merges produce, not the merge commit. So the rule bans the braid and the stranded base, and picks an integration method deliberately.
- Options: merge commit (`--no-ff`) as default — what lands is exactly what CI tested, PR boundaries visible, `--first-parent` readable; rejected as default: reads well only with discipline most repos don't hold, and normalizes back-merging. Squash-merge everything — one commit per PR, no hygiene demand, trivial revert; kept as the sanctioned alternative, not the default: discards a well-built sequence (refactor separated from the behavior change), coarsens bisect to whole PRs, buries authorship. Semi-linear (rebase, then `--no-ff`) — genuinely the best of both, shipped by GitLab and Azure DevOps; rejected on availability only: GitHub does not implement it and GitHub is the paved-road platform. Rebase-merge with an explicit commit-hygiene bar — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules:
  - "The fork this record settles is the integration method: rebase-merge onto a linear `main`, with squash-merge as the sanctioned alternative."
  - Rebase-merge publishes every commit, so it is conditional — each commit must be complete, buildable and reviewed on its own. A repo that will not hold that bar uses squash-merge and records that as its branching decision. Both are compliant; drifting between them per-PR is not.
  - The rest of R23 is deliberately not decided here because none of it is a live fork: rebasing onto the base instead of back-merging, basing every PR on the mainline, never rewriting a branch someone else builds on — settled practice whose alternative is simply wrong. Those are rules, with their why inline, in `standard/docs/conventions.md` (merged into each repo's `AGENTS.md` at adoption) — normative because R23 says so. Named here only so nobody reopens them as options.
- Consequences: positive — `main` is a sequence of finished units: readable, bisectable at commit resolution, revertable one change at a time; the failure that cost three PRs is illegal by construction (no stacking on a base that will be rewritten); the rule ships instead of living in one repo's contributing file. Negative — GitHub's rebase-and-merge mints new SHAs and always rewrites committer information, so signatures do not survive and the landed commits were never tested in exactly that form — a merge queue is the mitigation where that matters; force-push discipline becomes load-bearing ("never rewrite a branch others build on" is a red flag, not a guideline); the hygiene bar is real work — a team that only pretends publishes `fix test` onto `main`, which is worse than squashing honestly. Follow-ups: adopting repos turn on the platform's linear-history protection and set the merge button to match their recorded choice; the shipped PR template carries the check.
- Confirmation: review, the shipped PR template's checkbox, and the platform's require-linear-history branch protection. No file in the repo proves a merge-button setting, so `self-verify` cannot see this: R23 is a review-tier rule.
- Revisit when: GitHub ships semi-linear merge (rebase then `--no-ff`), or a context arrives where what ships must be the exact commit CI tested — then a merge queue, or merge commits read with `--first-parent`, wins on that repo's own record.
- Related: R23 and R7 in `standard/SPEC.md`; `standard/docs/conventions.md` (Branch and history); the stacked-PRs case study; the open question `../open-questions/rebase-merge.md` this decision is held open against; ADR-001. Narrowed by ADR-035.

## ADR-027 — The database schema lives in the repo, with a typed twin (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-01)

- Tags: data, schema, disaster-recovery, types.
- Context: the standard says where every kind of knowledge lives and said nothing about the database schema — in a product repo the most consequential structure and the one thing a backup restores *into*. Two ordinary failure modes: (1) the schema exists only in the running database — the repo holds a migration chain; the current shape is a fold nobody performs by reading; losing the database means replaying and hoping; reviewing a table means opening a client against an environment; (2) every access path restates the row shape — each query, DTO and handler carries its own idea of a row; nothing enforces agreement; they diverge one column at a time. The operating rule already assumed the fix: an agent never writes to a remote database, it prepares a reviewed `.sql` file for a human to apply; the shipped guard's denial message names `database/schema/` as where that file belongs; the standard never said the directory should exist.
- Options: migrations are the schema (rejected as sufficient, kept as necessary: replay is not a read; nobody reviews a table by reading twelve deltas; a chain that breaks halfway has nothing underneath). The ORM model is the schema, DDL generated from it (rejected: binds the most durable asset to a library's lifetime and to what it can express — partial indexes, check constraints, triggers, grants and extensions do not round-trip; generated DDL is a subset presented as the whole). A `pg_dump --schema-only` artifact committed by CI (rejected as the primary copy: machine output, unreviewable diff, records what happened rather than what was intended; a fine *check* against authored DDL). Executable DDL in the repo plus a typed definition the code goes through, kept 1:1 — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules — "A repo that owns a database carries that schema twice, on purpose, and the two copies are one declared pair.":
  - `database/schema/` holds executable DDL, complete enough to rebuild the database from a checkout alone — the disaster-recovery copy, what a reviewer reads, the artifact a schema change ships as.
  - A typed, documented definition in the stack's idiom (Zod in TypeScript, Pydantic in Python) is the shape every read and write path goes through — parsing, validation and inference come from it, not from inline restatements.
  - The pair is 1:1: every table, column, constraint and enum present in one is present in the other; each side names its counterpart; a change to either lands in the same PR as the change to the other.
  - Either side may be generated from the other where a stack has a trustworthy generator; generation changes who types the characters, not the rule. Migrations stay how a change reaches a database — the delta, never the readable current state.
- Consequences: positive — disaster recovery starts with a checkout; a reviewer reads the current shape in one file; the code has one idea of a row, the table's; the pairing is a declared edge — the shape the coupling guard already checks, so it can become mechanical. Negative — two artifacts to keep in step; until a per-stack generator or drift check exists, the 1:1 claim is held by review; a generator that silently drops constraints is worse than none. Follow-ups: per-stack mechanics (where the typed definition lives, how generated, whether types agree) belong in the stack repos.
- Confirmation: `scripts/schema-pair.mjs`, shipped as a manifest guard, so `self-verify` counts a broken pair as drift. It proves what is provable without knowing the twin's language: the declared edge resolves both ways, and every name the DDL defines — table, column, enum type, enum label — appears in the twin (compared case- and separator-insensitively, so a camelCase twin of a snake_case column matches). A repo with no `database/schema/` reports that and passes; the rule is conditional on owning a database. Deliberately outside it: whether the *types* agree (needs the twin parsed structurally — a stack-repo check); a field in the twin with no column behind it is not drift (the database is the source of what exists; a typed module legitimately carries input shapes and derived fields).
- Revisit when: a stack ships a generator plus a drift check making one side genuinely derivable from the other — then the rule fixes the direction; or a repo arrives whose database is not owned by it (a shared, externally governed schema), which the rule deliberately does not cover.
- Related: R24 and R19 in `standard/SPEC.md`; ADR-012; ADR-018 (the schema file carries no change-log section). Cited by ADR-051 (`schema-pair` skips itself when no schema exists).

## ADR-028 — Work cycles live in the repo, and bind only at scale (status: Accepted (2026-08-02); renamed by ADR-041 (cycle -> sprint), date: 2026-08-02)

- Tags: backlog, cycles, planning, tracker, scale.
- Context: the backlog holds intents ordered by risk x leverage, and that is all. For one person enough; for several people in parallel three questions have no home: (1) who is doing what, in what period? (the `owner` column names a *role* — product / architect / dev / agent — not a person; nothing says which items a team picked up now); (2) what is this period for? (teams commit to outcomes — "checkout stops losing carts"); (3) when will this land? (the most common PO question; the repo cannot answer even approximately). ADR-010 decided execution state and work history live in the tracker, rejecting "everything stays in the repo" because the repo accretes debris and git is the history. That holds for per-item state and not for one thing.
- Options: A — leave it to the tracker (cycles are sprints, a tracker feature) — rejected: puts "when will this land" behind a tool the standard does not mandate and many teams do not pay for; the tracker's history is not reachable from a checkout. B — cycles in the repo, per-item state included — rejected: the tracker re-implemented worse; the debris ADR-010 rejected; two systems kept in step by hand. C — cycles in the repo as intent + one aggregate outcome, per-item state left to the tracker — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C) / rules:
  - A cycle is a bounded period of work with an owner, a goal and an agreed end date. Several run in parallel, one per team. Lives at `docs/cycles/<team>/<slug>.md` (now `docs/sprints/` per ADR-041).
  - One item, one place: an intent is in the backlog pool or in exactly one cycle, never both and never two. Moving it into a cycle removes its row from the pool; closing a cycle without finishing it returns the row. Mechanically checkable (one id, one location) and therefore checked, not asked for.
  - The end date is agreed, not imposed: a cycle ends when its owner says so; the date is a planning input, not a commitment the standard enforces — no framework, no ceremony, no fixed length; different teams may run different rhythms.
  - `/cycle-close` writes one aggregate outcome block: items planned, items finished, items returned to the pool, commits in the window, days elapsed. This is the narrow amendment to ADR-010, with the argument ADR-010 did not have: "the grouping is not recoverable afterwards" — git can tell how many commits landed between two dates, not that these seven intents were what the team believed it would finish and that five did (the pool mutates, items get re-scoped, the version of the backlog that made the plan is only reachable by knowing which commit). "An aggregate that cannot be recomputed is not a restatement of history; it is a measurement" — dropping it means the repo can never answer "how fast do we actually go".
  - Per-item execution state, who-did-what and work history remain the tracker's. The repo gains one block per closed cycle, not a log.
  - Estimation is arithmetic over that record, and it says when it cannot: `/timeline-update` reads every cycle, derives throughput from closed ones, projects the open ones and the pool. With too few closed cycles it says so and gives no date — "this standard would rather return nothing than a number it cannot support."
  - Scale only: a solo repo never meets any of this; core keeps the backlog and nothing else.
- Consequences: the repo answers "when will this land" without a tracker, improving as evidence accumulates. One more artifact to keep true (a cycle nobody closes) — `/timeline-update` surfaces a cycle past its date and unclosed rather than silently projecting from it. ADR-010 clause 4 narrowed, not overturned (status `Accepted, revised by 028`). "Sprint" deliberately not the word (carries planning poker, retrospectives, velocity-as-commitment; "borrowing the word borrows the argument"); `cycle` is Linear's term, so it arrives pre-explained — held open in `../open-questions/work-periods.md` (overturned by ADR-041).
- Confirmation: a guard fails when one intent id appears in more than one place across the pool and every cycle file; `/timeline-update` refuses to project from fewer closed cycles than it can defend and says which cycles are past date and still open; `self-verify` counts the cycles directory only at `scale`.
- Revisit when: a second team runs a genuinely different rhythm and the single `docs/cycles/<team>/` shape needs per-team configuration; or the first real timeline projection compared against what happened is wrong enough to be worse than no projection.
- Related: ADR-010 (narrowed), ADR-011 (why scale only), ADR-006 (a cycle's items still name the persona they serve), `work-periods` open question. Renamed by ADR-041; cited by ADR-029, ADR-030, ADR-040, ADR-044, ADR-051.

## ADR-029 — Measurement forecasts the work; sizes only cover the cold start (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-02)

- Author: bodurkalukasz. (No Tags row.)
- Context: ADR-028 left open how a team answers *will this fit before the date*. Industry default is estimation — story points, planning poker, velocity as commitment — reasonable when designed (points a proxy for time under uncertainty, calibrated by a stable team's throughput). Two things broke that: (1) the throughput base is no longer stable — with an agent in the loop the same nominal "three-pointer" is twenty minutes when the first attempt lands and two days when it does not; variance is no longer a property of the item, so the number stops carrying information while still costing a meeting; (2) the number gets spent — points summed into velocity, velocity becomes a commitment, the commitment becomes what is managed; follows mechanically from numbers being summable. The repo already measures what estimation approximated: a cycle file carries `Opened` and `Target` dates and `cycle-close` records what finished, so the real distribution of item durations is recoverable from history. Remaining problem: the cold start — no closed cycles, no measured distribution; `timeline-update` refuses to project below three closed cycles, leaving a new team with nothing exactly when deciding what to pull.
- Options: A — story points (summable, familiar; calibration base does not hold; summability turns the estimate into a commitment) — rejected. B — no estimates; forecast only from history (honest, correct once history exists; answers nothing during the cold start — "a real cost, not a principled abstention") — rejected on its own. C — T-shirt sizes as a cold-start estimate, superseded by measurement — **chosen**. D — sizes permanently alongside measurement — rejected: two forecasting inputs at once is how a size gets quietly mapped to a number (`S`=1, `M`=3, `L`=5) and becomes the currency; "There must be no moment when both count."
- Decision / rules — "Measurement is the forecast. `size` is an optional cold-start estimate and an informational field, and it is never an input to a projection once measured cycles exist.":
  - `size` is `S`, `M` or `L`, optional, on backlog and cycle rows.
  - Standing use is a splitting trigger: an `L` means split this before pulling it.
  - Below three closed cycles, `timeline-update` may reason from sizes and must say that it is doing so, and that the result is an estimate rather than a measurement.
  - At three closed cycles and above, the projection comes from measured item durations and sizes are ignored entirely. No blended mode.
  - Sizes are never summed, never converted to numbers, never charted.
  - An item that does not finish inside its cycle is split, not re-sized — keeps item counts comparable without a currency; replaces re-estimation.
- Consequences: a usable answer from day one, a trustworthy one from cycle four, with an explicit dated handover; the forecast improves on its own. Cost: the first three cycles are projected from guesses and will sometimes be wrong — the skill says so in output. Second cost: burndown charts, velocity trends, time tracking are not here and this standard is not where that argument reopens — they live in a tracker (ADR-010). Item counts assume rough comparability, true only because of split-do-not-resize; if not followed, throughput numbers degrade quietly.
- Confirmation: partly mechanical. `cycle-guard` checks the `blocked:<id>` references introduced with this change — a block names an intent which exists, is not the row itself, and is not already done — on every run. The exclusions are not enforced by a script: nothing stops a team adding a points column or summing sizes; enforced by review and this record. "A guard that tried to detect 'this markdown column is being used as a currency' would be guessing."
- Revisit when: a team runs four or more cycles and reports that the measured projection is *less* useful than sizes were — meaning measurement captures the wrong thing; absent that, accumulating history strengthens the decision.
- Related: ADR-028, ADR-010, the skills `timeline-update`, `cycle-open`, `cycle-close`. Cited by ADR-030, ADR-041.

## ADR-030 — The current holder of an in-flight intent is cycle state, not history (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-02)

- Author: bodurkalukasz.
- Context: ADR-010 put work history in the tracker; `cycle-open` states the consequence: *do not assign people; the row's owner is a role*. Two things made that unworkable: (1) the adoption intake offers in-repo tracking — a team answering "we track work here" has no tracker, so "it lives in the tracker" names a place that does not exist for them; (2) a cycle without a holder is not a commitment — `owner` is a role (`dev`, `product`, `architect`); a cycle whose rows all say `dev` records that developers will do some development, "which is not information"; *who has this one* has no answer in the repo, and answering it in a meeting is the state cycles were meant to replace.
- Options: A — hold the line; assignment only in a tracker — rejected: fails outright for in-repo teams; leaves the most-asked question unanswerable. B — full per-item execution state in the repo (assignment, transitions, timestamps, time spent) — rejected: rebuilds a tracker badly. C — the current holder only, on cycle rows, archived with the cycle — **chosen**: one field, present tense, bounded lifetime.
- Decision / rules — "`assignee` names the person currently holding an in-flight intent, on cycle rows only." Boundary against ADR-010 is *tense*:
  - The pool has no assignee — an item nobody picked up is not yet anyone's; "a pool that assigns work has become a queue of orders."
  - A cycle row carries exactly one current holder — not a list of who touched it, not when it changed hands, not how long each held it. Reassignment overwrites; the previous holder is not recorded (history is ADR-010's).
  - When the cycle closes, the file is archived as written — a record of what that cycle looked like, not a queryable log; nothing reads assignees across cycles, no skill aggregates them.
  - ADR-010 narrowed, not superseded: work history still lives in the tracker; one field of present state moves into the repo, no accumulation.
- Consequences: an open cycle answers "who has this" without a meeting or second tool; in-repo teams get a coherent story. Cost (deliberate): who *used to* hold an item is unrecoverable from the repo — a team that needs that needs a tracker. Second cost: a stale assignee after someone changes teams looks authoritative and is wrong; nothing detects this. Per-person throughput is not derivable and not meant to be — a field that could be aggregated into individual velocity would be (same reasoning as ADR-029).
- Confirmation: review, not a script. `cycle-guard` checks that an intent lives in exactly one place and blocks point at something real; it cannot check an assignee is a real person, still on the team, or current (would need a roster the standard does not have). Mechanical: `cycle-close` reports rows whose status did not change during the cycle — surfacing an abandoned item (usual symptom of a stale assignee) without claiming to detect the cause.
- Revisit when: a team reports they wanted the previous holders after all — meaning archived-as-written is too thin and the boundary is in the wrong place; a general preference for more tracking is not.
- Related: ADR-010 (narrowed, as ADR-028 narrowed it), ADR-029 (sibling decision on what the repo will and will not measure).

## ADR-031 — One domain, and the URL says which surface you are on (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-03)

- Author: bodurkalukasz.
- Context: the ecosystem is not one repository: the core carries the method; a stack repository carries the tooling for a technology; ADR-025 says a repo adopts the latest of each independently. Each has a landing page and a documentation site, generated by the same generator from its own markdown — four surfaces, and nothing decided where they live. The generator carried a `base_path` and no site set it; the header switcher hardcoded "here" on the core entry and sent the stack entry off to GitHub — right on the core's pages, wrong on every stack page. Pressure: a reader landing on a stack page wants the method it implements; a search engine must be told these are one product.
- Options: A — a domain per repository (`repositorystandards.com`, `node.repositorystandards.com`) — rejected: splits the authority a single domain accumulates, doubles certificate and DNS work per stack, makes every cross-link off-site. B — a path per repository, docs nested under it (`/node/`, `/node/docs/`) — rejected: surface first and audience second, so `/docs/` stops being one place; a reader must know which stack before finding any docs. C — surface first, stack second — **chosen**.
- Decision — one domain `repositorystandards.com`, surface first in the path: `/` = the core's landing page; `/docs/` = the core's documentation; `/node/` = the Node stack's landing page; `/docs/node/` = the Node stack's documentation. "Documentation is one tree with a stack living inside it ... *the docs, for Node*."
  - Each repository builds its own site and knows only two things about where it is served: `site_root` (where its landing sits) and `base_path` (where its docs sit). Nothing else in the generator is layout-aware; a second stack is two config lines.
  - Every internal link is written root-absolute against `base_path`. "This is not a style preference." A relative link resolves against whatever the browser believes the base to be; a visit to `/docs` without the trailing slash (what people type, what a chat link carries) re-based every link one level up and broke the sidebar; under this layout `/docs/node` without the slash would resolve a stack's links into the core's tree — "worse than a 404 because some of them would work."
  - The ecosystem switcher decides "here" by comparing against the site's own base, not by being told; any hardcoded answer is correct on exactly one site.
- Consequences: cross-surface links are same-origin (a stack page points at a core rule and stays on-site; the switcher becomes navigation). One certificate, one analytics property, one robots and sitemap story, search authority accrues to one domain. Adding a stack costs a path and two config lines, no DNS. Cost: surfaces are joined at deploy rather than at build — nothing in either repository can verify the whole tree; a link from one surface into another is checked only against the layout this record describes, so this file, not a script, keeps them consistent. The redirect from `/docs` to `/docs/` is load-bearing and belongs to the host; its absence is now a correctness bug.
- Confirmation: `site-check` fails any generated page carrying a relative internal link (root-absolute rule is mechanical inside a repository); the switcher's "here" is derived. Publishing belongs to this repository and needs no second one: a project site with a custom domain is served at the domain root, not under its repository name (the `/<repo>/` prefix applies only to the default `<org>.github.io` address), so one repository can publish every path. The workflow builds this repository's surfaces, then walks `stacks.json` and builds each registered stack into its own prefix — one list, so a stack cannot be registered and unpublished by disagreement between files. A stack whose repository is not reachable yet is warned about, not skipped silently ("a missing surface nobody mentions reads as a surface that was published"). Nothing is committed: the site is uploaded as an artifact; no repository carries build output; no repository needs write access to another. Not mechanical: nothing checks that `/docs/node/` actually serves the stack's build (no repository can see the deployed tree) — verified by opening it after a deploy.
- Revisit when: a stack needs its own brand or domain for a non-technical reason (a partnership, a separate product identity) — a positioning change; "Wanting a shorter URL does not."
- Related: ADR-025 (separate builds joined at deploy), ADR-014 (one authored tree; the path says which zone you are in).

## ADR-032 — Re-entering a spec mid-development is core; tracker sync is an extension (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Author: bodurkalukasz.
- Context: the loop is not one-way — a spec is written, planned, broken into tasks, built, and then something changes and you go back to the spec while work is in flight; validation found this unsupported in two ways (cases `SPEC-22`, `TRACK-20`, `TRACK-21`, all `fail`):
  1. `spec-update` correctly establishes the delta ("the spec on this branch describes the target; `git diff` against main is the change delta"). Nothing downstream reads it: `spec-plan` reads the whole current spec and regenerates artifacts wholesale; an incremental plan happens only when the agent notices the capability is already live.
  2. Task ids are positional (`T001`, `T002`, ...) and regenerated from scratch each run; R13 sharpens this — task scaffolding is deleted when work closes, so round two holds no record of what round one's ids meant.
  3. The Jira bridge keys a sub-task by that id ("created only if no sub-task with that id exists"), so on round two a new `T003` describing different work matches the old `[T003]` and is skipped. Nothing errors; the board reads complete; "Work is silently lost rather than duplicated, which is the worse failure."
  - The obvious fix (globally stable task ids + a reconciling exporter) drags a tracker's concerns into the middle of the standard; most adopting repos never connect a tracker, and R15 says execution state lives in the tracker while the repo holds intents.
- Options: stable content-derived task ids in core (hash the task's scope) — rejected as a core change: every repo carries an identity scheme whose only consumer is an exporter most don't run; a hash in a task line is noise to a human. A generation marker in the key (`[R2-T003]`) — stops the collision but every round mints a fresh set of sub-tasks; "trades silent loss for guaranteed duplication" — rejected on its own. Split the problem by who needs it — **chosen**: re-entry detection in core; identity and reconciliation in an optional extension.
- Decision / rules:
  - Core gains re-entry detection, and only that: when planning or task-breaking runs against a capability that already has plan or task scaffolding present, the skills MUST compare against what is there and report the difference — what the spec change adds, what it invalidates, what is untouched — rather than silently overwriting. No new file format, no identity scheme, no external system (scaffolding is already on disk during development, exactly the window in which re-entry happens).
  - Tracker synchronisation is an extension: an optional sidecar `specs/<capability>/tracker.json` a repo has only if it syncs to a tracker, carrying the external keys already described in `tracking-work.md` plus a per-item fingerprint so an adapter can distinguish *the same work whose description changed* from *new work*. Core does not read it; no shipped guard requires it; `self-verify` does not count its absence as drift; the manifest entry is `required: false`.
  - The no-overwrite rule stands: the bridge never edits an issue it did not create; `TRACK-21` is closed by reporting divergence — the adapter says "this Story's source has changed since it was exported" and a human decides.
- Consequences: the round trip becomes a mechanism for every adopter; the check fires only when scaffolding exists. The silent-loss defect is fixed where it lives (the adapter's key). Cost: two places describe work-in-flight (scaffolding on disk and the sidecar) and can disagree — the sidecar is derived and disposable; the repo wins; the adapter reports rather than reconciles. Cost: the extension is per-capability, so a repo syncing many capabilities carries many small files (state next to the capability, at the price of no single index). An adapter is now a documented extension point, inviting others (Linear, GitHub Projects) without core changing.
- Confirmation: `SPEC-22`, `TRACK-20`, `TRACK-21` re-run and move from `fail`; `self-verify` on a repo without the sidecar must report the same drift number as before it existed.
- What this rules out: putting tracker identity, external keys or sync state into the spec, the task list or the backlog row itself.
- Revisit when: an adopter with no tracker still has to think about the sidecar (the "ignorable" claim is false), or a second adapter cannot be built against the extension without changing core (the seam is in the wrong place).
- Related: ADR-010; `docs/method/tracking-work.md`; R13 (scaffolding is ephemeral), R15 (backlog and tracker). Cited by ADR-033, ADR-040 (tracker bridge required at no profile).

## ADR-033 — The spec loop reads the decision log before it writes, and an Accepted record outranks the request (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Author: bodurkalukasz. Tags: method, specs, decisions, lifecycle.
- Context — two `severe` validation findings, both `fail`:
  - `SPEC-18`: neither `/spec-specify` nor `/spec-clarify` reads `docs/decision-records/` (grepping both returns only the `PATCHED(...)` provenance comments). Both are told to read `docs/discovery/` before asking — which ADR-024 makes explicitly non-normative. So the loop's entry point consulted the one source that binds nothing and never the one that binds everything; a spec contradicting an Accepted record passes the clarify gate and every guard. The only backstop was `AGENTS.md`'s red-flag list "Contradicting an Accepted ADR" — not BDR, the stream a feature request actually collides with.
  - `SPEC-17`: retirement awareness reached only one of two entry points. `/spec-impact` checks the target's `Status` and stops on `retired`; `/spec-update` refuses to edit a retired spec; `discovery-digest` refuses to route new material into one. `/spec-specify` — the PO's entry point and the one `AGENTS.md` names for new behaviour — contained the word `retired` zero times, while instructing that an existing capability directory means "update the existing spec in place". Reproduced on the fixture: `shift-notifications` is `Status: retired` per BDR-004, whose text says a future in-house reminder need is a new capability, specced fresh; a PO asking for exactly that walks into an edit of the retired spec.
  - One defect from two sides: retirement is only the case where the governing record left a flag on the spec file; the general case (a record that governs the request and left no flag) had no detection.
- Options: leave it to review and the red-flag list — rejected (a red flag fires when an agent already noticed; review is after the spec is written). A mechanical guard — rejected as primary: contradiction is semantic; a guard can check a *cited* record's `Status` is current (`/spec-reconcile` does) but cannot see a spec that silently re-decides something it never cites; "A guard that appears to cover this and does not is worse than none." Read every record in full at the start of every run — rejected: cost scales with record count, mostly waste; "an agent told to read sixty records reads sixty records badly"; bounded read instead (index table, then full only where the subject overlaps — the README's gist column is written to be read instead of the records). Put the check only in `/spec-impact` and route everything through it — rejected: makes the PO path depend on a developer skill firing first. Delegate to `specs/constitution.md` — rejected on two observed counts: the template scopes its own check to `/spec-plan` and `/spec-update`, and specify loads it at step 4 — after step 2 has decided which directory to write into, where the retirement check must fire; in the pre-fix reproduction the constitution contributed to a stop only because the agent went looking for the record on its own. (The template's hard stop said "ADR" while its altitude list said "ADR / BDR" — corrected too.)
- Decision / rules:
  - The decision log is read before the draft, at both entry points: `/spec-specify` before drafting; `/spec-clarify` before asking. The read is bounded by the index — the README table, then in full only records whose subject overlaps. That bound rests on `decision-records-check`, which fails a record on disk the index does not list; where that guard is not running, the directory is listed too. (Dry run: the agent grepped the fixture's CI, found the guard wired nowhere, listed the directory — and BDR-004, the governing record, was on disk and absent from a BDR index that stopped at 003.)
  - A capability is matched by subject, not by slug: the short name specify generates is its own; `shift-reminders` and `shift-notifications` are one capability under two names. `ls specs/` and match on what the capability is about — a near-miss slug is how one capability acquires two rival specs and how a retirement is routed around without anyone deciding to.
  - Precedence is stated: an Accepted record in scope outranks the dossier, outranks the draft, and outranks whatever the user says in the moment. What a record settles enters the spec as settled content citing it — never as a `[NEEDS DECISION]` marker and never as a question. `Proposed` / `Rejected` / `Superseded` bind nothing; the supersession link leads to the record that does.
  - A contradiction stops the run. Exactly two legitimate routes: change the request to fit, or supersede the record (`/adr-write`, `/bdr-write`). Writing the spec anyway is not one, including when the user asks for it: "an answer that collides with an Accepted record is a supersession, not a clarification."
  - Retirement is checked at every entry point, including the PO's: `/spec-specify` reads the matched spec's `Status` at the step that decides where to write; `retired` stops it before drafting. The stop is *surface and hand back*, not *refuse*: a new capability specced fresh is often right and the retiring record frequently says so, but the call belongs to the user with the record in front of them; the agent may not proceed alone, under the old slug or a new one.
  - R6 says the binding half out loud: the validation suite cited R6 for "an accepted record binds future work in its scope until superseded" while R6's text covered only how a record is changed; the rule now carries both halves.
- Consequences: the collision is found before a spec exists. Cost: every specify and clarify run pays an index read (a repo with no records pays nothing; many pays one table). Cost: this is prose an agent can skip, not a guard — placement is the mitigation: the consequential checks sit inside the step that already does the thing they qualify (the queue-building step in clarify, the directory-resolution branch in specify); the intake block is unavoidably a preamble (dry run flagged "a less careful agent skims the numbered Outline and treats everything above it as preamble"), so the Outline opens by saying the intakes are steps and can end the run before step 1; if real runs still skip it, the answer is a citation duty something can check, not more prose. Cost: the retirement rule is now written at two entry points — deliberate, because written once on the developer path is precisely how `SPEC-17` happened. `/spec-clarify` can now end a session by refusing an answer — new, and the point.
- Confirmation: `SPEC-17` and `SPEC-18` re-run and move from `fail`; mechanically: `grep -c -i retired` over `standard/.claude/skills/spec-specify/SKILL.md` is no longer 0, and both spec-specify and spec-clarify name `docs/decision-records/`. The semantic part (that an agent actually stops) was tested by paired dry runs of one PO request against the `test-greenfield-core` fixture, pre-fix and post-fix, each agent told to follow the documented procedure literally and report which reads were instructed and which were its own initiative. Pre-fix the agent did stop, saying opening the governing record was its own move ("spec-specify's text never mentions decision records at all"); post-fix, reading the decision-record index was its first action and it cited the step. Both late additions — the guard-dependency of the index read and subject-over-slug matching — came from the post-fix run finding the fix's own holes.
- What this rules out: a guard that claims to detect a spec contradicting a record it never cites — semantic contradiction is not greppable; shipping something that looks like that check buys false confidence "at the exact point this standard is selling trust."
- Revisit when: a spec lands contradicting an Accepted record with both skills' procedure followed (the bounded index read is too coarse), or the stop fires so often on records that did not really govern that authors learn to wave it through.
- Related: ADR-024, ADR-032 (same shape: a documented loop assumption nothing performed); R5 (decisions are recorded), R6 (an accepted record binds until superseded), R8 (behaviour is specified by capability). Cited by ADR-050.

## ADR-034 — Embargoed work is a private mirror of the repo, not an exemption from R3 (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Author: bodurkalukasz. Tags: security, knowledge.
- Context: the standard had no word for work that is real, recorded, and not yet publishable. R3: project knowledge MUST live in the repo; R11: a capability's code and spec land in the same PR; R15: the backlog holds the intents. Followed literally in a public repo, those publish an unfixed vulnerability the moment somebody starts fixing it — and a customer's confidential detail and an unannounced commercial move. Validation case `SEC-02` executed it: no rule carried an embargo or confidentiality clause; the manifest's `exceptions` has four kinds (`file`, `section`, `content`, `key`) and no embargo. The real failure mode: teams quietly break it — the fix is developed in a private fork with no spec, no record, no backlog item — because the standard only offered "publish it" or "you are non-compliant"; "A rule that forces disclosure teaches people to work outside the standard exactly when the work is most sensitive."
- Options: an embargo exception on R3, R11 and R15 ("unless confidential" clauses) — rejected: three separate holes each readable as a general licence to keep knowledge out, none says anything about the material coming back; concedes the premise that the standard requires too much when the real problem is conflating *the repo* with *the public*. A new `embargo` kind in `exceptions` — rejected on what `exceptions` is: a standing structural deviation read by `self-verify` at drift time that deliberately lowers the adoption percentage; an embargo is a temporary state of one piece of work in an otherwise aligned repo — encoding it there penalises correct behaviour and leaves a permanent artifact of a temporary condition. A private mirror of the repo, for a bounded period, rejoining the mainline — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules:
  - Embargoed work is "deferred publication of repo content, not knowledge kept outside a repo." Material whose *publication* is the harm may be held in a private mirror of the repo — the hosting platform's private security-advisory fork is the paved road — and MUST rejoin the mainline when the embargo lifts.
  - R3 carries the normative wording and is the only place it is stated. Three properties make it an embargo rather than a hiding place: every rule binds inside the mirror (nothing deferred except the moment the world can read it; nothing reconstructed from memory at disclosure); the embargo is bounded at the start by a lifting condition and an owner (a permanent private home is the R3 violation it always was); the near misses are named out loud ("a clause like this is read by someone looking for permission").
  - R19's recorded security baseline gains the matching axis in `docs/method/security-baseline.md`, so each repo answers where its embargoed work lives and who lifts it, rather than deciding under pressure during an incident.
- Consequences: positive — only one rule moved; once "the repo" may temporarily be a private mirror, R11 and R15 need no exception (three holes became one clause); the disclosure moment stops being a writing exercise (spec, record, backlog item exist; publishing is a merge). Negative — a private mirror is real overhead (a second remote; a merge back that can conflict), paid only by public repos while an embargo is live; nothing mechanical can check an embargo lifted — the only guard against a permanent private home is review reading R3. Follow-ups: none.
- Confirmation: `SEC-02` re-runs the same procedure against R3 and the `exceptions` schema; beyond that review's call, deliberately — "a guard that pretended to check it would be theatre"; what is now checkable is that the vocabulary exists.
- Revisit when: an adopting repo reports the private-mirror path with the merge back impractical — a conflicted rejoin, or a platform whose private fork cannot host the tooling the standard requires.
- Related: R3, R11, R15, R19 and the security baseline's embargo axis; validation case `SEC-02`.

## ADR-035 — A maintained release line is an integration target, not a stacked branch (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Tags: git, workflow, releases, history, backports.
- Context: R23 said "Every PR MUST be based on the mainline, never on another open PR's branch". Both halves came from the stacked-PRs failure (ADR-026). The first half is wider than the failure it was written for: it makes *any* base other than the mainline illegal, including an ordinary security backport. The single most-repeated finding of the 2026-08-04 validation round, confirmed on five repositories (recorded in `docs/validation/targets.json` or that round's observations): odoo/odoo — 98 branches, four supported release lines plus master; openssl/openssl — five maintained `REL` branches with a per-PR backport matrix, 614 CVE references in `CHANGES.md`; dotnet/runtime — servicing branches with a written backport policy; FFmpeg/FFmpeg — a `Changelog` section per version (the `## Unreleased` idea applied per line); PowerShell/PowerShell, opentofu and erlang — their own per-release changelog composition. Under R23 as written a maintainer cannot ship a CVE fix to a supported line (the PR is based on `3.0`). R18 had the matching gap: one `## Unreleased` heading, one slot for a fix shipping in five releases. Recorded as `SHAPE-06`, severity severe. The distinction the old wording missed: another open PR's branch is unsafe because it *will be rewritten* when it lands; a maintained release line is long-lived, protected, never rewritten, and outlives the PRs based on it.
- Options: leave R23 alone; multi-line repos out of scope — rejected on evidence (five confirmations; a backport is the most ordinary act a release line performs). Fix it in the method docs only — rejected on the precedence rule: `SPEC.md` states that where another document appears to add a requirement the spec page wins; guidance cannot legalize what a MUST forbids. Add a new numbered rule for release lines (R26) — rejected: the defect is that two existing rules assume a single line; a new rule would restate R23 while R23 kept forbidding it; "The fix belongs where the assumption lives." Permit any long-lived branch as a base — rejected as too loose (re-opens the stacked-PR failure); what makes a release line legal is that the repo *declared* it supported, protects it, never rewrites it. Make the support matrix machine-checkable (release lines in `standard.manifest.json`, `self-verify` counting undeclared ones as drift) — rejected for now: nothing in a checkout proves which branches are supported (a branch existing is not a branch supported); R23 is review-tier by ADR-026's own confirmation.
- Decision / rules — "A repo MAY maintain more than one release line, and each maintained line is a first-class integration target - a legal base for a PR, exactly as the mainline is.":
  1. What qualifies: a long-lived, protected branch never rewritten that the repo has declared as supported, with its branching decision (R7). An undeclared branch is not a release line; another open PR's branch never is.
  2. The rules bind it identically: everything R23 requires of the mainline binds each line — one finished unit of work per PR, updated by rebasing onto it, never back-merged, never rewritten once anyone builds on it, landed by the integration method the repo recorded.
  3. Ordering and the changelog: a fix applying to more than one line lands on the mainline first and reaches each supported line as its own PR against that line — unless the mainline no longer carries the affected code, which the PR says. Each line carries its own changelog with its own `## Unreleased` heading; a PR writes its entry under the heading on the branch it targets.
  - The ordering is the decision: fixing a release line first and the mainline later or never is how a bug ships back on the next major; requiring mainline-first costs the backporter a rebase and buys the guarantee no supported line is ahead of the mainline in known fixes.
  - R25 needs no change: written per release; a line's version has its own single home on its own branch (what R4 asks).
- Consequences: positive — the standard describes repos it accidentally made non-compliant, in its own vocabulary; the backport path is written where a contributor looks (conventions block, decision checklist, changelog process); `SHAPE-06` closes. Negative — R23 is longer and carries a conditional most adopters never exercise; multi-line repos inherit a real obligation: N supported lines mean N PRs and N changelog entries for one fix; nothing mechanical enforces mainline-first (review-tier). Follow-ups: repos supporting more than one line record the set and support window with their branching decision, and turn on branch protection for each line.
- Confirmation: review, plus the decision checklist's branching fork — a repo with more than one supported line either names the lines there or has not decided; `self-verify` cannot see this (judgment tier, per `../method/self-verify.md`).
- Revisit when: a platform makes the support window readable from the repo itself (a declared support matrix, or branch protection legible to a checkout) — then the fifth option becomes buildable; or a real repo needs a line that is maintained but deliberately not based on the mainline's history (a vendored fork, a long-term-support rewrite), not covered here.
- Related: R23, R18, R7; `standard/docs/conventions.md`; ADR-026 (extended, not superseded); ADR-018 and the changelog process; `SHAPE-06` in `../validation/README.md`. Cited by ADR-042 (triage found it already-closed), ADR-044.

## ADR-036 — A retired spec is frozen against extension, not against correction (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Author: bodurkalukasz.
- Context: a capability is retired by a decision record: the spec stays as the record of what was built and why, `Status` flips to `retired`, the capability-map entry stays though its globs match nothing (`capability-spec.template.md` says all three). Nothing said what happens when a later, unrelated change makes a sentence in the retired spec false (a shared enum renamed, a table dropped). Three shipped rules met the case (`DEC-10`, first recorded 2026-08-04) and each pointed a different way: (1) `spec-update` step 1 — if an affected spec has `Status: retired`, stop before editing it; (2) `spec-reconcile` step 5 — cross-spec contradictions are a finding and must be resolved in this change or filed (rule 8, no silent drift); (3) `spec-impact` step 1 checks `retired` on the primary capability only, so a retired spec reached as a ripple target is never flagged. The loop could not detect the case, the resolver was told to resolve it, the editor was told not to touch it — no legal path; R4 broken by whichever rule the agent followed. Underlying confusion: `retired` was written to stop a retired capability being quietly extended back into life; read as freezing the file's text — a stronger claim no rule in `SPEC.md` makes.
- Options: freeze the file (false sentence stays; contradiction recorded elsewhere) — rejected: leaves wrong text as current truth (R4 forbids); a retired spec with an unknown subset of false sentences teaches readers to trust none. Reopen the capability (re-spec a falsified retired spec) — rejected: retirement is a recorded decision (R5); undoing it by side effect is how a retired capability comes back without anyone deciding. Delete the stale section — rejected: the contract section is most of what makes the retired spec worth keeping; deleting makes the file less true while looking tidier and destroys evidence a future reader needs for a migration still visible in the data. Frozen against extension, open to correction — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules — "`retired` freezes behaviour, not truth.":
  - A retired spec MUST NOT gain, extend or re-scope behaviour (what `spec-update`'s stop rule protects, unchanged); a new need in a retired area is a new capability, specced fresh.
  - A retired spec MUST be corrected when a later change makes one of its statements false, in the same PR as the change that falsified it (same coupling R11 requires). The correction is bounded to making the sentence true *as history*: state what the retired capability did, name the change that superseded it, stop. Never describes behaviour it should now have; `Status` stays `retired`.
  - If the correction cannot be written without deciding something (which reading was right, whether old behaviour should be restored), it is not a correction: stop and write the record first (R5).
  - The three skills say one thing: `spec-impact` checks `Status` on every capability it reaches, primary and ripple, reporting a retired ripple target as a correction target; `spec-update` keeps the stop rule for extension and names the correction path for the falsified-statement case; `spec-reconcile`'s cross-spec step resolves a contradiction involving a retired spec in one direction only — the live spec wins, the retired one is corrected to say what it did.
- Consequences: one answer instead of three (the answer R4 implied); a retired spec stays trustworthy sentence by sentence. Cost: a change has a wider blast radius — retired specs are in scope for the ripple search (real work on changes touching shared contracts; previously skipped silently). Cost: "correction" and "extension" are separated by judgment; a determined author can call an extension a correction — the bound (history only, no new behaviour, status unchanged) is what a reviewer holds them to.
- Confirmation: review, and the three skills' own text now name the same path. Deliberately no guard: no check can tell a correction from an extension by reading a diff (one would gate on diff size or section, trivially satisfied by splitting); "Saying 'review only' out loud is more honest than a guard that would report green on the case it was written for."
- What this rules out: a retired spec treated as immutable text; a retired capability extended under the name of a correction.
- Revisit when: corrections to retired specs start carrying new `MUST`s — the retired file growing rather than pinned to history.
- Related: R4, R5, R11; `standard/specs/capability-spec.template.md`; `standard/.claude/skills/spec-impact`, `spec-update`, `spec-reconcile`.

## ADR-037 — A repo may register more than one technology stack (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Author: bodurkalukasz.
- Context: R20 names one file for Layer 2 registration — a stack declares what adopting it means in `stack.manifest.json`, "a repo that adopted one carries it", `self-verify` counts one drift across both layers. `self-verify.mjs` implemented it literally — one filename, read once. Validation case `GATE-12` on `flutter/flutter` (a Dart framework beside a native engine, permanently): it could register exactly one; the second stack's entries were not checked, contributed nothing to drift, and nothing said a stack had been ignored — `drift 0 - 100% adopted` with half its technology layer unverified. Reproduced: a fixture carrying `stack.manifest.json` and `stack.dart-flutter.manifest.json` reported only the first. The prior round logged it as a contract change rather than fixing unilaterally (R20 names the filename; an engine inventing a second convention is how a standard stops meaning one thing).
- Options: a `stacks` array inside one `stack.manifest.json` — rejected: the file is authored and shipped by the stack repo, one per technology (ADR-016); a combined file has no owner; the adopting repo would hand-merge two upstream files and own the conflict forever. A `stacks/` directory of manifests — rejected: more structure than needed; orphans the single-stack name every adopter carries. Tell a two-stack repo to pick one — rejected: not a real answer; failure mode silent. A namespaced sibling filename — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules — a repo carries one stack manifest per stack it has adopted, and `self-verify` reads all of them:
  - `stack.manifest.json` is unchanged and remains the name for a repo with one stack; nothing an existing adopter carries has to move.
  - A repo whose stacks coexist adds `stack.<technology>.manifest.json` per stack. The engine matches `/^stack(?:\.[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]*)?\.manifest\.json$/` at the repo root and reads every match in filename order, so two runs report in the same order.
  - Each file produces its own note naming the file and its `technology`; the drift number stays one number across every layer (R20's actual promise).
  - An unparseable stack manifest is drift naming the file.
  - Two stacks declaring the same path is reported, not resolved: the path is checked once per declaration (counts twice) and the run names both files; collapsing would pick a winner between two upstream repos this one does not own.
- Consequences: a two-stack repo can be verified at all; the single-stack path is byte-for-byte what it was. Cost: a duplicated path inflates the denominator slightly (the alternative hides a real disagreement). Cost: the filename carries meaning (the technology) and nothing checks it matches the manifest's own `technology` field — a mismatch is cosmetic (both printed); a check would fail repos whose stack repo named the file differently. The registry (`stacks.json`) is untouched.
- Confirmation: `GATE-12`; four cases in `tools/self-verify-drift-test.mjs`: two stacks both count and are named; a shared path is checked once per declaration and the doubling stated; a file merely resembling the name (`mystack.manifest.json`) is not read as a stack; an unparseable second manifest is drift rather than a skip.
- What this rules out: a repo declaring the same stack twice under two names; any automatic reconciliation of two stacks' claims — "The engine reports; the stacks' owners decide."
- Revisit when: the filename-vs-`technology`-field mismatch starts causing real confusion; or the duplicate-path warning proves too noisy, or the standard wants to reconcile two stacks' claims automatically.
- Related: ADR-016, ADR-022, R20 (amended by one clause). Cited by ADR-042.

## ADR-038 — The adopted percentage measures structure; substance stays the judgment tier (status: Accepted (2026-08-06), date: 2026-08-06)

- Tags: verification, metrics, honesty.
- Context: `self-verify` reports a drift number and an adopted percentage. Three manifest entry classes scored differently: a `copy` entry carries a `sha256` and is compared against it (added in `e42d39a`); a `merge` entry cannot be hashed (adapted on purpose) so it may declare `requiredKeys` naming what must survive the merge; a `fill-from-repo` entry has neither and cannot — the adopter writes the content. Measured: on a sparse repository, six files reading `# Title` and `TODO.` moved the reading from `drift 19 - 21% adopted (5/24)` to `drift 19 - 37% adopted (11/30)` — sixteen percentage points for six files saying nothing; `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `SECURITY.md`, `docs/PRODUCT.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` each scored `PASS` on three lines. A placeholder warning existed ("a warning, never drift: substance stays the judgment tier's call") with two holes: it only recognised surviving template placeholders (a self-typed stub was invisible), and it walked a hardcoded list of eight files that `CONTRIBUTING.md` was never on.
- Options: A — required sections per `fill-from-repo` entry (`sections` mechanism) — rejected: converts substance into ceremony (an adopter adds the heading and writes `TODO` under it; the number goes green on strictly less information); imposes a document shape on files the adopter writes their way; would move these entries into drift, changing what the number means for every adopted repo. B — a minimum-substance signal (word count, prose threshold) — rejected: "measuring prose by the yard"; a genuine two-sentence `SECURITY.md` naming an address and a response time is complete; a threshold teaches padding; "a number nobody can justify is a number nobody should gate on." C — say what the number is, detect only what is unambiguous, leave the rest to review — **chosen**.
- Decision (Option C):
  1. The adopted percentage is a structural measure and says so where printed: counts entries present, and content only where the content is the standard's own. When any authored file reads as unfilled, the verdict line states that the percentage counts entries present, not substance written. No numbered rule changes.
  2. The warning detects "visibly nothing written", never "not enough written": two shapes only, both cleared by one real sentence — a body with no content beyond its headings, and a body whose entire content is a marker meaning nobody has written this yet (`TODO`, `TBD`, `WIP`, `coming soon` and their spellings). Still a warning, never drift.
  3. The file list is derived from the manifest, not written in the script.
  - "Whether what IS written is any good remains the judgment tier" — reviewed at PR as `self-verify.md` describes; "a check that pretends to is worse than none, because it converts a reviewer's question into a green tick."
- Consequences: positive — the number can no longer be inflated silently; the manifest is the single source of truth for which files are authored. Follow-up (1.0.18): the clarifier is printed on every verdict, not only when a file is flagged — an external adopter read a clean `93% adopted (74/80)` as a grade and titled their PR "assessment to 93%". Positive — no existing adopter's drift number changes. Negative — a determined adopter clears the warning with one real but empty sentence (accepted; the alternative teaches padding). Negative — the percentage still rises when files are added, including the standard's own copied files — recorded in the validation suite, not resolved here.
- Confirmation: `tools/self-verify-fill-test.mjs` holds the boundary both ways: a self-written `TODO` stub warns, a heading-only file warns, each spelling of the nothing-yet marker warns, and a terse-but-real `SECURITY.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md` do not; neutralising the check turns exactly the three positive cases red and leaves the two negatives green. `docs/method/self-verify.md` states the split under "Which rules the number covers" and in the judgment-tier section.
- Revisit when: a `fill-from-repo` entry acquires a genuinely machine-checkable property that is not a proxy for prose quality (e.g. a `SECURITY.md` that must contain a reachable contact, where reachability is testable) — then it belongs in drift and this record should be narrowed.
- Related (by citation elsewhere): ADR-051 (guard stops at structure), ADR-057/058 (percentage does not move for marked files).

## ADR-039 — A capability may be bound to a repository this one does not own (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-06)

- Author: bodurkalukasz.
- Context: R11 says every capability has an entry in `specs/capability-map.json` binding it to code globs; the coupling guard reads that map; the rule assumes the code is in this repo. Validation case `GATE-11` on `bazelbuild/bazel`: rule implementations for several languages live in satellite `rules_*` repositories it does not own — the capability is real, its spec belongs here, no glob here reaches its code. Same shape: a plugin architecture, a vendor SDK, a service whose client is generated from another repo's schema. Two moves, both defects: a glob that matches nothing (`--audit` reports it, correctly) or no map entry (`--audit` reports an orphan spec, correctly). "a map you argue with is a map that stops being maintained, and every capability in it loses its guard together."
- Options: leave it — rejected (teaches people to ignore the audit). A repo-level exemption list (`$unwatchable: ["rules-go", ...]`) — rejected: separates the declaration from the capability it describes; a place things accumulate. Cross-repo coupling (resolve the satellite repo, check its diff) — rejected outright for now: needs network, credentials, a checkout of somebody else's repo in a guard whose value is being dependency-free and running in five seconds; cannot work for a vendor SDK. An entry form that names the repository, with a reason — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules — an entry in `specs/capability-map.json` may be `{ "external": "<repo>", "reason": "<why no glob here reaches it>" }`:
  - Both fields are required; an external entry with no reason is refused by the guard with the map unusable, like a malformed glob entry.
  - Nothing is enforced for it: not counted as a glob, never reported as a glob matching nothing.
  - The capability still has its spec here: an external binding satisfies R11's entry requirement; `--audit` still reports a map key with no spec directory.
  - It is printed on every audit run and counted in the verdict line — "A hatch nobody sees is a hatch that widens."
  - Mixing is allowed: a capability may hold globs *and* an external entry; the part living here still couples.
- Consequences: a repo with satellite implementations holds a complete, honest map. The audit distinguishes three states: watched, deliberately unwatchable, broken — only the third fails. Cost: a capability's code can go unguarded by declaration (true of a repo not containing the code); the reason plus per-run note keeps it from being a shortcut. Cost: the guard cannot verify the named repository exists, is spelled right, or still holds that code — "a record, not a link that is followed."
- Confirmation: `GATE-11`; six cases in `tools/spec-guard-test.mjs`: an external binding passes and is named, is not reported as an empty glob, is refused without a reason, still needs a spec, does not claim any local path, does not weaken coupling on local globs.
- What this rules out: reading another repository from inside the coupling guard; cross-repo coupling, if built, is a different mechanism with its own record.
- Revisit when: external entries appear for code that is in this repo (reasons read as excuses), or a repo's audit line shows more external bindings than globs.
- Related: R11 (amended by one clause), ADR-002, ADR-016. Cited by ADR-042.

## ADR-040 — The scale profile binds on reach, not on headcount (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-07)

- Author: bodurkalukasz; Decided by: the author. Tags: profiles, adoption, manifest, solo.
- Context: ADR-011 settled the mechanism and the principle, not the trigger. The sentence reaching adopters was in the profile picker (`../method/adoption.md`) and the FAQ: "Start `core`, flip to `scale` when the second regular contributor arrives." Problems:
  - The trigger and the record name different populations: a headcount trigger at two makes a pair of developers the enterprise; nothing on the scale list is what a second pair of hands changes.
  - The promised discount is not the one the manifest gives: measured, `--profile scale` checks 91 entries, `--profile core` 82; self-verify prints `9 scale-only entries skipped (--profile core)`. In `SPEC.md` only R11's blocking coupling guard and R16's `spec-guard` step carry a *(scale)* marker — core discounts roughly a tenth of the tree and two clauses.
  - CI is described backwards: the picker says core runs guards "locally/pre-commit" (ADR-011 said the same), but `.github/workflows/spec-guard.yml` is a required entry at the core profile (R16); R16 puts `self-verify` and `spec-structure` in CI for every repo with only `spec-guard` marked *(scale)*; the shipped workflow runs the coupling guard advisory at core and blocking at scale, and the full-tree coupling audit blocking at every profile. A solo adopter who skips CI gets drift 1 on a required entry from a red PR.
  - Personas and architecture are core entries: `docs/personas.md` (R10) and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` required at both profiles; "full persona roster" and "C4 discipline" are how much gets written — a judgment nothing measures.
  - Two named discounts were never charged: the tracker bridge is required at no profile (ADR-032: optional sidecar); release-notes curation has no manifest entry at any profile.
  - The nine scale-only entries: `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `.github/pull_request_template.md`, `docs/analytics.md`, `docs/journeys`, `docs/research`, `docs/cycles`, `scripts/cycle-guard.mjs`, and the `spec-guard` and `cycle-guard` guards — contribution mechanics for people who cannot be told in person, research about users nobody in the repo is, work cycles for a team that must agree what it committed to. "Every one is triggered by somebody who is not in the room."
  - Field runs: every adoption inferred the profile from committer counts; the smallest team in the suite is 38 authors; nothing adopted at 2-5 people — the range the binary decides hardest.
- Options: A — keep headcount, move the number (three, five) — rejected: an arbitrary threshold is still the wrong variable; fails both directions (a solo library with ten thousand users needs curated notes; a co-located team of four needs none of the scale set). B — a third profile for small teams — rejected: ADR-011's anticipated third profile is a regulated `audit` tier for a different audience; splitting a nine-entry difference makes every future entry classify three ways. C — retrigger on reach, and state the measured discount — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules:
  - ADR-011's mechanism untouched (per-entry `profile`, `--profile core|scale`, rendered views, flag-plus-delta upgrade). Principle made specific: "**Core is whatever keeps knowledge alive; scale is whatever carries it to someone who is not in the room.**"
  - The trigger is a set of conditions, and headcount is not one. Any one is enough to be `scale`: work handed off asynchronously (somebody picks up a piece without the author reachable); somebody contributes from outside the conversation (outside contributor, rotating team, vendor, a maintainer arriving after everyone has gone); somebody outside the repo reads execution state (a stakeholder following a board); the repo has a release audience that is not its authors; it is designed for users nobody here is.
  - Two people at one desk shipping to each other are `core`, and compliant. One person shipping a library to ten thousand strangers meets the release-audience condition and is not.
  - The picker states the measured discount: names the nine entries and says what the flag does not change: CI is required at core, personas and architecture are core entries whose depth is a judgment, the tracker bridge and release-notes curation are required at no profile.
  - The 2-5 range gets a route, not a profile — "There is no third profile and there will not be one for team size." Two routes already in the mechanism: declare `scale` and except the documents it does not carry (each in the manifest's `exceptions` with a reason; self-verify reports excepted rather than drift and keeps it in the adoption denominator (R17)); or declare `core` and carry what a condition triggered (carrying more than required has never been drift, and is never verified). The first is the paved road for the six document entries (an exception carries a reason; a voluntary extra carries nothing). It does not extend to the other three: `scripts/cycle-guard.mjs` and the two guards are outside the hatch by design — waiving a live check removes it instead of recording a deviation — so declaring `scale` means accepting that R11's coupling guard blocks rather than advises.
  - "Reading `core` as 'we are only two, so none of this is for us' is the one wrong answer the conditions exist to prevent."
  - No entry changes profile in this record; inventing which way to move "is what `PROF-3` is gated against."
- Consequences: a future entry classifies itself by asking who reads it (the test ADR-028 and ADR-032 already applied); solo/paired adopters are told the truth about CI (core is visibly heavier than the old picker claimed). Cost: "reach" is a judgment where "the second contributor" was a fact — conditions will be argued about. Cost: the picker gets longer (one section in one document). The nine entries are named in prose, so the manifest and the picker can disagree: `docs/facts.json` declares the count against the manifest (catches an entry changing profile; not a rename).
- Confirmation: `docs/method/adoption.md` and `docs/faq.md` state the conditions, name the nine entries, say what the flag does not change, and no longer say guards run locally; `docs/facts.json` declares the scale-entry count with the manifest as its home so `facts-check` fails when an entry moves profile; reproducible: `node scripts/self-verify.mjs --profile core` prints the number of skipped entries, matching the picker.
- What this rules out: a headcount anywhere in the profile picker, in any document; a third profile for team size (the `audit` tier stays open on its own terms).
- Revisit when: an adopter in the 2-5 range cannot answer the conditions without already knowing the artifact list (conditions describe the answer rather than ask the question); or every repo meeting one condition meets all five (reach is a single bit; the binary was right, only its label wrong).
- Related: ADR-011 (revised), ADR-028, ADR-032, R16, R17 and ADR-004, `docs/open-questions/profiles.md`, `PROF-3` in `backlog.md`.

## ADR-041 — The bounded period of work is called a sprint (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-07)

- Decided by: the author. Tags: backlog, sprints, planning, vocabulary, scale.
- Context: ADR-028 named the bounded period a **cycle**; its substance (a goal, an agreed and movable end date, the intents pulled in, one file per team, pool-or-exactly-one-period invariant) has held. The name was chosen against `sprint` deliberately (recorded in `work-periods`: "sprint carries the whole ceremony apparatus - planning poker, retro, velocity as a commitment ... Borrowing the word borrows the argument"); `cycle` picked because Linear uses it. It did not arrive pre-explained: "people read `cycle` and stop" — every reader spends the same first question "is this a sprint?" (answer: yes, minus some ceremony). The dashboard made it loud: a tab label needing a sentence of explanation. "evidence wins" — the cost of `cycle` is paid by every reader forever; the cost of `sprint` is paid once, to the Scrum-fluent, in one clause.
- Options (table): keep `cycle` (shipped, matches Linear, no baggage / every reader pays the tax; they stop reading); `sprint`, defined here — **chosen** (universally understood; maps 1:1 onto any tracker / imports expectations — points, velocity, fixed timebox — that must be denied explicitly); `sprint` on the surface, `cycle` in the tree (no migration / two names for one thing — the drift this project keeps finding); `iteration` (neutral / as unfamiliar as `cycle` without Linear's precedent).
- Decision / rules — the bounded period of work is a sprint, everywhere: `docs/sprints/<team>/<slug>.md`, the `sprint-open` and `sprint-close` procedures, `scripts/sprint-guard.mjs`, the `work-sprints` capability spec, every dashboard label. What a sprint is here:
  - a goal in one sentence, an agreed end date that may move, and the intents pulled in; several run in parallel, one per team;
  - an intent is in the backlog pool or in exactly one sprint, never both;
  - no estimates: sizes are a splitting trigger and a cold-start fallback, never summed (ADR-029); no story points, no planning poker;
  - no velocity as a commitment: throughput is measured from closed sprints and used to project, never to promise; a sprint that does not finish is a fact about the projection, not a failure to be explained;
  - the date is agreed, not a timebox: it can move; what may not move is the record that it moved; an unfinished intent is returned to the pool or split, never quietly re-dated;
  - no ceremony is prescribed: planning, review, retrospective are a team's business; the two shipped procedures are not meetings.
  - Scale-only, as before: a single-maintainer repository is not out of compliance for having none.
- Consequences: ADR-028 stands as the record of why the artifact exists and what it holds, superseded only on the name. Renames in the shipped tree: `docs/cycles/` -> `docs/sprints/`, `cycle-guard.mjs` -> `sprint-guard.mjs`, the two skills, the capability spec, the manifest entries; no releases yet, so no adopter carries old paths from a tag — the showcase fixture is migrated in the same wave. The open question `work-periods` is answered rather than deleted: records the first answer was overturned by use. Risk taken knowingly: somebody arrives expecting Scrum and finds no points and no velocity — a one-sentence conversation, once per reader.
- Revisit when: readers assume the borrowed word imports points and a velocity commitment despite the denial, treating their absence as the standard being incomplete — if that shows up in the field the way the `cycle` confusion did, reopen the same way.
- Related: ADR-028, ADR-029, `work-periods`. Cited by ADR-044, ADR-051.

## ADR-043 — An existing decision process gets an exception, not a second home (status: Accepted (2026-08-09), date: 2026-08-09)

- Tags: methodology, adoption, decisions.
- Context: R5 requires a contestable, re-litigable choice be recorded as a decision record, and `docs/decision-records` is a required manifest entry every align run fills. Nothing in Step 0 asks whether the target already has one. git's design decisions run on its mailing list (older than this standard by decades); vim's through its own maintainer-led process; neither writes an ADR. When found, ask rather than assume: adopt this standard's ADR/BDR mechanism going forward (the default), or keep the repo's own process, recorded as an exception on R5's manifest entry with the reason named. No new rule number.
- Consequences: positive — a repo with a working decision process no longer gets a second one by omission; the choice is named and checkable. Negative — one more detection and possible question; on the majority of repos the signals are absent and the question is never surfaced.
- Confirmation: `SKILL.md`'s Step 0 measurement pass names the detection and the two-answer question; a repo answering "keep mine" carries a `docs/decision-records` exception with a stated reason instead of an empty required directory.

## ADR-044 — R18 splits by publishable unit, not by team (status: Accepted (2026-08-09), date: 2026-08-09)

- Tags: releases, changelog, monorepo.
- Context: R18 already splits along one axis — a repo with more than one release line (R23) carries one changelog per line. No answer for `rails/rails`: thirteen gems (`activerecord`, `actionpack`, `activesupport`, ...), each independently versioned and released with its own `CHANGELOG.md`, all changing together on one branch. Not a release-line problem (one branch, one history) — the split is by which publishable unit a change belongs to. R18 as specified would ask for one root changelog.

- Revisit when: a real repo needs both axes at once — multiple release lines and multiple independently-publishable units in the same tree.
- Related: ADR-028 and ADR-041 (the mechanism that does not transfer), ADR-035 (the release-line clause the new clause sits beside).

  - Decision records stay separate: an ADR or BDR is a record of a fork *taken*; a backlog row is a live intent or a standing doubt. Folding records in would lose the immutability a record is for, or force every accepted ADR into an open-ended table. "This was checked directly with the owner rather than assumed."
  - Status is per-type, not one shared vocabulary: `task` and `bug` keep `todo` / `doing` / `blocked` / `done`. `idea` keeps `idea` / `exploring` / `approved` | `parked` | `dropped`, graduating to `graduated`. `open-question` gets two values: `open` (nothing decided yet) and `decided` (a decision stands) — an open question has no completion state; a decided entry is still actively open to a better answer (the type's permanent condition, stated in `docs/open-questions/README.md` since before this record). A third state for "currently being re-litigated" was considered and dropped (every decided entry is equally open to challenge; the strength of the standing answer lives in the row's `why` text). Reusing `done` for a decided open question was considered and dropped (`done` reads as settled-forever).
- Consequences: `standard/scripts/generate-dashboard/index.mjs`'s `parseBacklog()`/`asItem()` and `src/page.js`'s backlog-tab rendering gain optional support for `type` and a per-type status legend — backward compatible (a `backlog.md` without a `type` column reads every row as `task`). `docs/open-questions/README.md` and `docs/ideas/README.md` lose their tables, replaced by a pointer to `backlog.md`; `docs/README.md` and `tools/docsite.mjs`'s nav untouched (the `open-questions.html` / `ideas.html` pages still generate from the folders). `CONTRIBUTING.md` wording fix: a new open question is added to `backlog.md`, not listed in the README. This is a repo-own (zone 1) decision: the shipped standard's R14 (ideas) and R15 (backlog) contracts are untouched — an adopter is not required to run `docs/open-questions/` at all, and one that does keeps its shape. Nothing supersedes ADR-010.
- Compliance: `backlog.md` parses with a `type` column on every row; the two READMEs carry no table; every topic that had a table row has an equivalent `backlog.md` row linking to its detail file.
- Revisit when: the row's `why` text proves insufficient to signal how strong a decided open question's standing answer is — then the `open-question` status vocabulary needs a structured field, not just prose.
- Related (by citation elsewhere): ADR-051 (the `decided` exclusion from archiving follows this), ADR-053.

## ADR-047 — the adoption ping is informed, not asked, and carries the minimum that still means something (status: Accepted, date: 2026-08-10)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: nothing answers "how many repos actually run this standard" — honest answer "unknown"; `PRODUCT.md` claims real-world mechanics and the README claims field-run adoption, but a skeptic has no count (see `EXHIBIT-1` in `backlog.md`). Passive option considered first: `align-to-standards` leaves `.standards-version` in every aligned repo, so a GitHub code search for that filename gives a count with no code — rejected as primary source because most adopting repos are private; the number would be a floor that silently under-reports. A consent-gated ping (ask, wait for yes/no) — rejected: the owner had watched a related idea (asking adopters for feedback at the end of a run) talked out of existence on the grounds that almost nobody answers a voluntary prompt; a yes/no gate costs nothing to skip, so it would be skipped; same reasoning kills a self-registration list (an `ADOPTERS.md` an adopter PRs, the Kubernetes/Envoy pattern). What survives: a ping that fires automatically and is disclosed, not requested — the Homebrew, Next.js and .NET CLI telemetry model: on by default, named plainly in output, switched off by one environment variable. Only honest if the payload could not identify who sent it — "a disclosure covers a fact, not a permission slip".
- Decision / rules:
  - `align-to-standards` sends one ping when a run reaches a completed state (drift 0, or an explicit terminal outcome for a run that did not fully align), and *tells* the adopter: "Sending an anonymous signal about this adoption (stack, standard version, final drift) - no repo name, no code content. Turn it off with `REPOSTDS_NO_TELEMETRY=1`." (English, per `AGENTS.md`'s working-language rule — the skill runs against any adopter's repo.)
  - Payload exactly: `{"event": "adoption_completed", "stack": "node", "standards_version": "1.4.0", "drift": 0, "fully_aligned": true, "date": "2026-08-10"}`. No repo name, org, URL, or free-text field (free text would eventually carry whatever an adopter typed). `date` is day-granularity, not a timestamp (an exact time is an axis an adopter's activity log could be correlated against). No field persists across pings (no client-generated UUID, no repeat-visit token) — a stable identifier is still tracking.
  - `REPOSTDS_NO_TELEMETRY=1` (checked before the request is built) skips the ping entirely — what makes "inform, don't ask" defensible.
  - Endpoint v1: the account's free `*.workers.dev` address, not a subdomain of `repositorystandards.com`. Original plan: NS-delegate just `stats.` so the rest of the domain's DNS in home.pl stays untouched — but Cloudflare's self-serve "connect a domain" flow refused a bare subdomain ("provide the root domain, not a subdomain"), live-tested. A custom subdomain would mean an Enterprise plan or moving the whole zone to Cloudflare — bigger and riskier. Live at `https://stats.repositorystandards.workers.dev`; the account's workers.dev subdomain was renamed from its default to `repositorystandards` (the account's only other tenant, `stayget.com`, uses it for DNS, not Workers). `GET /` returns `{"count": N}`; `POST /` accepts the payload — no `/adoption` path, the worker name (`stats`) already says what it is.
  - Storage: Cloudflare D1, one append-only table. A plain KV counter was rejected once the payload grew past a single number — `drift` and `fully_aligned` are worth querying later ("what's the drift distribution across real adoptions").
- Consequences: a new small service outside this repo's CI (a Worker + a D1 database, deployed by the maintainer via `wrangler`, not built by `checks.yml` or `pages.yml`). `align-to-standards`'s completion step gains the notice text and the `curl` call, gated by the env var — repo-own (zone 1): an adopter who forks or vendors the skill without this repo's Worker URL has a call that fails silently or is stripped; nothing in R1-R25 depends on it. No dashboard reads this data yet (turning D1 rows into a number `EXHIBIT-1` or the site can point at is separate follow-up). The custom-domain question (`stats.repositorystandards.com`, or migrating the zone) stays open.
- Compliance: the completion step prints the disclosure and issues the POST unless `REPOSTDS_NO_TELEMETRY` is set; the POST body matches the schema field-for-field with no additional keys; the Worker persists only those fields, no request metadata (IP, headers).
- Revisit when: the free `workers.dev` address has proven the mechanism works (custom-domain question explicitly left open for that point); or the payload ever needs a field beyond the fixed schema — any addition, especially free text, reopens identifiability.
- Related: ADR-045 — a different mechanism for a different purpose (`record-run` is consent-gated, carries far more detail, feeds the human-prompting corpus; this ping is automatic-and-disclosed, minimal, only feeds a count; neither touches the other's storage). Cited by ADR-055 (the ping reports its count; counter read 12), ADR-056 (the badge pattern the pill copies).

## ADR-048 — Gate artifacts are read for shape, not presence (status: Accepted (2026-08-10), date: 2026-08-10)

- Tags: methodology, adoption, verification, consent.
- Context: ADR-042 made Gate 0's intake a required manifest entry because a run that skipped it left an indistinguishable diff; the same argument applies to Gates 2 and 5 and was never made. The checkmap (`docs/method/adoption.md`) calls the gate order and "every gate produces its artifact" rigid, framework-enforced, and specifies two outputs precisely: Gate 2 emits a health report (maturity per pass as `absent` / `partial` / `solid`, top risks, findings grouped by owner role); Gate 5 emits a counted backlog in a given shape, every item naming its owner role, the total described as "the go/no-go signal". Nothing read either. A real adoption on 2026-08-10 (a Node application, ~250 files changed) reached `self-verify` drift 0 having produced neither; the owner got the numbers only when they asked, and prose in place of both artifacts; their verdict: the adoption had happened *to* them rather than guided them — "the run measured the repo and skipped the person, and the green number then certified a process that had not happened." Two further findings: the count's format is documented where the adopter never looks (the checkmap, a Zone 1 method page; `standard/docs/backlog.md` — the template an adopted repo receives — carries the `owner` column but never mentioned the scope block); the consent question is asked before the evidence exists (Step 0 asks "plan-only or execute" before a single assessment pass; assessment-only is already a legal stop after Gate 2, so the go/no-go machinery exists and is not used as one).
- Options: A — strengthen wording in the skill — rejected ("Wording that is followed when convenient is what produced this run"). B — make the artifacts required manifest entries and stop — ADR-042's mechanism, necessary but not sufficient: a file can exist, be non-placeholder, satisfy `fill-from-repo`, and still omit the count — rejected alone, adopted as half of D. C — a review-time checklist — rejected (blast radius landed by the time anyone reviews a 250-file PR). D — a manifest entry for the missing artifact plus a guard that reads both artifacts' shape — **chosen**; "A guard cannot carry a manifest exception, so neither half can be waived by an adopting repo that would rather not."
- Decision (Option D):
  1. New rule R27 (`SPEC.md`): the assessment and count gates MUST leave artifacts that carry what the gate is for — all eight passes rated on the three-value scale, top risks named, findings grouped by owner role, and a scope block whose stated total is the sum of its own categories with every item naming its owner role.
  2. New manifest entry `docs/adoption-assessment.md`, `adapt: fill-from-repo`, `required: true`, `profile: core`, `rule: R27` — sibling of the `docs/adoption-intake.md` entry.
  3. New template `standard/docs/adoption-assessment.md`.
  4. New guard `standard/scripts/adoption-gates.mjs`, registered in the manifest's `guards`, run in CI with `tools/adoption-gates-test.mjs`. It checks shape and arithmetic, never judgment ("it cannot tell a considered `partial` from a guessed one, and does not pretend to"). It skips itself when no assessment file exists (self-verify already reports that as drift; counting twice makes one gap look like two).
  5. `standard/docs/backlog.md` gains the alignment scope block, so the format reaches the repo that must produce it.
  6. The consent question splits: Step 0 still asks "plan-only or execute" and records the answer, but that answer is an intent. The consent that licenses a wave is re-asked after Gate 2, with the drift number and the count in front of the user. A user who said "execute" at Step 0 and stops after reading the report has not gone back on their word, and the skill says so.
- Consequences: positive — an adoption reaching drift 0 has necessarily produced the two artifacts a human reads to decide, with a total that adds up and an owner on every item; the arithmetic check catches a hand-maintained total drifting from its parts. Negative — one more required file on every adopted repo, including assessment-only (the run that most obviously has one); the guard enforces a shape — a repo whose honest assessment does not fit eight passes says so as a deviation rather than silently omitting rows ("the alternative is a report whose missing pass is indistinguishable from a pass nobody ran"). Neutral — R27 starts with no validation case, like R26.
- Confirmation: `standard.manifest.json` carries the `docs/adoption-assessment.md` entry and the `adoption-gates` guard; `SPEC.md` carries R27 and its rule count reads R1-R27; `onboard.md` writes the report as the assessment pass's closing action and stops for the go/no-go before the first wave; `standard/docs/backlog.md` carries the scope block; a repo whose scope block claims a total its categories do not sum to fails `adoption-gates` while `self-verify` still reads drift 0.
- Revisit when: agents start producing shape-correct but guessed or hollow ratings that pass the arithmetic guard — reproducing this record's failure mode one level deeper, as ADR-042's gap reappeared here.
- Related: ADR-042 (mechanism reused, argument extended), ADR-020 (the plan-only-vs-execute question split into intent and consent), ADR-006 (a gate artifact that had to be more than present). Principle applied by ADR-049 (Confirmation says what is not checked), ADR-050, ADR-057.

## ADR-049 — A discovery entry is typed, and the stamp bounds asking rather than reading (status: Accepted (2026-08-13), date: 2026-08-13)

- Tags: method, discovery, provenance, retrieval.
- Context: ADR-024 holds and is not reopened. A design review on 2026-08-13, prompted by the owner asking what a reader can get back out of a dossier, found three things the shape does not carry. Motivating case: a subject gets researched from scratch and partway through somebody remembers it was explained at length on a call months earlier — "The whole point of writing extracts down is that this cannot happen."
  - An entry has no type and no purpose: meeting-or-mail survives only as a slug in the filename (`kickoff-meeting`, `mail-from-provider`) — free text, not filterable or countable; *why* the session happened is asked for nowhere — "notes record answers and not the question."
  - An entry does not say what came of it: the state column (`new -> folded-into-spec | superseded-by: <record> | open`) says consumed but never by what; `folded-into-spec` names no spec; the trail from one meeting to one decision exists only in whoever remembers it.
  - Nothing bears the subjects a session touched: a dossier is one topic by construction, but one session is rarely one topic (a call about invoicing bears on pricing, tax and refunds); material is findable only by whoever knows what it was filed under.
  - The stamp does two jobs, one nobody chose: ADR-024 rule 4 says agents ask only about entries newer than `Last reconciled:`; every consumer (`spec-clarify`, `spec-impact`, `spec-plan`) reads *only above the stamp*, nothing instructs reading below it — so the stamp also marks everything under it as not worth opening. That is where the durable material sits: a decision leaves for an ADR/BDR, behaviour for a spec, work for the backlog; what has no other home is **understanding** — how something works, why a constraint exists. "The mechanism that prevents explaining something twice is the same mechanism that hides the explanation."
- Options: A — a generated index file (`docs/discovery/README.md` written by a client-side script with `--check` in CI) — rejected: a second description of what entries already say; needs a shipped script, manifest entry, test and workflow step to reproduce what one typed field answers with `grep`; a hand-maintained index is worse and already argued against in `docs/tree/docs-discovery.md`; revisit if dossier count makes searching insufficient. B — YAML front-matter on entries — rejected on consistency: artifact metadata in this standard is a two-column table at the top (every ADR and BDR); front-matter is the skills' shape. C — a new home for explanations (`docs/explainers/`) — rejected: material whose provenance matters is a discovery entry by ADR-024's definition; "The explanation was never in the wrong place; it was in a place nobody was told to read." D — type the entry header and split the stamp's two jobs — **chosen**.
- Decision:
  1. An entry carries a typed header table, shipped as `standard/docs/discovery/_entry-template.md`, filled by `discovery-digest`: **Kind**, **Date**, **Present**, **Purpose**, **Touches**, **Raw**, **Outcome**. A table, not front-matter; field names fixed because read by `grep`.
  2. Kind is a closed vocabulary: `meeting`, `call`, `mail`, `thread`, `ticket`, `document`, `note`.
  3. Purpose is required — one line on why the session happened; the skill asks when the handover does not say.
  4. Touches is the cross-cutting axis: subjects the material bears on beyond the dossier it is filed in; does not change where anything is stored (per-topic filing stands).
  5. Outcome is per entry: the record, spec or backlog row that came out of it, or `none yet`.
  6. `## Explained here` is a named section in the entry template for the understanding a session produced — "an optional section with a name gets filled".
  7. The stamp bounds asking, not reading (narrows ADR-024 rule 4): `Last reconciled:` governs what may be raised with a human; it carries no authority over what may be *read*: an agent researching a subject or explaining how something works searches `docs/discovery/` in full, including entries older than the stamp, and cites what it finds instead of re-deriving it. Ships in `standard/AGENTS.md` as its own trigger, beside the one that files material.
  8. The dossier README's entry table mirrors the header (`Date | Kind | Source | Touches | State | Outcome`).
  9. No new SPEC rule and no new artifact: R5 names the home; this changes the shape of what goes in it.
- Consequences: the five questions a reader arrives with (meeting or mail, when, why, what came of it, what else it bears on) are answered by the header. Retrieval stops depending on knowing the filing: `Touches` plus full-text search over an unbounded dossier makes the motivating case fail safely. `discovery-digest` asks for one more thing on intake (`Purpose`) and fills `Outcome` on later passes — a real cost every time material lands. Existing dossiers do not become invalid: added fields are absent, not wrong; entries are append-only (ADR-024), so a dossier gains the header shape as new material arrives.
- Confirmation: mechanical — the entry template and amended dossier template ship in the tree (`tree-check` holds them; the manifest's `sha256` for `discovery-digest/SKILL.md` covers the procedure). Not mechanical — nothing verifies a real `Purpose` or honest `Touches`, nothing forces an agent to search before researching; `docs/discovery` is optional `fill-from-repo`, so a client-side gate would fire on a folder most repos do not carry, and the field is prose; the check is `discovery-digest`'s `Done When` list and review (stated plainly "because ADR-048 has just finished arguing that an artifact nobody reads is not a gate").
- Revisit when: a repo carries enough dossiers that searching `Touches` stops being enough (option A's generated index earns its cost); an entry reaches review with `Purpose` empty or restating the title (the skill is not really asking); agents are observed re-researching something a dossier already explains after this ships (the `AGENTS.md` trigger is not firing — fix the trigger's wording).
- Related: ADR-024 (rule 4 narrowed by point 7; everything else stands), ADR-010, ADR-048.

## ADR-050 — A decision record must name what would reopen it (status: Accepted (2026-08-13), date: 2026-08-13)

- Tags: decision-records, verification, guards.
- Context: `discovery-digest` step 4 pulls every record's `## Revisit when` across the decision log and checks new discovery material against it — the only mechanism that reads decisions *back*; without it a record sits past its own trigger and only an agent's unbroken context would notice. Measured on this repo's log 2026-08-13: 25 of 48 records carried the field, 23 did not — including every record from 040 to 048, the nine most recent. The tripwire covered roughly half while the skill's text said "Every ADR/BDR carries a `## Revisit when` field". The drift is not carelessness: the field was asked for in two places that cannot check anything — the shipped `_template.md` prints a prompt and `adr-write` lists it among sections — nothing read it back, so it decayed the way ADR-048 describes, "in its own decision log."
- Options: A — soften the claim (reword step 4 to "records that carry a `## Revisit when` field") — rejected: a mechanism half-useful forever; "An accurate description of a broken tripwire is still a broken tripwire." B — require it going forward only, grandfather the 23 — rejected: the missing 23 are the newest records, the ones most likely near a live trigger. C — require it, backfill, and gate it — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules:
  - Every decision record that still stands carries a filled `## Revisit when` section, and `scripts/decision-records-check.mjs` fails the build without one. `Proposed` is included: naming the signal is part of writing the record, not of accepting it.
  - Filled, not present: a missing heading, an empty body, and a heading still carrying the template's own prompt text all count as no signal (the third has a heading and non-empty body, so heading-counting calls it filled). Guidance comments are stripped before the body is judged.
  - Superseded and rejected records are exempt — a record that no longer binds has nothing to reopen. Status is matched at the *start* of the value, so `Accepted - supersedes ADR-013` is an accepted record that still owes its own signal.
  - An honest "nothing reopens this" is a real answer and must be written as one (e.g. "nothing short of dropping the two-layer split"). "An invented threshold is worse than an empty section, because it fires - or fails to fire - on a number nobody meant."
  - The 23 records are backfilled from what each already stated: a rejected option's condition, a named cost, a self-declared limit. Five already answered under `## How we would know we were wrong`; those headings were renamed rather than joined by a second section (two headings for one question is the drift this project keeps finding).
  - The skill's claim states its limit: step 4 says the set is complete because the guard makes it so, and that a log predating the guard will still hold records that cannot be tripped — those get a line in the report rather than silence.
- Consequences: positive — step 4 greps a complete set backed by a check; both `_template.md` files and both writing skills say the section is required and an honest "no signal" is legal; the 23 backfilled sections are the first time several decisions state what would end them (most already contained the answer in `Options considered` or an accepted cost). Cost — an adopter whose log predates the guard meets 23-ish failures at once on the alignment run; the failure names each record and what it needs. Cost (same as ADR-048) — the guard reads shape, never judgment; it removes the silent half of the corpus, not the possibility of a hollow answer. Follow-up: nothing checks a `Revisit when` is *specific* — a review question; a heuristic would repeat the mistake.
- Confirmation: `tools/decision-records-check-test.mjs` holds eight new cases: a missing section, an empty one, each template's prompt left in place (ADR and BDR templates are worded differently), a heading whose only body is an HTML comment, a superseded record exempted, an accepted record that supersedes another still required, an honest "nothing reopens this" accepted; the live case asserting this repo's own `docs/decision-records/` does not trip its own guard covers this; the guard runs with `--block` in `.github/workflows/checks.yml`.
- Revisit when: backfilled or new sections read as boilerplate that satisfies the guard and can never fire ("when this stops working") — the guessed-rating failure ADR-048 names; the answer would be a review duty, not a stricter regex. Or adopters with pre-guard logs routinely suppress the check rather than backfill — the gate fires at the wrong moment and belongs at record-write time with alignment only reporting it.
- Related: ADR-048, ADR-033 (the other direction of the same loop), ADR-024.

## ADR-051 — Closing a backlog row is a relocation, not a deletion (status: Accepted (2026-08-13); revised by ADR-053, date: 2026-08-13)

- Decided by: Łukasz Bodurka. Tags: methodology, backlog, verification.
- Context:
  - The standard says when a row may close and never where it goes. `standard/docs/backlog.md` "What feeds this backlog": "A finished item leaves the backlog only when its **definition of done** is met" (when). Same file, "Format" section, as an aside in the status-vocabulary sentence: "(drop `done` rows on release, or let the Backlog.md tool archive them)" — the only place answering where. No rule number, no guard, no destination, two options of which one is not ours.
  - The tool named is a third party's and nothing here has ever run it: `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` records Backlog.md (github.com/MrLesk/Backlog.md) as "not taken from, but deliberately stayed compatible with"; no file installs, wraps, requires or tests against it; no manifest entry; no `checks.yml` check. The delete option is the one adopters can actually follow — "a deletion rule wearing an archival rule's clothes."
  - The parenthesis is already load-bearing on tooling: when `sprint-guard` was built, a lingering `done` row in the pool was considered as a third violation and left unchecked (recorded in `docs/validation/ai-prompting/runs/2026-08-06-q-track.json`): the page "licenses it in the same sentence that declares the vocabulary", and a guard failing every such row "would make that licence impossible to follow."
  - The dashboard's default measured what the file contains: the backlog view opened with `state = { q: '', type: null, hideDone: true }` and a "show finished" chip starting unpressed — a view hiding part of its input by default expects the file to be full of rows nobody wants to read. (The chip is gone as of 0.9.13 and the pool is open-only by construction.)
  - Deletion is what this repository actually did: the 0.9.0 release cut (`bb1a3f3`) removed seventeen closed rows from `backlog.md` in one commit; two epics outlived it as a heading, a paragraph, a table header and no rows — "Gate health - the guards stay honest" and "The wizard co-authors, it does not hand out templates" — reading as abandoned for nine days (headings removed in 0.9.15). Some deleted content survived by diligence: GUARD-3's two measured drifts — `llms.txt` claiming twenty rules while `SPEC.md` had 21, `AGENTS.md` claiming 11 lifecycle skills against a tree holding 12 — live on in the header comment of `standard/scripts/facts-check.mjs` and in `standard/docs/conventions.md`.
  - The workaround costs more than the rule saves: this repo's pool carries `FIELD-1` and `EXHIBIT-1` at `todo` while holding finished, measured, dated work (`FIELD-1` the paragraphs, `EXHIBIT-1` the half of its question that is answered), because closing would throw it away — the failure the page warns about: "a row that lingers after its definition of done is met teaches everyone the list is stale".
  - The downstream case: `mybrand` (github.com/bodurkalukasz/mybrand), an adopter; `backlog.md` is 314 lines; 12 of 17 near-term rows closed, none archived; what the closed rows hold exists nowhere else: `INV-3` — a live check of grant state on the production database; the `public` schema lockdown genuinely applied; the `storage` schema block runs without error and is a silent no-op (the connection-string role does not own `storage.*`); a security control that runs clean and protects nothing; the exposure is unreachable only because PostgREST answers `PGRST002` on every table — an accident recorded as an accident. `STORAGE-2` — a design killed by a probe: Supabase image transforms answered `403 FeatureNotEnabled`, and the quality parameter on the plain object URL was silently ignored. `STORAGE-4` — a missing `apikey` header meant every upload and delete had been failing silently. `INV-2` — a row whose premise turned out half wrong. `INV-1` — outcome "working as designed, no change needed": no commit, so no CHANGELOG entry, so "the CHANGELOG cannot be where it goes"; delete the row and the next person asks the same question and pays again.
- Options: A — say the existing rule more firmly (keep deletion, drop the tool, name the moment) — rejected: "the current state with better grammar"; deletion is what makes people not close rows. B — archive by tool (adopt Backlog.md properly) — rejected: puts a third-party CLI between an adopter and their markdown, against R15's posture; answers where the *row* goes, nothing about the *content*. C — leave closed rows in place forever and let `hideDone` handle it — rejected: the dashboard is a projection for people who never open the repository; the file is what everyone else reads; mybrand's 314 lines is the cost after a few months. D — closing a row is a relocation — **chosen**.
- Decision — "A row does not close until its content has somewhere else to be. Then the row moves, it does not vanish.":
  1. The finding goes where findings live: a decision to a decision record, behaviour to the capability spec, raw material and provenance to a discovery dossier (`docs/method/taxonomy.md` already says which is which).
  2. The shipped change goes to the CHANGELOG under `## Unreleased` (R18 already requires this).
  3. The row moves to the archive, carrying its columns as written plus one `where` cell naming what its content became.
  4. A row whose content cannot be relocated was not done. Three honest readings, each with an action: the work is not finished (row stays open); the finding needs a record nobody has written (write it — a row of its own); the row is being abandoned rather than completed (status is not `done`).
  - Which vocabularies close: `task` and `bug` close on `done`. Ideas close on `graduated` and `dropped` (R14 already makes a graduating idea point at what it became; a `dropped` idea's file says why). `split:<id>` does not archive (the split pair is how `sprint-guard` reads a partially finished intent; the remainder row is live work). `decided` on an `open-question` does not archive — deliberate: the dashboard's `CLOSED` set already excludes it ("a standing decision open to challenge is the point of the type, not a completed state to hide (ADR-046)").
  - Where the archive lives: one file `docs/backlog-archive.md`, with `backlog-archive.md` at the root as its alternate path (the same pair `backlog.md` declares). One file rather than one per period; period structure lives inside as release headings (`## 0.9.0 - 2026-08-10`), leaving the split available later (a `git mv` of trailing sections). The file does not exist until the first row is archived; its `standard.manifest.json` entry is `required: false` at the core profile — same shape as `scripts/sprint-guard.mjs` (shipped, declared, not owed by a repo that does not need it yet).
  - When rows move, and who moves them: at the release cut, by whoever cuts the release (the release is a moment that already exists, belongs to one person, involves reading the changelog). *(scale)* `/sprint-close` archives the rows its sprint finished (already writes the sprint's outcome and holds the one-place invariant). Never automatically: choosing the destination is a judgment; "A command may prepare the move and refuse a row with no destination; it may not decide the destination."
  - What the dashboard does with it: closed rows stay reachable in the view — the binding clause of the section; named the "show finished" chip. Which control carries it was reopened (0.9.13): the chip was removed before the archive was built (it made the count under the list mean two things depending on a button nobody remembers pressing; the reader is served better by Timeline and Reports), leaving a requirement with no surface — tracked as `ARCHIVE-VIEW-1`, to be answered before the first archive is cut. Answered, and the answer is no surface (0.9.17, ADR-053): the clause is narrowed to nothing — the generator reads no archive, Timeline reads the sprint projection, "not hidden" was written against masking rows inside the pool view rather than against a file with its own name. `ARCHIVE-VIEW-1` closed into `backlog-archive.md` — this rule's first execution. This record does not touch the generator (two changes in flight in `standard/scripts/generate-dashboard/`; the dashboard change is a follow-up).
  - The guard, `scripts/backlog-archive-check.mjs`, core profile, blocking: `kind: diff` — a PR that removes a row from `backlog.md` without that id appearing in the archive fails (would have fired on `bb1a3f3` seventeen times); exempted: an id that appears in a sprint file (left the pool, did not close) and a `split:<id>` pair whose remainder is live. An archive row with an empty `where` fails (clause 4 made mechanical). A `where` naming a repository path that does not exist fails (same class as `decision-records-check` on an index row citing a missing file). An id present in both `backlog.md` and the archive fails (one-place invariant extended from pool/sprint to pool/archive). The skip applies to the archive's own checks, never to the removal check: the three archive-reading checks skip with no archive (as `sprint-guard`, `schema-pair`, `adoption-gates` skip themselves); the removal check fires whenever a base ref and a pool exist and says to create the archive ("an adopter who has archived nothing is precisely the adopter about to delete their first closed row"). Deliberately not checked: whether the destination's content captures the finding — substance, per ADR-038.
- Consequences: positive — closing stops being a choice between destroying knowledge and letting the list rot; clause 4's diagnostic catches the most expensive wrong row (the one everybody believes is finished); the `INV-1` class (verified, correct as built, nothing shipped) gets a home. Negative — another file to keep true; a `where` pointer whose target is rewritten still resolves (the guard proves the file exists, never that the finding is still in it); "a quietly rotten archive is worse than none." Negative — a row split across two files can rot in two places; mitigation: the archive holds no prose of its own (the row plus a pointer; an index of relocations, not a second account) — a discipline the guard cannot enforce. Negative, "the strongest case against this record": it sits uncomfortably against ADR-018 (history has one home; living documents carry no change-log sections); claimed as a narrowing, not an exception: the CHANGELOG answers "what shipped, in which release" ordered by release and cannot answer "what happened to `INV-3`"; a closed row that shipped nothing never enters it; "If the maintainer reads the archive as a second history rather than as an index into the first, this record should be rejected outright rather than trimmed." Negative — the release cut gains a judgment step. Negative — the diagnostic is gameable (a `where` can point at any existing file; the guard catches the empty and broken answer, never the lazy one). Follow-ups: the dashboard change; a command that prepares the move and refuses a row with no destination; the seventeen rows removed at `bb1a3f3` are recoverable from that commit if the maintainer wants real initial content.
- Confirmation: the rule and its guard landed in one change: `scripts/backlog-archive-check.mjs` with its `standard.manifest.json` path entry, hash and `checks` entry; the optional `backlog-archive.md` path entry beside `backlog.md`; `tools/backlog-archive-check-test.mjs` covering the four refusals and each exemption ("a guard nobody can close a row past is as broken as one that never fires"); the line in `checks.yml`. Two corrections from building it: the draft said the guard "skips itself where no archive file exists" — taken literally that exempts every repository this rule is written for; only the archive-reading checks skip. The tree ships `docs/backlog-archive.md` as a template (not called for by the draft) because `tree-check` requires every declared manifest entry to exist in the shipped tree; the template carries the header, the `where` column and the instruction to delete the file until the first row moves. Unconfirmed: whether a `where` pointer keeps pointing at a file that still discusses the finding.
- Revisit when: a sample of `where` pointers in a real adopter's archive shows more than a small share resolving to files that no longer discuss the row's finding; or the archive grows past the size that made the pool unreadable, at which point the per-period split stops being optional.
- Related: ADR-018 (narrowed; weigh this against it first), ADR-046, ADR-010 (an archive of closed intents is neither intents nor execution history), ADR-028 and ADR-041 (`/sprint-close` as the scale trigger), ADR-038, R15 in `standard/SPEC.md` (given a second half). Narrowed by ADR-053.

## ADR-052 — Alignment tracks a provenance commit, not a version string (status: Accepted (2026-08-18), date: 2026-08-18)

- Tags: manifest, update-to-latest, self-verify, drift.
- Context: `.standards-version` is the only thing a repo's manifest copy carries about where it last aligned. Since CONTRIBUTING.md's PR-bumps-PATCH-by-default rule (R18/R25), `main` can carry unreleased change under a version number that hasn't shipped, so the string no longer names one exact tree. `update-to-version` derived its delta by resolving it back to a commit via `git log -- VERSION` — ambiguous by design. Second gap: when the standard removes a path (a retired script, a renamed skill folder), nothing checks the removal happened; a repo that skipped or half-applied an old update keeps the stale file forever.
- Options: keep deriving the commit from the version string — rejected: one version number can name a range of trees on `main`. Fingerprint the shipped tree (content hash) — rejected: identifies the state without locating it; `git diff` needs two refs and a hash cannot be resolved back without searching history. Generate `removedPaths` from the diff between two releases — rejected: a diff cannot tell "removed" from "renamed" or "moved under a profile flag"; a wrong auto-entry fails every adopter's CI.
- Decision — two manifest additions, one per root cause:
  - `provenanceCommit` — a new top-level field in the manifest a repo carries, holding the standards repo's full commit SHA it last aligned to. `update-to-version` (renamed `update-to-latest` — "the target was always latest, never a pin, which is already `.standards-version`'s stated job") writes it on every successful run and reads it back to compute the next delta. First run after this ships: back-fill once from the old lookup, then carry forward. `.standards-version` keeps its job — the human-facing bookmark, still what self-verify's version check and the update-watch workflow read.
  - `removedPaths` — an append-only list in the manifest (`{ path, since, note }`), hand-maintained at release time like `CHANGELOG.md`, not generated. `self-verify` gains a check phase: every listed path must not exist on disk, reported as drift like any other guard, waivable only through the existing `exceptions` mechanism. Needs no provenance data — an unconditional existence check; a repo that never had the old path passes trivially.
- Consequences: positive — the delta is computed from the tree a repo actually has; a removal is something CI fails on, not something remembered from a changelog. Cost — `removedPaths` is hand-maintained (same failure mode as `CHANGELOG.md`: an entry can be forgotten). Follow-up — the one-time back-fill lookup (`git log -- VERSION`) stays as dead weight once every aligned repo has run it.
- Confirmation: `self-verify.mjs` reports a `removedPaths` violation like any guard failure; a repo carrying a listed path with no exception fails at drift > 0; `update-to-latest`'s SKILL.md reads `provenanceCommit` first and falls back to the version-string lookup only when the field is absent.
- Revisit when: the back-fill path stops finding a matching commit for some legitimately old repo — the fallback then needs a documented failure mode instead of an assumed match.
- Related: ADR-004 (the exceptions mechanism reused to waive a `removedPaths` entry).

## ADR-053 — The backlog view does not carry the archive (status: Accepted (2026-08-19), date: 2026-08-19)

- Tags: backlog, dashboard, methodology.
- Context: ADR-051 made one dashboard clause binding ("Closed rows stay reachable in the view ... Work that has been relocated has not been hidden") and named the "show finished" chip. The chip was removed in 0.9.13 for an unrelated defect (it made the count under the pool mean two things depending on a button), leaving the requirement with nothing carrying it — `ARCHIVE-VIEW-1`. Two things true of the code, neither checked when the requirement was written: no surface reads the archive (`standard/scripts/generate-dashboard/index.mjs` contains no reference to `backlog-archive.md`); Timeline, the first-named candidate, reads `docs/sprints/TIMELINE.md` (sprint throughput, forecast, verdict against target) — closed rows are not among its inputs. Nothing broken yet only because no archive exists anywhere; the requirement bound nothing for six days while blocking the first cut. The question it answers — *what happened to `<id>`* — is a lookup by id against a file; opening the file answers it, as does `git log -p backlog.md`.
- Options: A — build a control on the Backlog tab — rejected: reinstates the defect that removed the chip, for a reader who has not appeared. B — give Timeline a second input — rejected: one tab answering two unrelated questions; couples a core-profile file to a scale-profile projection. C — no surface at all — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules: "The archive is read as a file, not through the backlog view." No tab, no control, no generator input, at either profile. ADR-051's reachability clause is narrowed to nothing rather than reassigned: it was written against hiding closed rows *inside the pool view* (a mask over a live list — `hideDone`); a separate file is not a mask; "not hidden" is satisfied by the row being in a file with its own name. Where closed work surfaces as history (what a period finished, how much moved), it is built from the changelog and the sprint records.
- Consequences: positive — the first archive cut is unblocked; `ARCHIVE-VIEW-1` closes without building anything; each dashboard tab keeps one input. Negative — a reader who only sees the generated dashboard cannot see a closed row (deliberate: period questions via Timeline and Reports; id lookup is one file open away). Negative — narrows a clause of a record accepted six days earlier, on measurement rather than use; "If it is wrong it is wrong cheaply - a surface is additive, and nothing here removes data."
- Revisit when: someone asks *what happened to `<id>`* and neither `backlog-archive.md` nor `git log -p backlog.md` answers it ("a sentence somebody says rather than a threshold to invent"); or an archive grows past the point where opening the file is a search — the size at which ADR-051 says the release headings become the split.
- Related: ADR-051 (narrowed; clauses 1-4, the guard and the `where` pointer untouched), ADR-046 (why a `decided` open question stays in the pool).

## ADR-054 — Asking is a mechanism with provenance, not an instruction (status: Accepted (2026-08-19); revised by ADR-062 (the `record.participation` point is retired), date: 2026-08-19)

- Tags: elicitation, guards, adoption, validation, methodology.
- Context:
  - The central promise is that adoption is guided: the agent asks the questions a repository cannot answer for itself; the owner decides. Every layer repeated the promise; nothing implemented it. Measured 2026-08-19 across the shipped tree and transition skills: zero of twenty-three skills contained a single call to `AskUserQuestion`. The instruction existed as prose in the right file (`onboard.md` said to mark inferred work unconfirmed and put the interview in the backlog).
  - A complete adoption against a real repository asked, in 1140 transcript lines, exactly one question (about duplicated agent hooks — the only thing mechanically visible). In the same run it wrote five personas nobody described, appended a section to thirty-three decision records their owner had written himself, seeded a twenty-item backlog with owners assigned, replaced the repository's own `docs/ADR` and `docs/BDR` naming with the standard's across seventy-eight files and fifty-three broken links, touched twenty-one `.scratch/` directories for link hygiene without surfacing unfinished work in them — and recorded in the intake a sentence attributed to the owner declaring a full migration that he never typed. "The blocking was never the problem: `AskUserQuestion` blocks the moment it is called. The gap was reaching the call."
  - The evidence was worthless in a way that looked like evidence: thirty-eight validation run records, 486 observations, all green. The ai-prompting corpus (31 runs, 377 observations) never prompted a person and was never meant to. The human-prompting corpus (7 runs, 109 observations) kept no session transcript and scored `asked: true` by reading whether a skill's *text* instructs asking — "prose measured by prose."
  - A laundering path: the model writes a summary of its own session; compaction replays that summary as a *user* turn; a quote invented by the agent then finds "coverage" in the agent's account of having invented it. Anything reading the transcript textually believes it.
- Options: sharpen the prose — rejected ("the failure mode is not misunderstanding"). Gate the finished artifact only (review-time check that the record looks interviewed) — catches the omission after five personas exist; kept as one layer, not the only one. Refuse the write (a `PreToolUse` hook denying a write to a gated artifact until its question fired) — **chosen**: "Not the model's decision to make." Refuse the write with no way out — rejected: a run with nobody to ask would have no legal move, and a guard with no legal move gets removed.
- Decision — three layers, none sufficient alone, and a declared vocabulary for what happened to each answer:
  1. `.claude/elicitation/points.json` is the declaration: twenty points, each with the question, the answers in order, which is recommended, which provenance states it permits, and the paths it gates. `tools/elicitation-points-check.mjs` fails when a declared point has no call site, or when the skill offers something other than the declared recommendation first.
  2. `.claude/hooks/elicitation-guard.mjs` refuses the write: a `Write`/`Edit` to a gated path is denied unless that point's question fired in this session. It reads the transcript structurally — an assistant turn that really called the tool — never textually (closes the laundering path). Fails closed: no transcript means refused.
  3. `docs/adoption-provenance.md` records what became of the answer: one table, one row per point — the state, who answered, when, where it landed, and for a deferred answer the backlog row carrying the promise. `scripts/elicitation-provenance.mjs` fails when that row is named and missing.
  - Every question offers the same three answers: **answer now** (`human`), **suggest, I will verify later** (`provisional`, plus a backlog row), **stub, do not guess** (`absent`).
  - The first option is the recommended one, and it always points at convergence with the standard — its layout, its shape, the whole of it; where that is not the axis, the answer a person gives now rather than defers. Declared per point and checked, because judgement produced the opposite: on the first live run of the finished mechanism, four of five recommendations named the least convergent answer, including *keep your own layout and map the standard onto it* — "an adoption recommending against adopting." Keeping the repository's own way stays on the list (a standard imposed without consent gets reverted) but never first; `null` is reserved for a question with no such axis, which is consent itself.
  - One point, every path it is asked on: a question asked on two paths is two option lists, the second written by whoever needed it. The first field run reached the conventions question on the brownfield path where no file declared it, invented it, and recommended the repository's own conventions; the greenfield copy had led with the standard's defaults since written. A point names every file that asks it and the check reads all of them.
  - The declared points are a floor, not a ceiling: the questions worth asking in a real repository are mostly ones no list anticipated; inventing them is the product working. The ledger carries a second table for questions no point declares; `elicitation-provenance.mjs` fails without it; what accumulates there is evidence for growing the point list. Two points entered that way from the same run: `adopt.tracker`, and `adopt.profile` (the greenfield path asks it bundled with the stack; the brownfield path never asked, because the manifest is copied in early and carries a default).
  - The question is asked in the language the person is writing in — not a point and must not become one ("a question asking which language to use has answered itself wrongly by existing"). The language the *artifacts* use is a point (`adopt.language`): `AGENTS.md` has carried a `Working language` slot since the beginning with nothing asking for it, so it got filled with whatever the agent was writing — English, because the standard is.
  - The escape from the guard is the stub, never a bypass: a run with nobody to ask declares the point `absent` and leaves the gap visible; that write is allowed and the run reads as unfinished. "**Guessing is the only move with no legal path.**" A few points — who the repository is for, what the owner meant by adopting — refuse even the stub, and an unattended run stops there.
  - `pending` is the state of a freshly scaffolded repo, legal until the point is **reached**: until a path it gates holds an artifact that did not ship as a template. Then it is an adoption that stopped halfway and closed the door behind it.
  - Reaching is measured from the commit that introduced the point list, not from the one that introduced the standard (same tree on a fresh adoption; years apart on an existing one). Measured from `.standards-version`, every file written since adoption counts as something the adoption wrote without asking; "Questions can only answer for writes made after the questions existed." `.standards-version` was the first trigger tried and it is wrong: written at align time before any question, so keying on it fails every freshly adopted repo on its first run — and the shipped tree's own template, where every row is pending. "A guard that is red on arrival is one people delete rather than satisfy."
  - Every point declares a scope deciding what the guard accepts after the adoption: a `repository`-scoped answer belongs to the repository (who it is for, how records are kept, which profile) — a **committed** ledger row answering it satisfies the guard from then on (committed rather than written, because reading the working file would let one run add the row and the artifact in the same breath). A `work`-scoped answer belongs to the piece of work in front of the run (this specification, this digest, this run) — no row settles it. Without the distinction the guard is unlivable (points gating `docs/decision-records/**` and `docs/personas.md` answered at adoption would refuse an ordinary `adr-write` months later); the opposite mistake is quieter and worse (one row written at adoption licenses every artifact forever).
  - A point is declared at every path that asks it, and every skill that writes a gated path carries the call site: `skill` and `file` both take lists; the static check requires a call site in each with the same recommended answer. Learned twice: the field run (conventions question on an undeclared path), and the spec flow, where three points named `spec-clarify` alone while `spec-specify`, `spec-update` and `spec-reconcile` all write the file those points gate — a refusal with no instructions in three of the four skills that hit it.
  - Claims about human participation are separated from claims about machinery: every validation run record carries `provenance`: `none` (measures tooling, nobody prompted), `unverified` (claims a person, no transcript), or `human` (claims it and names a transcript that must exist on disk).
- Consequences: adoption gets slower and louder — "the product working, not the product regressing." Writing a specification is now an interview: `spec-specify` asks the boundary before writing one, `spec-clarify` asks with the tool rather than rendering a markdown table nobody downstream can see, `spec-update` asks before a boundary moves, `spec-reconcile` asks before a criterion is rewritten to match what was built (the flagship flow made zero `AskUserQuestion` calls before). An adopting repo receives a hook that can refuse its own agent's writes, shipped enabled; a repo that wants it off deletes the matcher visibly in a diff. The old validation numbers stop being quotable as evidence of guided adoption (486 observations stand as records of what they measured; they lose the implication). The counterfactual is asserted: `tools/elicitation-replay-test.mjs` replays recorded sessions past the real guard — the run that caused this is stopped at five of five writes; a run that only *claims* to have asked is stopped; a run that asked before each write passes untouched (catches a guard that refuses everything). Two layers stay separate: a run that asked and then invented the answer is the transcript checker's catch, not the guard's — a test pins that division. Node becomes load-bearing for the hook (consistent with the `.mjs` scripts; the wiring denies rather than passes when it cannot run). Existing adopters get it through `update-to-latest` in a specific order: the four files land first and the session restarts; the `PreToolUse` entry in `.claude/settings.json` is the half that gets dropped (merging a matcher into a file the repo has always edited is not a file copy) — "An update that lands the hook without wiring it produces a repo that believes it is guarded"; the ledger arrives all `pending` and is not back-filled (writing `human` across it is the fabrication this layer catches). The layer cannot bootstrap itself: a `PreToolUse` hook is wired when the session starts; an unaligned repository has no wiring, so the adoption run is the one run the hook does not cover unless landed first — the adoption lands the guard, the points and the ledger before writing anything else and says the session must restart (nothing written at that stage is gated). (This gap is closed by ADR-059 and ADR-060.)
- Revisit when: a point's question fires and is answered but the answer is then ignored while the ledger reads `human` (the guard sees a question happened, never that it was honoured; the fix would be a third assertion). The harvest table stays empty across several real adoptions (either the list covers everything — unlikely — or nobody fills it; say so rather than keep the section). The stub escape becomes the normal path (most rows `absent` — questions wrong or at the wrong moment). A repository's own layout conventions collide with a gated glob so often the guard reads as noise (globs are per-repo configuration not yet made configurable, deliberately, until a second repository to learn from).
- Related: ADR-010 (AI-led means the agent starts the loop without being asked, never that it answers for the owner), ADR-024 (nothing a spec has settled gets re-asked), `.claude/elicitation/README.md` (the contract, the three answers, the provenance table). Extended by ADR-055, ADR-057/058, ADR-059, ADR-060; revised by ADR-061 (`null` narrowing) and ADR-062 (`record.participation` retired).

## ADR-055 — the close of an adoption runs before the pull request, and the consent it needs is asked at the intake (status: Accepted, revised by ADR-061 (adopt.evidence gets a recommended default) and ADR-062 (record.participation retired), date: 2026-08-19)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: an `align-to-standards` wave ends with three steps producing everything this project learns from an adoption — the upstream friction review (ADR-021), the `record-run` offer (ADR-045), the anonymous adoption ping (ADR-047) — all written as the last items of the phase file, after "open one focused PR". Measured 2026-08-19 on the largest field adoption yet (25 commits, 253 files, 20 questions asked, good enough that its guard findings were adopted back): none of the three ran — no friction issue offered, no transcript mentioned, `GET https://stats.repositorystandards.workers.dev` still returned `{"count":12}` after. Across every adoption ever performed, no adopter has been asked for a transcript once. Cause: placement — the same defect ADR-054 was written about one level up; a step at the tail competes with a user waiting for a PR, where context is thinnest; everything printed after the PR reads as optional. Two fixes rejected: gating the push (denying `git push` until the consent question fired) puts the guard on an action rather than an artifact and, because consent is `work`-scoped and no committed row settles it, would demand a telemetry-consent answer before every push in every adopting repo forever — the unlivable-guard failure; instructing harder is what the standard had been doing.
- Decision / rules:
  - The close comes before the pull request: steps renumbered so reading order is execution order — 7 upstream friction, 8 `record-run`, 9 the ping, 10 the pull request, stated to be the final action of the wave.
  - The consent is asked in the intake round as a declared point: `adopt.evidence` added to `.claude/elicitation/points.json`, gating `docs/adoption-intake.md` (the artifact an adoption writes early and cannot skip), asked in the intake block beside `adopt.intent`: "This session can be kept as evidence for the standard - the questions it asked, the answers you gave, what it produced. May it be kept, and may an excerpt be sent upstream after you have read it?" Options, in order: **keep it, and I will read the excerpt before anything is sent** / **keep it local - assemble it, send nothing** / **record nothing**. `recommended` is `null` and `allowed_provenance` is `human` alone: "consent is never nudged, never inferred and never stubbed." Adds no new way to stall (`adopt.intent` already gates the same file on a human answer). (Options and `recommended` revised by ADR-061, then ADR-062.)
  - The answer governs step 8 and nothing else: `record-run` still shows the assembled file and takes a per-item yes before anything leaves (ADR-045) — the intake answer is permission to assemble and to come back, not to send. It does not govern the ping (no session content; disclosed rather than asked — ADR-047).
  - The ping reports the count it produced: reads the counter before and after its POST and states both numbers; a count that did not move is reported as a ping that did not land.
- Consequences: one more intake question, in exchange for the only evidence this project cannot generate itself. `record-run`'s own `record.participation` point unchanged — it gates the write of the run record, a path existing in this repository and not an adopted one, which is why it could never have fired during an adoption (retired by ADR-062). An adoption answering **record nothing** says so in the ledger and skips step 8 out loud. The site's live badge becomes a check on this record: if it stops moving while adoptions complete, step 9 has drifted again.
- Compliance: `skills/align-to-standards/steps.md` orders the wave 7-8-9-10 with the PR last; `adopt.evidence` appears in `standard/.claude/elicitation/points.json` with `recommended: null` and `allowed_provenance: ["human"]`, in the shipped ledger template, and as a real `AskUserQuestion` block in `skills/align-to-standards/intake.md` — verified by `tools/elicitation-points-check.mjs` for every declared point.
- Revisit when: an adoption completes and the counter still does not move, or a run reaches step 10 with step 8 unmentioned — ordering failed as instruction did; the next lever is the guard on the action rather than the artifact, with the livability problem solved first. Or consent at the intake proves worth less than consent at the close (an adopter who said yes early and objects to what was assembled) — the two-stage design (permission to assemble now, per-item consent to send later) is the bet against that.
- Related: ADR-054, ADR-045, ADR-047. Revised by ADR-061, ADR-062.

## ADR-056 — the release tag is made by CI, and the version is stated only where something reads it (status: Accepted, date: 2026-09-02)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: ADR-025 settled what a tag is (a milestone for changelogs and update notifications, never an anchor) but not who makes one. Two tags existed — `1.0.0` and `1.0.1` — both typed by hand after the fact; no workflow, script or gate creates a tag or notices a missing one. The project rejects that elsewhere: `docs/open-questions/staying-current.md` turns down the maintainer-marks-it option as depending on "a human remembering - the class of thing this project usually refuses to rely on". Second half — what a release cost: `docs/facts.json` declared the version on nine surfaces, itself an undercount: `site/index.html` stated a version in five places (header pill, disclosure prose, footer, twice inside the hero script), only one declared (only the pill had a structural hook). `tools/docsite.mjs` rendered the number into the shared shell, so every deployed documentation page restated it (not in the diff — `site/docs/` is generated in CI and ignored — but on the site). Two of those places sit inside coupled capabilities, so every version cut fired the spec coupling guard on `web-surface` and `verify-engine` and was merged past with `--admin` (`COUPLING-VERSION-1`, measured 2026-08-19; also in the changelog). "A guard that fires on every pull request regardless of what changed is one its own authors are being trained to override, and `--admin` is also the response to a real coupling violation, so the override carries no information either way." Summary: the number lived in ten places checked by two guards, and the tag was made by memory.
- Decision / rules:
  - The tag is created by CI: `.github/workflows/release-tag.yml` runs on every push to `main`; reads `VERSION`; exits successfully if that version is already tagged; builds an annotated tag whose body is that release's own `###` headings from `CHANGELOG.md`; pushes it. Its own workflow file rather than a job in `pages.yml`, because tagging needs `contents: write` where the deploy runs on `contents: read`, and because `pages.yml` carries `cancel-in-progress: true` — a cancelled deploy costs nothing, a cancelled tag is unrecoverable (the next run reads only the newer `VERSION`; the stepped-over version is never tagged).
  - The landing reads the newest tag at runtime: the header pill fetches the repository's tag list from the GitHub API, picks the highest `x.y.z`, shows it — same shape as the "Live adoptions" badge reading its count from the stats Worker (ADR-047). The pill starts hidden and stays hidden if the fetch fails — "silent about the version rather than confidently wrong about it."
  - Nothing else states the version: prose names the release line ("the first stable release line"); usage examples read `--version <x.y.z>`; `standard/SPEC.md` says the number lives in `VERSION`. One restatement survives, in `standard/standard.manifest.json`, because `self-verify` compares it against an adopter's `.standards-version` — read by a program.
  - The two gates invert: `site-check` fails *any* version-shaped string on the landing, not merely a wrong one; `tree-check` fails a shipped tree that restates the version at all. "Requiring none rather than the right one is the part that makes the rule decidable": "the current version appears somewhere" is satisfied by a page showing two numbers — and one shipped that way: four places reading `0.8.12` beside a pill reading `0.8.13`, both gates green.
- Consequences: a release is now `VERSION`, the `CHANGELOG.md` section, and the manifest's `version` field — none inside a coupled capability, so a cut stops firing the coupling guard and `--admin` stops being routine (`COUPLING-VERSION-1`'s definition of done, reached by the second of its two permitted routes: the version string stopped living inside coupled code). Tags from `1.0.2` on are authored by `github-actions[bot]`, not the maintainer — visible on every tag, the price of the tag existing. The pill is a network read: a rate-limited or offline visitor sees no version. The deployed HTML states the version nowhere, so an agent reading the site cannot get it from the page — `VERSION` in the repository and the tag list are the sources, both machine-readable. Eight declared restatements left `docs/facts.json` (the guard was doing real work — it caught a landing wrong in four places — and the answer is what `docs/tree/docs-facts-json.md` named as preferable: a restatement you can delete instead of declaring).
- Compliance: `.github/workflows/release-tag.yml` exists and runs on push to `main`; `site/index.html` contains no version-shaped string and carries the tag fetch beside the adoptions fetch; `tools/site-check.mjs` fails any version on the landing and `tools/site-check-test.mjs` drives four fixtures over that rule; `tools/tree-check.mjs` fails a shipped tree restating the version; `docs/facts.json`'s `standard-version` fact declares one claim.
- Revisit when: a release reaches `main` and no tag appears (the next lever is a gate failing a `VERSION` move whose tag never followed — which cannot live in this workflow, since a workflow that did not run cannot report that it did not run). The pill is empty for a meaningful share of visitors (GitHub API is rate-limited per IP, unauthenticated; then the version gets baked in at deploy time from the tag list — a generated copy with a bounded life). Something that cannot read the manifest needs the shipped tree to state its own version (the rule is "stated only where something reads it", not "stated once"; a real reader is a reason to add one back, declared in `docs/facts.json`).
- Related: ADR-025, ADR-047 (the client-side fetch the pill copies; the one named non-GitHub host on the landing).

## ADR-057 — an artifact the adoption drafted or stubbed says so at the top of the file (status: Accepted, revised by ADR-058 (one marker for both cases), date: 2026-09-03)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: an adoption question has three answers (ADR-054): a person decides now, the agent suggests and the person checks later, or a stub is left. The second and third leave work for a human, recorded in exactly one place: a row per *question* in `docs/adoption-provenance.md` (state `provisional` or `absent`, naming one backlog row). A run that drafts twelve retroactive decision records under one `suggest` leaves one row there and at best one backlog row. The artifacts say nothing: a retroactive ADR reconstructed from the code is written `Status: Accepted` with "recorded retroactively", reading as a decision somebody made; a persona roster inferred from a routes table reads as a roster; `self-verify` counts both as present (correct under ADR-038); the file a colleague opens six months later carries no sign nobody confirmed it. Two things the standard wants and cannot deliver: the counted backlog (R27) is meant to make onboarding continuable by anyone, but a lead who wants to hand "confirm ADR-013" to the architect and "confirm the personas" to product has one row saying *decision records, provisional*; the `self-verify` percentage has no companion number — six empty stubs move it as far as six real files.
- Options: a hand-written hand-off file (`docs/adoption-handoff.md`) — a fourth record of the same fact beside the provenance table, the assessment and the backlog, curated rather than measured; wrong the first time someone confirms an ADR and does not strike the line (the align skill already refuses curated lists for the open delta). The provenance table, one row per artifact — keeps the fact in one table but leaves the file mute; the table is per question by design. A marker in the artifact, and a list derived from the markers — **chosen**: nothing to curate; the marker leaves in the commit that verifies the content, and that commit is the record of who verified and when.
- Decision / rules — every artifact written under `suggest` or `stub` opens with a marker line directly under the title, and the marker is the unit the tooling reads:
  - ``> [NEEDS REVIEW] drafted by the adoption run on 2026-09-03 from the route table and `roles.js`. Backlog: PERSONAS-1.``
  - `> [STUB] nothing written here yet - architect fills it. Backlog: ADR-013-1.` (retired by ADR-058)
  - `[NEEDS REVIEW]` is the `provisional` state per file: the line says what it was drafted from and which backlog row tracks it. It does not name who acts on it — the artifact's own kind says that (an ADR reads as technical, a BDR and `docs/personas.md` as product).
  - `[STUB]` is the `absent` state per file: nothing was written; the line names who fills it and the backlog row — the "visible gap marker" the elicitation README promised.
  - One backlog row per marked artifact, so a row can be assigned without splitting. The per-question row in the provenance table stays and names one of them.
  - A drafted decision record is `Proposed`, not `Accepted`: "Recorded retroactively" describes where the content came from, not whether anybody decided it. The human who confirms flips the status and removes the marker in the same commit.
  - The marker leaves with the verification and with nothing else: no `verified-by` field, no date; `git log` on the file answers who removed it and when; a commit removing markers while changing nothing else is the signal that the review did not happen.
  - The percentage does not move (ADR-038): a marked file is present and counts as adopted. `self-verify` reports how many entries carry a marker, split into drafted and stubbed, next to the percentage.
- Consequences: positive — the file tells its reader it is unconfirmed; the hand-off list is derived from the tree and cannot go stale; "N artifacts await a human" is a number alongside "X% adopted"; a stub is distinguishable from a draft and both from a decision. Cost — the retroactive records the align skill writes change status: a run that ends with twelve Accepted ADRs ends with twelve Proposed ones and twelve backlog rows ("the truthful count and it looks worse"). Cost — one more convention an adopter can strip in bulk (shows up as a commit removing markers and touching nothing else). Follow-ups under the epic "Adoption leaves the human work visible": the shipped tree writes the marker (`NEEDS-REVIEW-1`), the checks read it (`NEEDS-REVIEW-2`), the dashboard shows it (`NEEDS-REVIEW-3`).
- Confirmation: nothing mechanical yet, stated rather than implied. The convention lands with `NEEDS-REVIEW-1`; the checks — `self-verify` counting marked entries, `elicitation-provenance` requiring every marker to name an existing backlog row — land with `NEEDS-REVIEW-2`. Until then the marker is reviewed by hand at the adoption's pre-PR review; a run that writes under `suggest` without it is a defect against this record.
- Revisit when: a field adoption's history shows markers removed in commits changing nothing else (the marker became a chore; the per-file state needs a different carrier or a stronger gate); the per-question rows in `docs/adoption-provenance.md` and the markers disagree on a repository and neither check catches it; an adopter's team assigns the marked rows without opening the files (the backlog row alone carries enough; the marker is duplicate).
- Related: ADR-006, ADR-020, ADR-042 (artifacts the marker most often lands on); ADR-038; ADR-048 (the marker is a shape the checks can read); ADR-054; R17 (recorded deviations), R27 (assessment and count artifacts). Revised by ADR-058.

## ADR-058 — one marker says a human has not looked yet (status: Accepted, date: 2026-09-03)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: ADR-057 gave the two answers leaving work for a person two markers. The maintainer applied the convention to a real adoption the day it was cut, and the second marker earned nothing: what a reader and the check `NEEDS-REVIEW-2` want from the line is one fact — has a person looked at this file yet — and both markers answer no. Whether the text under the marker is a draft or nothing is visible from the file; a marker restating it puts two strings where one grep should do. The `[STUB]` line was the less useful: "Nothing written here yet - architect fills it" tells the architect who is on the hook and not what the file is for — the run knew that when it chose to leave the file rather than guess, and the line threw it away.
- Options: keep both markers (an empty file is told apart from a draft without opening it; but the reader who never opens the file is the check, which reads the whole line anyway; cost is two strings to grep and keep in step). One marker, and the empty case says what belongs in the file — **chosen**.
- Decision / rules — "`[NEEDS REVIEW]` is the only marker." Directly under the title; one string to grep. What follows differs:
  - ``> [NEEDS REVIEW] drafted by the adoption run on 2026-09-03 from the route table and `roles.js`. Backlog: PERSONAS-1.``
  - `> [NEEDS REVIEW] nothing written here yet; should hold the options weighed and the reason the team keeps Postgres. Backlog: ADR-013-1.`
  - A drafted artifact's line is as ADR-057 had it minus one field: what it was drafted from and which backlog row tracks it; it does not name who acts on it (the artifact's kind says that; "A team small enough to run this standard at `core` (one or two people) does not need a field to tell it which of them a persona roster is for").
  - An empty artifact's line says nothing is written yet, what the file should contain in one sentence, and the backlog row.
  - Everything else in ADR-057 stands: one backlog row per marked file, a drafted decision record is `Proposed`, the marker leaves in the commit that verifies the content and with nothing else, the percentage does not move (ADR-038). The companion count `NEEDS-REVIEW-2` adds is one flat number; who should look at an entry is read from the backlog row the marker names.
- Consequences: positive — one string to grep, teach and check; an empty file says what it is for; the drafted case (the common one) does not change. Cost — the count no longer splits drafted from empty for free (the line still carries it for a reader). ADR-057's `[STUB]` clause narrowed (status `Accepted, revised by 058`); the three backlog rows under "Adoption leaves the human work visible" carry the single marker; nothing in the shipped tree changes because `NEEDS-REVIEW-1` had not landed.
- Confirmation: as for ADR-057 — nothing mechanical until `NEEDS-REVIEW-2`, which scans for one string; until then a run that writes `[STUB]`, or an empty artifact whose line does not say what the file should hold, is a defect at the adoption's pre-PR review.
- Revisit when: the check lands and a real team wants drafts and empty files apart on the dashboard without opening the files (the case for a second word, arriving with the count that needs it); or any of ADR-057's own triggers (they reopen the marker, not only its word).
- Related: ADR-057 (narrowed), ADR-054 (three answers stay three; two share one marker), ADR-038.

## ADR-059 — Landing the guard is a mechanism too, not only an instruction (status: Accepted (2026-09-03), date: 2026-09-03)

- Tags: elicitation, guards, adoption, bootstrap, validation.
- Context: ADR-054 named its own gap — "a `PreToolUse` hook is wired when the session starts, and an unaligned repository has no wiring - so the adoption run, which is the run this exists to stop, is the one run the hook does not cover unless it is landed first" — and closed it with `steps.md`'s numbered item "0.": land the four files, then stop and say the session must restart; deliberately ungated (gating them would need the guard wired before it exists). That fix is prose, told once, near the top — the exact shape ADR-054 diagnosed as insufficient. On 2026-09-03, LomondGroup/propertycloud PR #1199 (PC-2847) showed it fails the same way: a fresh adoption committed `docs/adoption-intake.md` a commit before the elicitation guard landed; `self-verify` was green throughout (nothing compared *when* the guard landed against *when* a gated artifact did); a human reading the diff caught it. Three things decide the fix: (1) a single instruction is a promise held only while remembered — a resumed, compacted session reaches phase 1 or 2's first write on a stale memory of "probably already did that"; (2) the gap is checkable only after the fact, from history — once both exist on disk nothing about content says which was first; `.standards-version`'s bare existence was rejected in ADR-054 as the wrong signal; order has to be read from commit ancestry, as `reached()` reads "the commit that introduced `.claude/elicitation/points.json`" — walked the other way; (3) all three of Gate 0, Gate 2 and the version pin predate the guard in the standard itself: `docs/adoption-intake.md` since 0.9.0, `.standards-version` since 0.4.0, `docs/adoption-assessment.md` since 0.9.6 — the guard since 0.9.23; any repo that adopted between and runs `update-to-latest` has legitimate history predating the guard's landing commit; a check keyed only on "did X land before the guard" fails every such repo.
- Options: restate the instruction more prominently — rejected as the only layer, kept as one of three. Gate the four bootstrap files behind an elicitation point — rejected (circular: the hook that would refuse the write does not exist yet). Scope the order check to commits reachable from `origin/HEAD`, or take a `--base` ref — rejected: `self-verify.mjs` invokes every `kind: "static"` guard with no base ref (only `kind: "diff"` guards receive one, run separately against the PR's diff); a design needing a base ref would silently never receive one; harder to test (needs a fabricated origin remote) for a distinction the task does not need — "two commits either have an ancestry relationship or they do not, on any branch." Key the update-to-latest exemption off a date, or off the two commits sharing a parent — rejected: dates are restated by whoever writes the commit; two arbitrary adoptions months apart share no parent.
- Decision — three layers, checking different failure moments:
  1. Structural emphasis at the file the agent reads: the guard-landing step (`steps.md`'s former item "0.") moves to its own file `skills/align-to-standards/land-guard.md`, content preserved verbatim; `SKILL.md` gains a new **Step -1**, read before Step 0 - Intake and before any route is chosen, whose only content is the pointer; `steps.md` carries a pointer at the top instead of a numbered step it could mistake for optional. Named Step -1 rather than Step 0a so `intake.md` and `onboard.md`'s references to "Step 0" (the intake step) need no change.
  2. A cheap, repeated precondition at the start of every phase's first write: `steps.md` (step 1), `greenfield.md` and `onboard.md` each open their first write with the same three-line check — `test -f .claude/elicitation/points.json && grep -q elicitation-guard.mjs .claude/settings.json && test -f docs/adoption-provenance.md` — a nonzero exit means stop, read `land-guard.md`, land the guard, restart. "it does not ask the agent to *reason* about whether the guard probably already landed, it reads the tree." `stack.md` and `layer2.md` carry no such check because both routes require a pre-existing `.standards-version`, implying a prior aligned run that had the guard as a required manifest entry.
  3. A mechanical after-the-fact backstop in the guard's own script: `scripts/elicitation-provenance.mjs` now checks that the guard's own commit — the oldest commit that added `.claude/elicitation/points.json` (the marker `reached()` reads) — is an ancestor of the oldest commit that added each of `docs/adoption-intake.md`, `docs/adoption-assessment.md` and `.standards-version`; never a date comparison. Exempted whenever `.standards-version` already existed the commit before the guard's own commit — the update-to-latest shape, read from what the tree looked like one commit before the guard landed. This check is R28, `blocks: true`, alongside the ledger-consistency checks the script already ran.
  - None of the three depends on the others: a session skipping Step -1 meets the precondition at phase 1; one talking past it meets the commit-order backstop once it commits; only a run defeating all three reaches PC-2847's state, and it now fails a required, blocking check.
- Consequences: the four bootstrap files stay deliberately ungated (the new checks look at commits that exist; they never refuse a write). A repo whose history put a Gate artifact before the guard fails a required check instead of relying on review (the direct fix for PC-2847). A repo retrofitting the guard via `update-to-latest` is not penalized for prior history. Three checks instead of one instruction is more surface, not a wash (three lines of bash in three files; one function in a script that already runs). The order check anchors on one file, `.claude/elicitation/points.json`, not all four — consistent with `adoptionWrote()`'s single marker; the four land together, so one stands for the group.
- Revisit when: the phase-entry precondition gets skipped the same way the instruction did (then move the check somewhere an agent cannot reach the first write without running it, not a fourth restatement); a repo needs the exemption narrower than "any prior `.standards-version`" (e.g. predating the guard by so many major versions that one lineage is indefensible); a repo's guard-landing step commits the four files across more than one commit, so the single-marker anchor stops representing "the guard landed".
- Related: ADR-054 (extended; closes "the layer cannot bootstrap itself"), ADR-042 (a step performed vs a step leaving a checkable record), ADR-020 (Step -1 before intake), `standard/SPEC.md` R28.

## ADR-060 — The adoption is guarded from the checkout that drives it (status: Accepted (2026-09-03), date: 2026-09-03)

- Tags: elicitation, guards, adoption, bootstrap.
- Context: hours after ADR-059, a fresh adoption skipped the intake question round entirely and wrote `docs/adoption-intake.md` out of the skill's own prose. The run's own account: "I read the skill instead of running it. I took Step 0's routing table, picked the route, and never executed the question round inside it. Worse: I read `intake.md`'s structure - the list of points and what each is for - and used it to write `docs/adoption-intake.md` with my own answers, rather than to ask." Nothing refused it, measured: `standard/.claude/settings.json` wires the guard — but that is the tree that ships into an adopted repo and binds there only after Step -1 and a restart; the standard's own checkout carried `launch.json`, `serve.json` and `settings.local.json` under `.claude/`, no `settings.json` and no hooks; the user-level settings on the machine wire the other shipped guards and not this one. `align-to-standards` says where it runs: "From a checkout of `repository-standards` - this skill is never shipped to a client repo." So the repository that authors, ships, tests and documents this guard is the one that never ran it, in the one session it exists for. ADR-059's layers do not reach it: Step -1 and the phase precondition land and check the guard in the *target*; hooks bind from the session's own project directory; a target's `.claude/settings.json` is never read by a session rooted in the driving checkout; the ancestry backstop fires only once the run commits into a repository it has also given a guard. Why it had never been wired: unscoped, it cannot be — this repository carries the same gated paths any adopter does (`PRODUCT.md`, `docs/personas.md`, `backlog.md`, `docs/decision-records/**`, `specs/**/spec.md`, `AGENTS.md`) and the only ledger under it is the shipped template, every row `pending`; wiring it the adopter way refuses every ordinary write, and "a guard nobody can work under gets removed." Second defect: the guard resolved the ledger, and the `git show HEAD:` check behind a committed answer, relative to the working directory; its rename check had learned better ("asked in the right repository, which is not always this one"); a driving checkout holding answered rows would have vouched for writes into a repository that answered nothing.
- Options: restate that the question round is executed, not read — rejected as a layer (the prose in `intake.md` already says this and was read as content). Wire the guard here the way an adopter wires it — rejected (every ordinary write is to a gated path with a `pending` ledger; the first hour ends with the wiring deleted). Let a repository declare the narrower scope via an environment variable — the first shape tried, rejected on the capability's own invariant: an adopted repository could set the same variable and exempt every write it makes; "The tree's shape cannot be set from a settings file." Give this repository its own answered ledger and adopt itself fully — a real option and a larger question; rejected here because the honest state for a question the mechanism never asked is not `human`, and writing `human` so daily work passes is the fabrication ADR-054 catches. Gate the question round on the phase boundary itself — rejected: no such event reaches a `PreToolUse` hook; `points.json` already names this blind spot for `adopt.continue` and `adopt.commit-plan`.
- Decision:
  1. The guard reads which side of an adoption it is on from the tree's own shape: a checkout carrying the shipped tree under `standard/` is one that drives adoptions; an adopter has that tree unpacked at its root and never matches. On the driving side the guard judges only writes whose owning work tree is not its own, and leaves the driving repository's tree to its own process. Deliberately not an environment variable (a switch any repository could set for itself; "this capability's own invariant says no configuration adds an outcome"). "The shape decides *whose* writes are judged, never what happens to a judged one."
  2. This repository wires it in its own `.claude/settings.json`, with the same `Write|Edit|NotebookEdit|Bash` matcher and the same deny-on-failure fallback as the shipped settings, pointed at `standard/.claude/hooks/elicitation-guard.mjs`.
  3. The ledger is read in the tree that owns the write — both the file and the committed row behind a settled repository-scoped point — mirroring the rename check. A move reaching two work trees at once has no single owner, so no ledger answers for it and it is refused (the segment rule these guards apply to chained commands, applied across repositories).
  4. The guard's own test asserts all of it: the driving checkout wires the guard it ships; a driving tree's own writes go through *and* the identical write is refused in a tree without that shape; an answer committed over here settles nothing over there. Runs in `checks.yml`, so unwiring fails a required check.
- Consequences: the adoption run is covered from its first write (verified against the write that prompted this record: `docs/adoption-intake.md` in another repository, no question asked, is refused naming the missing point). This repository's own work is untouched, and the escape cannot widen silently (the test asserts the same write refused in a tree without the driving shape). Both ways of running an adoption behave the same (driven from this checkout — the guard covers writes into the target; run inside the target after Step -1 — covers that repository's own); Step -1 stays as ADR-059 left it. A driving checkout is trusted about its own tree — "a real hole, named rather than papered over": an unasked artifact written *here* is not refused; accepted because this repository's writes are reviewed as its own work under its own gates, and the alternative is the unwired state. A run that skips the questions and writes nothing gated is still not caught here — intake is a required artifact (ADR-042), so such a run meets the guard when it writes the intake record; a run that never writes it fails the artifact gate instead.
- Revisit when: this repository gains its own honest provenance ledger (then the narrower judgement can shrink to template-only paths, or go); a second checkout starts driving adoptions (the wiring assertion knows about this one only); a defect lands in the driving checkout's own tree that this waved through — arguing for the self-adoption option rather than a fourth restatement.
- Related: ADR-054, ADR-059 (the bootstrap gap on the target's side; this is the driver's side), ADR-042, ADR-020, `standard/SPEC.md` R28.

## ADR-061 — `adopt.evidence` recommends sending, and its consent is asked once, not per item (status: Accepted, revised by ADR-062 (two answers, the held-for-review one dropped; record.participation retired), date: 2026-09-04)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: `adopt.evidence` (ADR-055) offers two options — send an anonymised excerpt after reading it, or send nothing — with `recommended: null` (consent must never be nudged). Downstream, `record-run` (ADR-045) shows the assembled batch once and then asks a separate yes/no per item (step 6, borrowing the per-issue pattern from ADR-021), so a full align session with twenty or more user turns produces twenty or more identical send-or-not prompts for one decision already made at intake. The owner, running the standard on his own repositories, named this directly: the same question asked dozens of times, with no faster path than reading the whole excerpt every time before saying yes. His requested shape, in order: send it (the default) / send it, once I have read it / send nothing.
- Decision / rules:
  - `adopt.evidence` gains a third option and a recommended default. Options in order: (1) **send it** (recommended) — `record-run` assembles the anonymised excerpt (machine paths and identity scrubbed, the repository named only as `/git/<repo>`) and sends it; the scrub is the safeguard, not a mandatory read; (2) **send it, once I have read it** — the same assembly, held for one more yes after it has been shown; (3) **send nothing**. `recommended` in `standard/.claude/elicitation/points.json` becomes `"send it"`. `allowed_provenance` stays `human` alone ("recommending an answer is not inferring or stubbing one").
  - `record-run` stops asking per item and stops re-asking what intake answered. The intake answer governs the whole run: **send it** — assemble, scrub, send, no further question, the run report says what went out; **send it, once I have read it** — assemble, scrub, show the whole batch once (not row by row), let the person edit or drop a row, then ask exactly one final yes/no (send now, or keep it local); **send nothing** — `record-run` does not fire.
  - The Level 1 / Level 2 richness choice (ADR-045) is unchanged and still asked once per run (until ADR-062); this decision touches only how many times, and with what default, consent is asked.
- Consequences: positive — one decision instead of a number growing with the session; a default matching how most people want to finish. Negative, stated plainly: the invariant in `record-run`'s "What this is not" section — "consent is per run and per item, never inferred from a prior yes" — no longer holds for the **send it** path; choosing it trusts the automated scrub alone (pattern matching, not a guarantee); "a deliberate trade of a safety net for speed, made by the standard's own owner on his own repositories". `record.participation` (whose run this is) untouched: an identity claim, not a send-or-not choice; keeps `recommended: null`. `specs/elicitation/spec.md`'s R28 text no longer states every consent point must declare `recommended: null`; `adopt.evidence` is the documented exception and `record.participation` the case the rule was written for.
- Compliance: `standard/.claude/elicitation/points.json`: `adopt.evidence.recommended` is `"send it"`, its `asks` and `why` rewritten; `skills/align-to-standards/intake.md`: the `[adopt.evidence]` block lists the three options in order; `skills/record-run/SKILL.md`: steps 5-6 rewritten so the intake answer governs the batch, no per-item question; `specs/elicitation/spec.md`: the `recommended: null` requirement narrowed; `tools/elicitation-points-check.mjs` passes ("first option equals recommended").
- Revisit when: a **send it** choice ships something a human read would have caught; or real runs show most people still choose to read first (the added option bought a third choice without changing outcomes).
- Related: ADR-055 (narrowed for `adopt.evidence`; placement-at-intake unaffected), ADR-045 (two-level design unchanged here; per-item pattern replaced with batch-once), ADR-021 (per-issue consent pattern unaffected elsewhere — a handful of friction/stack-request issues, not a session's worth of rows). Revised by ADR-062.

## ADR-062 — a contributed run is the full run, anonymised, or nothing (status: Accepted, date: 2026-09-16)

- (Front-matter style header.)
- Context: ADR-045 gave `record-run` two consent levels (Level 1: the user's literal turns with three summary yes/nos and one result line; Level 2: plus the agent's text, the tool log and the repository slug). ADR-061 gave `adopt.evidence` three answers. Two external records arrived: `anon-r3k` (2026-09-04) and `anon-x8v` (PR #143, 2026-09-16, closed unmerged) — both chose Level 1, both carry `provenance: unverified` and `transcript: null`, and not one verdict can be replayed by anybody not in the session (scoring done from memory by the session that ran it). ADR-045's own revisit clause named this ("Level 1 alone proves to still collect too little to explain a finding"); `record-run` already stated every finding needed the agent's text. "The cheaper branch was offered so that a smaller yes would beat a large no; what it collected was a record that is not evidence." Owner's decision 2026-09-16: the full run is always what is wanted, and that decision is not handed to the adopter — contribute something real that can be checked, or do not contribute; if they agree, everything is anonymised.
- Options: A — keep both levels, recommend Level 2 — rejected (a choice with a cheaper branch is still a choice; the two records show which branch gets taken). B — Level 1.5 (agent text without the slug) as a third option — rejected: three branches is still a menu with a cheapest item; "What Level 1.5 describes is not a third option; it is the one shape, made mandatory." C — one shape: the full run, fully anonymised, or nothing — **chosen**. Two further variants rejected: a held-for-review answer (ADR-061's **send it, once I have read it**) — the pull request `record-run` opens from the adopter's own account is the review (theirs to edit or close before anyone upstream reads it), so a second look adds a step without a safeguard; and the 2026-09-03 measurement behind ADR-061 showed adopters take the last answer on a list — a middle answer only moves where the run stalls. A named, not anonymised, variant — both external records were anonymised and private, so the name buys the evidence nothing (a finding is replayed from the turns, not the slug); a list of adopters willing to be named is a marketing surface, kept separate from the evidence record if ever kept.
- Decision (Option C) / rules:
  - `adopt.evidence` has two answers, in order: **send it** (recommended) / **send nothing**. Its `asks` says what goes out and that it is anonymised. `allowed_provenance` stays `human` alone.
  - The record carries the literal user turns, the agent's text verbatim, the tools that ran in order (names only, never their input or output), the scoring and the result line, and nothing identifying the repository: an opaque code not derived from its name, no owner, no paths, no hosts, no usernames.
  - `record-run` asks no consent question at the close. It reads the intake answer from the **Evidence** line of `docs/adoption-intake.md`; under **send it** it assembles the full run, scrubs it, shows the assembled record once in the session as a courtesy, and opens the pull request from the adopter's account; under **send nothing** it records nothing and says so.
  - `record.participation` (whose run this is, and may the excerpt be kept) is retired. Its second half is consent, settled at intake. Its first half is answered without asking: the PR opens from the adopter's own account, so `real-adoption` is read off the author — a maintainer's own run keeps the `validation` scope — and whether a person answered the questions or the agent answered for itself is read off the agent turns the one shape always carries. The ADR-054 failure (a maintainer's run recorded as an external adopter's) cannot recur because no answer is typed at the close.
  - The write gate on `docs/validation/**/runs/*.json` moves to `adopt.evidence`, so a run record still needs a human intake answer in the transcript before it is written.
  - Every record gets an opaque code; the subject line `feat(real-adoption): <code>, <stack> - what the run showed` never carries a repository name.
  - The step-3 scrub is unchanged and now also replaces the repository's name, owner and slug wherever they occur.
  - `tools/human-prompting.mjs` refuses a run record dated after 2026-09-16 that carries no agent turn and no transcript; records before that date are history and still pass.
  - Revises ADR-045 (decision point 2, the two-level split), ADR-061 (the three-answer set) and ADR-054 (the `record.participation` point; the provenance states on run records stand). Everything else in the three stands: the corpus `record-run` feeds, the scrub, the intake placement, the once-per-run consent, the recommended default, the guard.
- Consequences: positive — every record arriving from now on can be replayed (verdicts sit next to the text they were scored against). Negative — some adopters who would have sent prompts only will now send nothing (accepted: "the prompts-only record was not paying for the row it took"). `record-run` asks nothing at the close and the ledger template loses one row; what the retired question attested is now attested by the PR author and the transcript, neither of which the agent can type for itself. Negative — the scrub carries more weight since the agent's text is always in the record and may quote what a tool read; step 3 drops raw tool input and output outright; the PR from the adopter's account is the human read before the merge.
- Compliance: `standard/.claude/elicitation/points.json`: `adopt.evidence.asks` and `why` rewritten, `recommended` stays `"send it"`, the point gates `docs/validation/**/runs/*.json` as well as the intake file; `record.participation` removed; `tools/elicitation-guard-test.mjs` asserts the record is refused without the intake answer and allowed with it. `skills/align-to-standards/intake.md`: the `[adopt.evidence]` block lists two options. `skills/align-to-standards/steps.md` step 8, `standard/docs/adoption-intake.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `docs/validation/human-prompting/reporting.md`, `services/adoption-stats/README.md`: two-level and three-answer wording gone. `skills/record-run/SKILL.md`: one shape, no consent question at the close, opaque code on every record. `tools/human-prompting.mjs` and its test: the dated refusal.
- Revisit when: a PR opened under **send it** carries something the scrub missed and the adopter did not catch before merge (the PR is the review; this decision removed the in-session read); or real adoptions show **send nothing** taken so consistently that the corpus stops growing at all ("the trade here is fewer records for checkable ones, not none").
- Related: ADR-045 (two-level split revised; corpus destination, scrub and consent gate stand), ADR-061 (three answers narrowed to two; once-per-run consent and recommended default stand), ADR-054 (`record.participation` retired; mechanism and provenance states stand).
